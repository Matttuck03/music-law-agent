B. What a mix is

B1. Is a DJ mix a derivative work of each track, a compilation, or both? What turns on the answer?

  • Rows that frame it: definitions of derivative work [M-0521] and compilation [M-0569]. A 2016 policy paper says remixes are often derivative works, compilations or collective works [M-0554].
  • Gap: no saved authority decides it.

B2. What, if anything, does a user own in a mix made without permission?

  • Rows that frame it: no protection for parts using material unlawfully [M-0528]; protection only for new material [M-0529]; the Seventh Circuit's hard line [M-0532, M-0533]; the Second Circuit's fair use route [M-0535, M-0536].
  • Ask: what should our user terms say about ownership?

B3. Can any kind of mix be fair use: a transition-only blend, a mashup, a short clip?

  • Rows that frame it: the four factors [M-0547]; no bright lines [M-0205]; parody against satire [M-0548]; purpose and commerciality after Warhol [M-0551, M-0552]; burden on the user [M-0211]; the sampling split between circuits [M-0186, M-0188].
  • Gap: no saved case on a DJ mix or mashup.

B4. For artist-approved remix projects, what should the grant say and who should own the result?

  • Rows that frame it: transfers need a signed writing [M-0040]; rights can be split and owned separately [M-0539]; work made for hire is a closed list [M-0537, M-0047].
  • Gap: no saved remix deal terms.

C. Hosting, sharing and streaming

C1. Which exact licences does a public, on-demand library of user mixes need, and from whom?

  • Rows that frame it: an interactive service needs song licences as well as recording licences [M-0301]; performance rights in songs and recordings [M-0106, M-0107]; the 114 licence bars interactive services and short archives [M-0115, M-0578, M-0579]; a large service's account of mechanical and performance licensing [M-0086, M-0088]; licensed mix services' statements about direct deals (captured 2026-11-13) [M-0586, M-0589, M-0593].

C2. Can any part of our service run under the section 114 statutory licence?

  1. [M-0521] VERIFIED. A derivative work is one based on preexisting works; the statute's examples include a musical arrangement and a sound recording, and 'any other form in which a work may be recast, transformed, or adapted'.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 101 definitions. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 101 'derivative work'. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "any other form in which a work may be recast, transformed, or adapted"

  2. [M-0569] VERIFIED. A compilation is a work formed by collecting and assembling preexisting materials selected, coordinated or arranged so the whole is an original work of authorship.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 101 definitions. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 101 'compilation'. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "selected, coordinated, or arranged in such a way that the resulting work as a whole constitutes an original work of authorship"

  3. [M-0554] VERIFIED. 2016 Commerce white paper: remixes include mashups and sampling, and in many cases they will be derivative works, compilations or collective works under the Copyright Act.

    Source: Dept of Commerce Internet Policy Task Force, White Paper on Remixes, First Sale, and Statutory Damages (Jan. 2016). Pinpoint: White Paper (Jan. 2016), p. 7. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "In many cases, these remixes will constitute derivative works, compilations, or collective works, as defined in the Copyright Act"

  4. [M-0528] VERIFIED. Under Section 103(a), protection for a work employing preexisting copyrighted material does not extend to any part of the work in which the material has been used unlawfully.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 103 compilations and derivative works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 103(a). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "protection for a work employing preexisting material in which copyright subsists does not extend to any part of the work in which such material has been used unlawfully"

  5. [M-0529] VERIFIED. Copyright in a derivative work or compilation covers only the new material the author contributed and gives no exclusive right in the preexisting material.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 103 compilations and derivative works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 103(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "The copyright in a compilation or derivative work extends only to the material contributed by the author of such work"

  6. [M-0532] VERIFIED. Pickett v. Prince (7th Cir. 2000): section 103(a) does not let a stranger make a derivative work; the court read it to mean, at least so far as bears on that case, only that the right to make one does not authorise using material that infringes someone else's copyright.

    Source: Pickett v. Prince, 207 F.3d 402 (7th Cir. 2000). Pinpoint: 207 F.3d 402, Posner C.J.. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "we do not read section 103(a) as qualifying the exclusive right of the owner of the copyright of the original work to make derivative works based on that work"

  7. [M-0533] VERIFIED. Pickett: it is very difficult to see how a derivative work not made by the owner of the original could fail to infringe it, given the definition of derivative works.

    Source: Pickett v. Prince, 207 F.3d 402 (7th Cir. 2000). Pinpoint: 207 F.3d 402. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "It is very difficult to see how a derivative work not made by the owner of the original work could fail to infringe it, given the definition of derivative works"

  8. [M-0535] VERIFIED. Keeling v. Hars (2d Cir. 2015): the creator of an unauthorized work that stays within fair use and adds sufficient originality may claim 103 protection for her original contributions.

    Source: Keeling v. Hars, 809 F.3d 43 (2d Cir. 2015). Pinpoint: 809 F.3d 43. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "if the creator of an unauthorized work stays within the bounds of fair use and adds sufficient originality, she may claim protection under the Copyright Act"

  9. [M-0536] VERIFIED. Keeling v. Hars (2d Cir. 2015), facts: a parody stage adaptation whose author held no copyright or licence in the source film; a jury found the adaptation a fair use, and the court's holding on protection for her original contributions is in M-0535.

    Source: Keeling v. Hars, 809 F.3d 43 (2d Cir. 2015). Pinpoint: 809 F.3d 43, Background. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

  10. [M-0547] VERIFIED. Section 107 lists four fair use factors: purpose and character (commercial or nonprofit), nature of the work, amount and substantiality used, and market effect.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 107 fair use. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 107. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

  11. [M-0205] VERIFIED. Campbell: fair use requires case-by-case analysis and is not simplified by bright-line rules.

    Source: Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569 (1994) (U.S. Reports via Library of Congress). Pinpoint: 510 U.S. 569, 577 (Part II.A, opinion of the Court). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "The task is not to be simplified with bright-line rules, for the statute, like the doctrine it recognizes, calls for case-by-case analysis."

  12. [M-0548] VERIFIED. Campbell: parody needs to mimic an original to make its point and so has some claim to use its victim's work, whereas satire can stand on its own two feet and so requires justification for the very act of borrowing.

    Source: Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569 (1994) (U.S. Reports via Library of Congress). Pinpoint: 510 U.S. 569, 580-81. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "satire can stand on its own two feet and so requires justification for the very act of borrowing"

  13. [M-0551] VERIFIED. Warhol: if an original and the secondary use share the same or highly similar purposes and the use is commercial, factor one is likely to weigh against fair use absent another justification.

    Source: Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, Inc. v. Goldsmith, 598 U.S. 508 (2023). Pinpoint: 598 U.S. 508, syllabus. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "if an original work and secondary use share the same or highly similar purposes, and the secondary use is commercial, the first fair use factor is likely to weigh against fair use"

  14. [M-0552] VERIFIED. Warhol: reading Campbell to favour any use adding new expression would let transformative use swallow the owner's right to prepare derivative works.

    Source: Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, Inc. v. Goldsmith, 598 U.S. 508 (2023). Pinpoint: 598 U.S. 508, syllabus. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "transformative use would swallow the copyright owner’s exclusive right to prepare derivative works"

  15. [M-0211] VERIFIED. Estate of Smith (S.D.N.Y. 2017): fair use is an affirmative defense and the party asserting it bears the burden.

    Source: Estate of Smith v. Cash Money Records, Inc., 253 F. Supp. 3d 737 (S.D.N.Y. 2017) (Caselaw Access Project mirror). Pinpoint: 253 F. Supp. 3d at 748 (Part on fair use; burden sentence follows). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Fair use is an affirmative defense to copyright infringement"

  16. [M-0186] VERIFIED. Sixth Circuit rule: for sampling a sound recording, the court says get a license or do not sample (no de minimis or substantial similarity inquiry).

    Source: Bridgeport Music, Inc. v. Dimension Films, 410 F.3d 792 (6th Cir. 2005) (amended on rehearing) (Caselaw Access Project mirror). Pinpoint: 410 F.3d at 801. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Get a license or .do not sample."

  17. [M-0188] VERIFIED. The Ninth Circuit holds the de minimis exception applies to sound recording sampling, and expressly disagrees with the Sixth Circuit.

    Source: VMG Salsoul, LLC v. Ciccone, 824 F.3d 871 (9th Cir. 2016) (de minimis sampling). Pinpoint: 824 F.3d 871, Part II.B (slip op. at 32). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "We hold that the “de minimis” exception applies to actions alleging infringement of a copyright to sound recordings."

  18. [M-0040] VERIFIED. A transfer of copyright ownership, other than by operation of law, is not valid unless in a writing signed by the owner of the rights conveyed or the owner's authorized agent.
