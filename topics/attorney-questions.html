A. The stem tool

A1. Is splitting a lawfully obtained track into stems a reproduction or the making of a derivative work?

  • Why it matters: every feature starts with a stem split. If the split itself infringes, private use is not a safe starting point.
  • Rows that frame it: the recording owner's copy right covers copying that recaptures the actual sounds [M-0110]. The derivative right covers sounds rearranged, remixed or altered [M-0523]. Each owner controls derivative works [M-0522].
  • Gap: no saved case rules on stem separation.

A2. Does it matter whether the split runs on the user's device or on our servers?

  • Why it matters: it decides the product's architecture and who made the copy.
  • Rows that frame it: anyone who violates an exclusive right is an infringer [M-0220]. The safe harbor covers storage at the direction of a user [M-0343].
  • Gap: the base has no row on the test for who "does" the act when a server acts on a user's command.

A3. Does the home recording statute or the Sony fair use finding protect private stem splitting and mixing?

  • Rows that frame it: section 1008 bars actions over a consumer's noncommercial use of listed devices and media [M-0572, M-0573]. The Ninth Circuit kept computer hard drives outside the Act [M-0574, M-0575] and described its purpose as private, noncommercial use [M-0576]. Sony's fair use finding was about home time-shifting of TV [M-0561]. Section 117 names only computer programs [M-0577].

A4. What can we say in marketing, onboarding and the product itself without inducing infringement?

  • Why it matters: the tool maker cases turn on the maker's own words and design.
  • Rows that frame it: Sony's staple article rule [M-0560]. Grokster's inducement rule [M-0562, M-0563]. Cox's intent rule and the "tailored to infringement" route [M-0221, M-0222, M-0223].
  • The researchers' reading that inducement reaches a stem tool promoted for commercial songs is not confirmed by any case (unverified) [M-0564].

A5. Which theories did Cox leave open against us?

  • Rows that frame it: vicarious liability was not decided (analysis row) [M-0252]. A concurrence pointed to other common-law theories [M-0225]. The Court rejected knowledge plus inaction for contributory liability [M-0224].

A6. Can we ship the stem model we want?

  • Why it matters: a bad model licence is a problem separate from the music.
  • Rows that frame it: code licence silent on weights [M-0600]; maintainer statements that the weights licence is unclear and that he cannot grant one [M-0601, M-0602, M-0603]; non-commercial weights [M-0606]; silent projects [M-0604, M-0607, M-0609, M-0610]; training set with non-commercial tracks [M-0614].
  • Ask: does a non-commercial limit on training data bind the weights, who could enforce it, and who can give a commercial grant?

Claims cited on this page

Each claim gives its status, source, pinpoint, jurisdiction, the date it was checked and a verbatim quote from the source.

  1. [M-0110] VERIFIED. The sound recording reproduction right covers only duplication that recaptures the actual sounds.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "is limited to the right to duplicate the sound recording in the form of phonorecords or copies that directly or indirectly recapture the actual sounds fixed in the recording."

  2. [M-0523] VERIFIED. The sound recording owner's derivative right (106(2)) is limited to works in which the actual sounds fixed in the recording are rearranged, remixed, or otherwise altered in sequence or quality.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "limited to the right to prepare a derivative work in which the actual sounds fixed in the sound recording are rearranged, remixed, or otherwise altered in sequence or quality"

  3. [M-0522] VERIFIED. The copyright owner has the exclusive right to prepare derivative works, and to authorize others to do so.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "the owner of copyright under this title has the exclusive rights to do and to authorize any of the following"

  4. [M-0220] VERIFIED. Anyone who violates an exclusive right of the copyright owner is an infringer.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 501 infringement of copyright. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 501(a). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Anyone who violates any of the exclusive rights of the copyright owner as provided by sections 106 through"

  5. [M-0343] VERIFIED. The 512(c) safe harbor covers infringement by reason of storage at the direction of a user on the provider's system.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "for infringement of copyright by reason of the storage at the direction of a user of material that resides on a system or network controlled or operated by or for the service provider"

  6. [M-0572] VERIFIED. The Audio Home Recording Act bars infringement actions based on the manufacture, importation or distribution of a digital audio recording device, digital audio recording medium, analog recording device or analog recording medium, or on a consumer's noncommercial use of such a device or medium for making digital or analog musical recordings.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 1008 Prohibition on certain infringement actions. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 1008. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "based on the noncommercial use by a consumer of such a device or medium for making digital musical recordings or analog musical recordings"

  7. [M-0573] VERIFIED. The Act's digital audio recording device definition turns on a device whose digital recording function is designed or marketed for the primary purpose of making a digital audio copied recording for private use.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 1001 Definitions (Audio Home Recording). Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 1001(3). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "designed or marketed for the primary purpose of, and that is capable of, making a digital audio copied recording for private use"

  8. [M-0574] VERIFIED. RIAA v. Diamond (9th Cir. 1999): the Rio player was not a digital audio recording device because it copies only from a computer hard drive, not from transmissions or digital musical recordings.

    Source: RIAA v. Diamond Multimedia, 180 F.3d 1072 (9th Cir. 1999). Pinpoint: 180 F.3d 1072, end of Part IV. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "Because the Rio cannot make copies from transmissions, but instead, can only make copies from a computer hard drive, it is not a digital audio recording device."

  9. [M-0575] VERIFIED. RIAA v. Diamond (9th Cir. 1999): a computer hard drive is excluded from the Act's definition of a digital music recording, so the Rio, which copies from a hard drive, does not record directly from a digital music recording.

    Source: RIAA v. Diamond Multimedia, 180 F.3d 1072 (9th Cir. 1999). Pinpoint: 180 F.3d 1072, Part III. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "a hard drive is a material object in which one or more programs are fixed; thus, a hard drive is excluded from the definition of digital music recordings"

  10. [M-0576] VERIFIED. RIAA v. Diamond describes the Act's purpose as the consumer right to make recordings of copyrighted music for private, noncommercial use, quoting the Senate Report.

    Source: RIAA v. Diamond Multimedia, 180 F.3d 1072 (9th Cir. 1999). Pinpoint: 180 F.3d 1072, Part III. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "The purpose of the Act is to ensure the right of consumers to make analog or digital audio recordings of copyrighted music for their private, noncommercial use"

  11. [M-0561] VERIFIED. Sony v. Universal: the VTR was capable of substantial noninfringing uses both because many copyright holders would not object to time-shifting and because unauthorised private, noncommercial home time-shifting of broadcast television was fair use.

    Source: Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, 464 U.S. 417 (1984). Pinpoint: 464 U.S. 417, syllabus (Pp. 442-456). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "even the unauthorized home timeshifting of respondents' programs is legitimate fair use"

  12. [M-0577] VERIFIED. 17 U.S.C. 117(a) lets the owner of a copy of a computer program make or authorise another copy or adaptation of that program on stated conditions (an essential step in using it with a machine, or archival purposes); its text names only computer programs.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 117 Computer programs. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 117(a). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "it is not an infringement for the owner of a copy of a computer program to make or authorize the making of another copy or adaptation of that computer program"

  13. [M-0560] VERIFIED. Sony v. Universal (1984): selling copying equipment is not contributory infringement if the product is widely used for legitimate purposes or is merely capable of substantial noninfringing uses.

    Source: Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, 464 U.S. 417 (1984). Pinpoint: 464 U.S. 417, syllabus (Pp. 434-442). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "does not constitute contributory infringement if the product is widely used for legitimate, unobjectionable purposes, or, indeed, is merely capable of substantial noninfringing uses"

  14. [M-0562] VERIFIED. Grokster (2005): one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe, shown by clear expression or other affirmative steps, is liable for third-party infringement.

    Source: MGM Studios v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005). Pinpoint: 545 U.S. 913, 919 (syllabus Held; opinion at 936-937). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe copyright, as shown by clear expression or other"

  15. [M-0563] VERIFIED. Grokster: mere knowledge that a product could be or is used to infringe is not enough for liability, and ordinary product support is not inducement.

    Source: MGM Studios v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005). Pinpoint: 545 U.S. 913, 937. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "mere knowledge of infringing potential or of actual infringing uses would not be enough here to subject a distributor to liability"

  16. [M-0221] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): contributory liability requires that the provider intended its service be used for infringement, shown only by inducement or a service tailored to infringement.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Held: The provider of a service is contributorily liable for a user’s infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement,"

  17. [M-0222] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient to establish the required intent.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), Part II.B. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient to establish the required intent to infringe."

  18. [M-0223] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): intent can be shown by inducement or by a service tailored to infringement (not capable of substantial or commercially significant noninfringing uses).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "A service is tailored to infringement if it is “not capable of ‘substantial’ or ‘commercially significant’ noninfringing uses.”"

  19. [M-0564] UNVERIFIED (unverified). Analysis: a stem-splitting or mixing tool maker whose marketing or onboarding steers users to split and mix commercial songs they do not own is exposed to Grokster inducement through its own statements and design, not through the tool's capability alone.

    Source: MGM Studios v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005). Pinpoint: 545 U.S. 913, 919. Jurisdiction: analysis. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: analysis. Checked 2026-11-10.
