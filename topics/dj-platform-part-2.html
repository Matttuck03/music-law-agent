What needs no label deal (lowest risk, not cleared)

These are the lowest-risk pieces. None is risk-free. Each has open questions for the attorney.

  • A mixing and stem tool that runs on the user's own device, where no commercial audio reaches the platform's servers.
  • Why (analysis): selling a product that is capable of substantial lawful uses is not, by that fact alone, contributory infringement [M-0560], and mere knowledge that some users infringe is not enough for contributory liability [M-0563, M-0222]. No saved case applies these rulings to a stem or mixing tool, and they do not cover direct or vicarious liability [M-0220, M-0252].
  • Condition: marketing, onboarding and design must not steer users to commercial hits [M-0562, M-0223].
  • Open: whether a stem split is an infringing copy, even in private, is unsettled [M-0110]. The home recording statute does not clearly help [M-0572, M-0575].
  • Mixing of music where the user owns both the song and the recording, or has written permission from the owners of both.
  • Why: the owner of a work may authorise derivative works [M-0522], and the song and the recording are separate works [M-0025]. Get every permission in a signed writing (good practice; a transfer of ownership is not valid without one) [M-0040].
  • An opt-in catalogue: artists and small labels who own both the song and the recording and grant the platform remix rights in writing.
  • Why: this is a direct licence from the owners of both works [M-0025, M-0522]. No major label deal is needed for music the majors do not control (analysis).
  • Condition: the grant must cover copying, stem splitting, derivative works, public performance and any download or export the product offers (analysis) [M-0108, M-0106, M-0107].
  • Public sharing of mixes built wholly from that opt-in catalogue, or from music the user owns on both sides, the song and the recording [M-0025]. Condition (analysis): the grants cover public performance and sharing [M-0106, M-0107], and the platform can act when a user's ownership claim turns out to be false [M-0345, M-0348].
  • User covers, audio only, where the user records every sound. No label is involved, because no original recording is used (analysis) [M-0111]. Publisher-side licences are still needed: a mechanical licence for each song, which is compulsory only if the statute's steps are followed [M-0339, M-0545], and public performance licences for the songs if the platform streams the covers (see card M05) [M-0183]. Whether the platform itself can use the blanket mechanical licence is an open question for counsel [M-0122]. Limits are in card M22 [M-0540, M-0542].
  • The compliance kit, which costs nothing in deals: a registered copyright agent [M-0349], notice and takedown [M-0348], counter notice [M-0355], and a repeat infringer policy [M-0357].
  • Needed before any item above: a stem model with a clean licence for its weights. This is a precondition, not something that can launch today. None of the public models checked on 2026-11-13 has weights with a stated licence that clearly allows commercial use [M-0603, M-0606, M-0604]. See card M24.

Claims cited on this page

Each claim gives its status, source, pinpoint, jurisdiction, the date it was checked and a verbatim quote from the source.

  1. [M-0560] VERIFIED. Sony v. Universal (1984): selling copying equipment is not contributory infringement if the product is widely used for legitimate purposes or is merely capable of substantial noninfringing uses.

    Source: Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, 464 U.S. 417 (1984). Pinpoint: 464 U.S. 417, syllabus (Pp. 434-442). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "does not constitute contributory infringement if the product is widely used for legitimate, unobjectionable purposes, or, indeed, is merely capable of substantial noninfringing uses"

  2. [M-0563] VERIFIED. Grokster: mere knowledge that a product could be or is used to infringe is not enough for liability, and ordinary product support is not inducement.

    Source: MGM Studios v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005). Pinpoint: 545 U.S. 913, 937. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "mere knowledge of infringing potential or of actual infringing uses would not be enough here to subject a distributor to liability"

  3. [M-0222] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient to establish the required intent.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), Part II.B. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient to establish the required intent to infringe."

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 501 infringement of copyright. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 501(a). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Anyone who violates any of the exclusive rights of the copyright owner as provided by sections 106 through"

  5. [M-0252] VERIFIED. Analysis: after Cox v. Sony (U.S. 2026) a service provider is contributorily liable for a user's infringement only if it induced the infringement or the service is tailored to it; knowing that some users infringe is not enough. Vicarious liability is a separate theory that the Court did not decide (cert limited to contributory).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: analysis. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Held: The provider of a service is contributorily liable for a user’s infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement,"

  6. [M-0562] VERIFIED. Grokster (2005): one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe, shown by clear expression or other affirmative steps, is liable for third-party infringement.

    Source: MGM Studios v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005). Pinpoint: 545 U.S. 913, 919 (syllabus Held; opinion at 936-937). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe copyright, as shown by clear expression or other"

  7. [M-0223] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): intent can be shown by inducement or by a service tailored to infringement (not capable of substantial or commercially significant noninfringing uses).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "A service is tailored to infringement if it is “not capable of ‘substantial’ or ‘commercially significant’ noninfringing uses.”"

  8. [M-0110] VERIFIED. The sound recording reproduction right covers only duplication that recaptures the actual sounds.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "is limited to the right to duplicate the sound recording in the form of phonorecords or copies that directly or indirectly recapture the actual sounds fixed in the recording."

  9. [M-0572] VERIFIED. The Audio Home Recording Act bars infringement actions based on the manufacture, importation or distribution of a digital audio recording device, digital audio recording medium, analog recording device or analog recording medium, or on a consumer's noncommercial use of such a device or medium for making digital or analog musical recordings.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 1008 Prohibition on certain infringement actions. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 1008. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "based on the noncommercial use by a consumer of such a device or medium for making digital musical recordings or analog musical recordings"

  10. [M-0575] VERIFIED. RIAA v. Diamond (9th Cir. 1999): a computer hard drive is excluded from the Act's definition of a digital music recording, so the Rio, which copies from a hard drive, does not record directly from a digital music recording.

    Quote: "a hard drive is a material object in which one or more programs are fixed; thus, a hard drive is excluded from the definition of digital music recordings"

  13. [M-0040] VERIFIED. A transfer of copyright ownership, other than by operation of law, is not valid unless in a writing signed by the owner of the rights conveyed or the owner's authorized agent.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 204 execution of transfers of copyright ownership. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 204(a). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

  14. [M-0108] VERIFIED. Both musical works and sound recordings carry the reproduction right (copies or phonorecords).

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "to reproduce the copyrighted work in copies or phonorecords;"

  15. [M-0106] VERIFIED. Musical works carry an exclusive right of public performance.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(4). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "in the case of literary, musical, dramatic, and choreographic works, pantomimes, and motion pictures and other audiovisual works, to perform the copyrighted work publicly;"

  16. [M-0107] VERIFIED. Sound recordings carry a public performance right only by digital audio transmission.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(6). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "in the case of sound recordings, to perform the copyrighted work publicly by means of a digital audio transmission."

  17. [M-0345] VERIFIED. Absent actual knowledge, the provider must not be aware of facts or circumstances from which infringement is apparent (red flag knowledge).

  18. [M-0348] VERIFIED. On a compliant notice the provider must respond expeditiously to remove or disable the claimed material.

  19. [M-0111] VERIFIED. Sound-alike recordings that are an independent fixation do not infringe the sound recording copyright (114(b) imitation rule). The musical work remains a separate copyright, so (analysis) a sound-alike still needs a musical work licence.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "do not extend to the making or duplication of another sound recording that consists entirely of an independent fixation of other sounds, even though such sounds imitate or simulate those in the copyrighted sound recording."

  21. [M-0545] VERIFIED.
