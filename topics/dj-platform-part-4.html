DJ-mix platform playbook

Part 4 of 7: What stays off limits. Law last checked 2026-11-13.

Parts: 1 2 3 4 5 6 7

What stays off limits

  • Marketing or onboarding that invites users to split and mix commercial songs they do not own. This is the conduct Grokster punishes [M-0562].
  • Presets, search or templates built around named hit records. This risks the "tailored to infringement" finding in Cox [M-0223].
  • A platform-built shared library of stems split from commercial tracks. That is the platform's own copying, not user storage (analysis) [M-0110, M-0220, M-0343].
  • Public downloads of mixes that contain unlicensed commercial tracks [M-0108, M-0582].
  • Relying on the 114 statutory licence for saved or on-demand mixes [M-0115, M-0578].
  • Telling users any of these: that changing a song enough makes it theirs [M-0534, M-0552]; that crediting the artist makes it fine [M-0220]; that a set number of seconds is fair use [M-0203, M-0205]; that a cover licence covers a remix [M-0542, M-0540]; that not selling it makes it legal [M-0220, M-0549].
  • Telling users they own their mixes without limits. They own at most what they added [M-0529], and nothing in parts that use others' material unlawfully [M-0528].
  • Shipping stem model weights in a paid product when the weights are stated non-commercial or research-only [M-0606, M-0603], or when the licence is silent and no one has given a written grant [M-0604, M-0609].
  • Ads or creator payouts tied to mixes of unlicensed tracks. A direct financial benefit from infringement the platform can control costs it the safe harbor [M-0347, M-0515].
  • Ignoring takedown notices or repeat infringers [M-0348, M-0357].

Claims cited on this page

Each claim gives its status, source, pinpoint, jurisdiction, the date it was checked and a verbatim quote from the source.

  1. [M-0562] VERIFIED. Grokster (2005): one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe, shown by clear expression or other affirmative steps, is liable for third-party infringement.

    Source: MGM Studios v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005). Pinpoint: 545 U.S. 913, 919 (syllabus Held; opinion at 936-937). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe copyright, as shown by clear expression or other"

  2. [M-0223] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): intent can be shown by inducement or by a service tailored to infringement (not capable of substantial or commercially significant noninfringing uses).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "A service is tailored to infringement if it is “not capable of ‘substantial’ or ‘commercially significant’ noninfringing uses.”"

  3. [M-0110] VERIFIED. The sound recording reproduction right covers only duplication that recaptures the actual sounds.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "is limited to the right to duplicate the sound recording in the form of phonorecords or copies that directly or indirectly recapture the actual sounds fixed in the recording."

  4. [M-0220] VERIFIED. Anyone who violates an exclusive right of the copyright owner is an infringer.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 501 infringement of copyright. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 501(a). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Anyone who violates any of the exclusive rights of the copyright owner as provided by sections 106 through"

  5. [M-0343] VERIFIED. The 512(c) safe harbor covers infringement by reason of storage at the direction of a user on the provider's system.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "for infringement of copyright by reason of the storage at the direction of a user of material that resides on a system or network controlled or operated by or for the service provider"

  6. [M-0108] VERIFIED. Both musical works and sound recordings carry the reproduction right (copies or phonorecords).

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "to reproduce the copyrighted work in copies or phonorecords;"

  7. [M-0582] VERIFIED. The statutory licence requires the service to take no affirmative steps to cause or induce the recipient to make a phonorecord, so a download button on mixes is outside it.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2)(C)(vi). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "the transmitting entity takes no affirmative steps to cause or induce the making of a phonorecord by the transmission recipient"

  8. [M-0115] VERIFIED. The statutory licence requires that the transmission not be part of an interactive service.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2)(A)(i). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "the transmission is not part of an interactive service;"

  9. [M-0578] VERIFIED. The 114(d)(2) statutory licence for eligible nonsubscription transmissions requires that the transmission is not part of an archived program of less than 5 hours.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2)(C)(iii)(I). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "is not part of an archived program of less than 5 hours duration"

  10. [M-0534] VERIFIED. Pickett rejected the idea that a stranger may make a derivative work without permission if the original does not pervade the derivative; it would undermine the 106(2) right.

    Source: Pickett v. Prince, 207 F.3d 402 (7th Cir. 2000). Pinpoint: 207 F.3d 402. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "would inject enormous uncertainty into the law of copyright and undermine the exclusive right that section 106(2) gives the owner of the copyright on the original work"

  11. [M-0552] VERIFIED. Warhol: reading Campbell to favour any use adding new expression would let transformative use swallow the owner's right to prepare derivative works.

    Source: Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, Inc. v. Goldsmith, 598 U.S. 508 (2023). Pinpoint: 598 U.S. 508, syllabus. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "transformative use would swallow the copyright owner’s exclusive right to prepare derivative works"

  12. [M-0203] VERIFIED. Section 107 makes fair use a four-factor, non-exhaustive test; the statute states no number of seconds.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 107 fair use. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 107. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "In determining whether the use made of a work in any particular case is a fair use the factors to be considered shall include--"

  13. [M-0205] VERIFIED. Campbell: fair use requires case-by-case analysis and is not simplified by bright-line rules.

    Source: Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569 (1994) (U.S. Reports via Library of Congress). Pinpoint: 510 U.S. 569, 577 (Part II.A, opinion of the Court). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "The task is not to be simplified with bright-line rules, for the statute, like the doctrine it recognizes, calls for case-by-case analysis."

  14. [M-0542] VERIFIED. The compulsory licence is for making and distributing phonorecords of a nondramatic musical work (the composition); it is not a licence of any sound recording.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 115 compulsory license for making and distributing phonorecords. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 115(a)(1)(A)-(B). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "obtain a compulsory license to make and distribute phonorecords of a nondramatic musical work"

  15. [M-0540] VERIFIED. A compulsory licence under 115 includes the privilege of an arrangement only to the extent necessary to conform the work to the style of the performance; it may not change the basic melody or fundamental character.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 115 compulsory license for making and distributing phonorecords. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 115(a)(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "the arrangement shall not change the basic melody or fundamental character of the work"

  16. [M-0549] VERIFIED. Campbell: a commercial nature is only one element of factor one; no presumption that every commercial use is unfair.

    Source: Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569 (1994) (U.S. Reports via Library of Congress). Pinpoint: 510 U.S. 569, syllabus. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "The statute makes clear that a work's commercial nature is only one element of the first factor enquiry into its purpose and character"

  17. [M-0529] VERIFIED. Copyright in a derivative work or compilation covers only the new material the author contributed and gives no exclusive right in the preexisting material.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 103 compilations and derivative works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 103(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "The copyright in a compilation or derivative work extends only to the material contributed by the author of such work"

  18. [M-0528] VERIFIED. Under Section 103(a), protection for a work employing preexisting copyrighted material does not extend to any part of the work in which the material has been used unlawfully.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 103 compilations and derivative works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 103(a). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "protection for a work employing preexisting material in which copyright subsists does not extend to any part of the work in which such material has been used unlawfully"

  23. [M-0347] VERIFIED. The provider must not receive a financial benefit directly attributable to infringement where it has the right and ability to control it.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1)(B). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

  24. [M-0515] VERIFIED. Ninth Circuit (Mavrix v. LiveJournal, 2017): the 512(c) financial benefit test is a common-sense, fact-based inquiry, and the benefit need not be substantial or a large proportion of revenue.

    Quote: "The financial benefit need not be substantial or a large proportion of the service provider’s revenue."

  25. [M-0348] VERIFIED. On a compliant notice the provider must respond expeditiously to remove or disable the claimed material.
