Decision page: the two open choices

Two choices are not yet made. Each path below lists what it needs. The four combinations follow.

Choice A: private-only use, or public release

  • Path A1, private only. Users mix and save on their own device. Nothing is shared through the platform.
  • Needs: on-device processing where possible; no public links; lawful-use marketing; a stem model with a clean licence; clear user terms.
  • Rights in play: copies and the derivative work question on the user's side [M-0110, M-0522]. No public performance by the platform (analysis) [M-0106].
  • Main risk: tool maker liability if the product is promoted or shaped for infringement [M-0562, M-0223]. The open stem copy question [M-0110].
  • Who carries it: mostly the user (analysis). The platform as tool maker.
  • What it gives up: sharing, audience, most network growth.
  • Path A2, public release. Users publish mixes for others to hear.
  • Needs: licences for recordings and songs [M-0301, M-0106, M-0107], or a licensed partner; the full 512(c) kit as a backstop [M-0343]; a plan for the EU [M-0387].
  • Main risk: direct and secondary liability for hosted and streamed mixes; loss of the safe harbor through red flag knowledge or financial benefit [M-0345, M-0347]; statutory damages per work [M-0215, M-0217].
  • Who carries it: the user and the platform together.

Choice B: any music, or a licensed catalogue

  • Path B1, any music. Users bring any track, including commercial hits.
  • Needs: the full 512(c) kit [M-0343, M-0349, M-0357]; design and marketing that do not induce [M-0562]; a view from counsel on stems stored server-side.
  • Main risk: most uploads will be tracks no one cleared. The safe harbor has knowledge and financial benefit conditions [M-0344, M-0345, M-0347]. Cox narrowed contributory liability but left vicarious liability open [M-0222, M-0252].
  • Path B2, licensed catalogue. Users mix only tracks the platform has rights to: opt-in artists, direct deals, or a partner's catalogue.
  • Needs: written grants that cover remixing, since remixing is a derivative work right [M-0522, M-0523], plus the performance and copy rights the features use.
  • Main risk: small catalogue at first; the cost and time of deals. Legal risk is far lower because the owners agreed (analysis).

The four combinations

  • A1 plus B1, private tool, any music. Launch needs: no label or publisher deals, but a stem model whose weights are licensed for commercial use [M-0603, M-0606], marketing and design that do not steer users to commercial hits [M-0562, M-0223], and counsel's answer on whether a stem split is itself an infringing copy [M-0110]. Risk: unsettled and not rated. No saved case applies the tool maker rulings to a stem or mixing tool [M-0560, M-0562, M-0221], and the home recording statute does not clearly help [M-0575].
  • A1 plus B2, private tool, licensed catalogue. Launch needs: opt-in grants. Risk: lowest of the four (analysis), if the grants cover both the song and the recording and the stem model licence is clean.
  • A2 plus B2, public release, licensed catalogue. Launch needs: grants that cover public performance and sharing. Risk: low (analysis) if the grants are complete and each grantor really owns both the song and the recording. This is the first public feature to ship.
  • A2 plus B1, public release, any music. Launch needs: label and publisher deals or a licensed partner [M-0589, M-0593]. Without them the platform stands on the safe harbor alone [M-0343], in a product whose purpose makes red flag knowledge more likely (analysis) [M-0345]. Risk: highest. Do not ship this without counsel's sign-off.

Claims cited on this page

Each claim gives its status, source, pinpoint, jurisdiction, the date it was checked and a verbatim quote from the source.

  1. [M-0110] VERIFIED. The sound recording reproduction right covers only duplication that recaptures the actual sounds.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "is limited to the right to duplicate the sound recording in the form of phonorecords or copies that directly or indirectly recapture the actual sounds fixed in the recording."

  2. [M-0522] VERIFIED. The copyright owner has the exclusive right to prepare derivative works, and to authorize others to do so.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "the owner of copyright under this title has the exclusive rights to do and to authorize any of the following"

  3. [M-0106] VERIFIED. Musical works carry an exclusive right of public performance.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(4). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "in the case of literary, musical, dramatic, and choreographic works, pantomimes, and motion pictures and other audiovisual works, to perform the copyrighted work publicly;"

  4. [M-0562] VERIFIED. Grokster (2005): one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe, shown by clear expression or other affirmative steps, is liable for third-party infringement.

    Source: MGM Studios v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005). Pinpoint: 545 U.S. 913, 919 (syllabus Held; opinion at 936-937). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe copyright, as shown by clear expression or other"

  5. [M-0223] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): intent can be shown by inducement or by a service tailored to infringement (not capable of substantial or commercially significant noninfringing uses).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "A service is tailored to infringement if it is “not capable of ‘substantial’ or ‘commercially significant’ noninfringing uses.”"

  6. [M-0301] VERIFIED. An interactive service may not publicly perform a sound recording unless a licence has been granted for the public performance of any copyrighted musical work contained in it.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(3)(C). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "an interactive service may not publicly perform a sound recording unless a license has been granted for the public performance of any copyrighted musical work contained in the sound recording"

  7. [M-0107] VERIFIED. Sound recordings carry a public performance right only by digital audio transmission.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(6). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "in the case of sound recordings, to perform the copyrighted work publicly by means of a digital audio transmission."

  8. [M-0343] VERIFIED. The 512(c) safe harbor covers infringement by reason of storage at the direction of a user on the provider's system.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "for infringement of copyright by reason of the storage at the direction of a user of material that resides on a system or network controlled or operated by or for the service provider"

  9. [M-0387] VERIFIED. CDSM Art 17(1): an online content-sharing service provider performs an act of communication to the public when it gives access to user uploads, and must get authorisation.

    Source: Directive (EU) 2019/790 on copyright and related rights in the Digital Single Market (CDSM Directive). Pinpoint: Art. 17(1). Jurisdiction: EU. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "performs an act of communication to the public or an act of making available to the public for the purposes of this Directive when it gives the public access to copyright-protected works"

  10. [M-0345] VERIFIED. Absent actual knowledge, the provider must not be aware of facts or circumstances from which infringement is apparent (red flag knowledge).

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1)(A)(ii). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "is not aware of facts or circumstances from which infringing activity is apparent"

  11. [M-0347] VERIFIED. The provider must not receive a financial benefit directly attributable to infringement where it has the right and ability to control it.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1)(B). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "does not receive a financial benefit directly attributable to the infringing activity, in a case in which the service provider has the right and ability to control such activity"

  12. [M-0215] VERIFIED. Statutory damages range from $750 to $30,000 per work, as the court considers just, at the owner's election.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 504 remedies, damages and profits. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 504(c)(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "in a sum of not less than $750 or more than $30,000 as the court considers just."

  13. [M-0217] VERIFIED. Willful infringement raises the statutory maximum to $150,000 per work.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 504 remedies, damages and profits. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 504(c)(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "the court in its discretion may increase the award of statutory damages to a sum of not more than $150,000."

  14. [M-0349] VERIFIED. The 512(c) safe harbor applies only if the provider has designated an agent, by making it available on its service including its website and by providing it to the Copyright Office.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "by making available through its service, including on its website in a location accessible to the public, and by providing to the Copyright Office"

  15. [M-0357] VERIFIED. Under 512(i)(1)(A) a provider must have adopted and reasonably implemented, and inform subscribers of, a policy providing for termination in appropriate circumstances of subscribers and account holders who are repeat infringers; the 512 limitations apply only if so.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(i)(1)(A). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "has adopted and reasonably implemented, and informs subscribers and account holders of the service provider’s system or network of, a policy that provides for the termination in appropriate circumstances of subscribers and account holders"

  16. [M-0344] VERIFIED. The provider must lack actual knowledge that material is infringing.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1)(A)(i). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "does not have actual knowledge that the material or an activity using the material on the system or network is infringing"

  17. [M-0222] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient to establish the required intent.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), Part II.B. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient to establish the required intent to infringe."

  18. [M-0252] VERIFIED. Analysis: after Cox v. Sony (U.S. 2026) a service provider is contributorily liable for a user's infringement only if it induced the infringement or the service is tailored to it; knowing that some users infringe is not enough. Vicarious liability is a separate theory that the Court did not decide (cert limited to contributory).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: analysis. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Held: The provider of a service is contributorily liable for a user’s infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement,"

  19. [M-0523] VERIFIED. The sound recording owner's derivative right (106(2)) is limited to works in which the actual sounds fixed in the recording are rearranged, remixed, or otherwise altered in sequence or quality.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "limited to the right to prepare a derivative work in which the actual sounds fixed in the sound recording are rearranged, remixed, or otherwise altered in sequence or quality"

  21. [M-0606] VERIFIED. Open-Unmix README: the default umxl weights are licensed for non-commercial use only (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).

  22. [M-0560] VERIFIED. Sony v. Universal (1984): selling copying equipment is not contributory infringement if the product is widely used for legitimate purposes or is merely capable of substantial noninfringing uses.

    Source: Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, 464 U.S. 417 (1984). Pinpoint: 464 U.S. 417, syllabus (Pp. 434-442). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "does not constitute contributory infringement if the product is widely used for legitimate, unobjectionable purposes, or, indeed, is merely capable of substantial noninfringing uses"

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

  24. [M-0575] VERIFIED. RIAA v. Diamond (9th Cir. 1999): a computer hard drive is excluded from the Act's definition of a digital music recording, so the Rio, which copies from a hard drive, does not record directly from a digital music recording.

    Quote: "a hard drive is a material object in which one or more programs are fixed; thus, a hard drive is excluded from the definition of digital music recordings"
