DJ-mix platform playbook

Part 6 of 7: Risk register, Rollout order. Law last checked 2026-11-13.

Parts: 1 2 3 4 5 6 7

Risk register

  • Risk 1, inducement by marketing or design.
  • What: the platform is held liable for users' infringement because of its own words or design [M-0562, M-0223]. The researchers' reading that this applies to a stem or mixing tool is their analysis and no case confirms it (unverified) [M-0564].
  • Who carries it: platform.
  • Lower it: lawful-use marketing, no hit-based presets, written review of all launch copy.
  • Risk 2, direct liability for server-side copies and streams.
  • What: the platform itself copies, splits, renders or streams works [M-0220, M-0110]. The base has no row on the test for who "does" the act (gap).
  • Who carries it: platform.
  • Lower it: process on the device; store per user only; ask counsel.
  • Risk 3, loss of the safe harbor.
  • What: red flag knowledge without removal [M-0345, M-0346], direct financial benefit [M-0347, M-0515], no registered agent or a lapsed one [M-0349, M-0354], no real repeat infringer policy [M-0357].
  • Who carries it: platform.
  • Lower it: run the kit from day one; keep money away from unlicensed mixes.
  • Risk 4, statutory damages at scale.
  • What: $750 to $30,000 per work, up to $150,000 if willful [M-0215, M-0217], counted per work (analysis row) [M-0254]. One mix can hold many works.
  • Who carries it: user and platform.
  • Lower it: licensed catalogue; fast takedown; no public downloads.
  • Risk 5, the stem copy question.
  • What: a court could hold that splitting a track is an infringing reproduction even in private [M-0110]. Unsettled.
  • Who carries it: user first; platform if the split happens on its servers.
  • Lower it: on-device splitting; attorney opinion before launch.
  • Risk 6, stem model licence.
  • What: weights that are non-commercial, research-only or silent [M-0606, M-0603, M-0604], trained on a set with non-commercial tracks [M-0614].
  • Who carries it: platform.
  • Lower it: licensed or self-trained model; written grant.
  • Risk 7, wrong statutory licence.
  • What: treating saved mixes as covered by section 114 [M-0115, M-0578, M-0579] or remixes as covered by a cover licence [M-0542].
  • Who carries it: platform.
  • Lower it: use these licences only where card M22 and card M23 say they fit.
  • Risk 8, foreign law.
  • What: EU platform liability for uploads [M-0387, M-0388]; UK and Australian moral rights [M-0628, M-0626]; narrow exceptions only [M-0240, M-0622]; Canada's exception limited to non-commercial acts by individuals [M-0616].
  • Who carries it: platform and user.
  • Lower it: launch in one country first; get local advice before each new country.
  • Risk 9, user ownership claims.
  • What: users believe they own mixes and try to license or monetise them [M-0529, M-0528].
  • Who carries it: user; platform through support load and disputes.
  • Lower it: plain terms and in-app wording.
  • Risk 10, vicarious liability.
  • What: liability based on control plus financial benefit. Cox did not decide it (analysis row) [M-0252].
  • Who carries it: platform.
  • Lower it: pricing that does not earn from infringing mixes; counsel review.

Rollout order

  • Step 0, before building further.
  • Put the attorney questions (the page "Questions for a licensed music attorney") to a licensed music attorney.
  • Pick a stem model with a clean weights licence or get a written grant.
  • Write marketing rules: lawful uses only, no hit names.
  • Step 1, private tool. On-device mixing and stem splitting, local save, no sharing. User's own files.
  • Step 2, opt-in catalogue. Sign written remix grants with independent artists and labels. Let users mix that catalogue.
  • Step 3, public sharing of mixes built wholly from the opt-in catalogue or the user's own music. Launch the 512(c) kit at the same time.
  • Step 4, cloud save for private mixes, per user, only after counsel confirms the storage design.
  • Step 5, commercial tracks in public mixes, only through direct deals or a licensed partner.
  • Step 6, live mixing, on the same licensed footing as Step 5.
  • Step 7, downloads and export, only for content whose licences cover downloads.
  • Step 8, other countries, one at a time, with local advice. Canada, the UK, the EU and Australia each have their own rules (card M25).

  1. [M-0562] VERIFIED. Grokster (2005): one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe, shown by clear expression or other affirmative steps, is liable for third-party infringement.

    Source: MGM Studios v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005). Pinpoint: 545 U.S. 913, 919 (syllabus Held; opinion at 936-937). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe copyright, as shown by clear expression or other"

  2. [M-0223] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): intent can be shown by inducement or by a service tailored to infringement (not capable of substantial or commercially significant noninfringing uses).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "A service is tailored to infringement if it is “not capable of ‘substantial’ or ‘commercially significant’ noninfringing uses.”"
