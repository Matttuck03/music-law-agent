What this page is

  • A plain-words map for the team building a platform where ordinary people mix existing songs: blends, transitions, mashups, recorded mixes and maybe live mixes, fed by a tool that splits a song into stems.
  • It assumes users may feed in anything, including commercial hits.
  • It covers US law in depth and flags Canada, Australia, the UK and the EU where they differ sharply.
  • Every legal statement carries a row id in square brackets. Rows marked "(analysis)" are reasoning, not quoted law. Where the law is unsettled or the base has no source, the page says so.
  • Detail lives in cards M21 (remixes), M22 (covers), M23 (stem tools and DJ mixes), M24 (licensed models, distributors, stem model licences) and M25 (feature by feature).

The ground rules in six lines

  • Every track is two copyrights: the song and the recording [M-0025]. Each owner controls copies and derivative works [M-0108, M-0522].
  • Remixing the actual recorded sounds is named in the statute as part of the recording owner's rights [M-0523].
  • Playing songs to the public needs a performance licence [M-0106]. Streaming recordings online does too [M-0107].
  • No saved source shows a remix exception or a compulsory remix licence in US law. A 2016 government paper looked at both and recommended neither (policy paper, not law) [M-0556].
  • A tool maker's contributory liability turns on intent: what it says and what it builds the product to do [M-0562, M-0221, M-0223]. Vicarious and direct liability are separate theories that the 2026 ruling did not decide (analysis row) [M-0252].
  • A host of user uploads can use the 512(c) safe harbor only if it meets every condition [M-0343, M-0345, M-0347, M-0349, M-0357].

  2. [M-0108] VERIFIED. Both musical works and sound recordings carry the reproduction right (copies or phonorecords).

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "to reproduce the copyrighted work in copies or phonorecords;"

  3. [M-0522] VERIFIED. The copyright owner has the exclusive right to prepare derivative works, and to authorize others to do so.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "the owner of copyright under this title has the exclusive rights to do and to authorize any of the following"

  4. [M-0523] VERIFIED. The sound recording owner's derivative right (106(2)) is limited to works in which the actual sounds fixed in the recording are rearranged, remixed, or otherwise altered in sequence or quality.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "limited to the right to prepare a derivative work in which the actual sounds fixed in the sound recording are rearranged, remixed, or otherwise altered in sequence or quality"

  5. [M-0106] VERIFIED. Musical works carry an exclusive right of public performance.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(4). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "in the case of literary, musical, dramatic, and choreographic works, pantomimes, and motion pictures and other audiovisual works, to perform the copyrighted work publicly;"

  6. [M-0107] VERIFIED. Sound recordings carry a public performance right only by digital audio transmission.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(6). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "in the case of sound recordings, to perform the copyrighted work publicly by means of a digital audio transmission."

  7. [M-0556] VERIFIED. The Task Force concluded the record had not established a need to create a specific remix exception or a compulsory licence for remix uses, and recommended guidelines, more voluntary licensing and education.

    Source: Dept of Commerce Internet Policy Task Force, White Paper on Remixes, First Sale, and Statutory Damages (Jan. 2016). Pinpoint: White Paper (Jan. 2016), p. 4. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "the record has not established a need to amend existing law to create a specific exception or a compulsory license for remix uses"

  8. [M-0562] VERIFIED. Grokster (2005): one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe, shown by clear expression or other affirmative steps, is liable for third-party infringement.

    Source: MGM Studios v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005). Pinpoint: 545 U.S. 913, 919 (syllabus Held; opinion at 936-937). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe copyright, as shown by clear expression or other"

  9. [M-0221] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): contributory liability requires that the provider intended its service be used for infringement, shown only by inducement or a service tailored to infringement.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Held: The provider of a service is contributorily liable for a user’s infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement,"

  10. [M-0223] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): intent can be shown by inducement or by a service tailored to infringement (not capable of substantial or commercially significant noninfringing uses).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "A service is tailored to infringement if it is “not capable of ‘substantial’ or ‘commercially significant’ noninfringing uses.”"

  11. [M-0252] VERIFIED. Analysis: after Cox v. Sony (U.S. 2026) a service provider is contributorily liable for a user's infringement only if it induced the infringement or the service is tailored to it; knowing that some users infringe is not enough. Vicarious liability is a separate theory that the Court did not decide (cert limited to contributory).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: analysis. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Held: The provider of a service is contributorily liable for a user’s infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement,"

  12. [M-0343] VERIFIED. The 512(c) safe harbor covers infringement by reason of storage at the direction of a user on the provider's system.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "for infringement of copyright by reason of the storage at the direction of a user of material that resides on a system or network controlled or operated by or for the service provider"

  13. [M-0345] VERIFIED. Absent actual knowledge, the provider must not be aware of facts or circumstances from which infringement is apparent (red flag knowledge).

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1)(A)(ii). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "is not aware of facts or circumstances from which infringing activity is apparent"

  14. [M-0347] VERIFIED. The provider must not receive a financial benefit directly attributable to infringement where it has the right and ability to control it.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1)(B). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "does not receive a financial benefit directly attributable to the infringing activity, in a case in which the service provider has the right and ability to control such activity"

  15. [M-0349] VERIFIED. The 512(c) safe harbor applies only if the provider has designated an agent, by making it available on its service including its website and by providing it to the Copyright Office.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "by making available through its service, including on its website in a location accessible to the public, and by providing to the Copyright Office"

  16. [M-0357] VERIFIED. Under 512(i)(1)(A) a provider must have adopted and reasonably implemented, and inform subscribers of, a policy providing for termination in appropriate circumstances of subscribers and account holders who are repeat infringers; the 512 limitations apply only if so.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(i)(1)(A). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "has adopted and reasonably implemented, and informs subscribers and account holders of the service provider’s system or network of, a policy that provides for the termination in appropriate circumstances of subscribers and account holders"
