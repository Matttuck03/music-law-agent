M01 Composition and master

Part 1 of 2: Bottom line, Rules, Gotchas. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: low.

Answers questions like: What are the two copyrights in a recorded song; Who usually owns the composition and who owns the master; Is the audio of a mix posted with video a sound recording; Does a sound recording copyright cover the song underneath; Which exclusive rights attach to a composition versus a master; Can one party license only part of a copyright

Parts: 1 2

Bottom line

  • In the US (federal), a recorded song carries two separate copyrights: the musical work (composition) and the sound recording (master) [M-0028, M-0029, M-0025].
  • Owning or licensing one never gives you the other. Different people usually control them, and any use in a video needs permission from each owner (analysis).
  • The statute defines "sound recordings" to exclude sounds accompanying an audiovisual work [M-0030, M-0337, M-0508]. That carve-out is why a track placed in a video needs direct sync and master use licences (analysis).
  • Nothing on this card says any song is cleared. Name the missing licence and who grants it.

Rules

Two works

  • 17 U.S.C. 102(a) lists musical works, including any accompanying words, as copyrightable [M-0028]. It lists sound recordings as their own category [M-0029, M-0105].
  • The Copyright Office says a sound recording is a separate work from the composition, and a sound recording copyright is not a substitute for copyright in the underlying composition (regulator guidance, Circular 56A) [M-0025].
  • The Office treats a sound recording as a derivative work of the underlying composition (regulator guidance, not statute) [M-0026]. Section 101 defines a derivative work as one based on preexisting works, such as a sound recording or musical arrangement [M-0035].
  • Authors of the composition are composers, lyricists and songwriters. Authors of the recording are performers, producers and sound engineers, as the circular chart lists them [M-0027].

Definitions that matter for video

  • A "sound recording" results from fixing a series of sounds, but not including the sounds accompanying a motion picture or other audiovisual work [M-0030, M-0337, M-0508].
  • "Phonorecords" are material objects fixing sounds, other than sounds accompanying an audiovisual work [M-0031, M-0338, M-0119].
  • "Copies" are material objects other than phonorecords in which a work is fixed [M-0033]. The statute does not say how that applies to a video file with music in it.
  • Reading of the definition (unverified): the audio track of a short video is not itself a sound recording, but a commercial track inside it keeps its own sound recording and composition copyrights [M-0032]. This is an inference, not statutory text.
  • The 114 and 115 statutory licences are not a route for music placed in a video. Sync (composition) and master use (recording) are direct licences (analysis) [M-0030].

Rights that attach

  • Reproduction (106(1)): both musical works and sound recordings carry it, subject to sections 107 to 122 [M-0108, M-0334].
  • Public performance of a musical work (106(4)) is an exclusive right of the composition owner [M-0106, M-0335, M-0183].
  • Sound recordings have a public performance right only by digital audio transmission (106(6)) [M-0107, M-0336].
  • Performance societies license public performance of nondramatic musical works for owners. That is the performance leg only [M-0341].
  • The performance right is separate from reproduction, distribution and sync (analysis) [M-0183].

Ownership and splitting rights

  • Copyright vests first in the author. Authors of a joint work are co-owners [M-0037]. A joint work is prepared by two or more authors intending their contributions to merge into inseparable or interdependent parts of a unitary whole [M-0036].
  • Ownership can be transferred in whole or in part by any conveyance or by operation of law [M-0038].
  • Any exclusive right, and any subdivision of it, can be transferred and owned separately [M-0039]. So one right (for example sync) can sit with a different party from another (for example performance).
  • A transfer of ownership includes an assignment or exclusive licence but not a nonexclusive licence [M-0034].
  • Work made for hire: an employee's work within the scope of employment, or a commissioned work in a listed category with a signed written agreement [M-0045, M-0046]. The employer or commissioning party is then the author and owner unless a signed writing says otherwise [M-0048].
  • The listed categories do not name sound recordings. Whether a master is made for hire turns on the employee prong or fitting a category such as compilation or part of an audiovisual work (analysis, unsettled) [M-0047]. Detail is in M13.

Gotchas

  • "I bought or streamed the song" gives a listener right, not a sync or master use licence (analysis).
  • A master copyright does not cover the song, and a composition licence does not cover the recording [M-0025].
  • Section 101 excludes video audio from "sound recording", but that does not strip the embedded song of protection (unverified) inference [M-0032]. Do not read the carve-out as "music in videos is free".
  • Statutory 114 and 115 licences are not a shortcut for videos [M-0030].
  • The M-0047 "unsettled" point is analysis. Source support beyond section 101 was not checked on the ledger.
  • Circular 56A is regulator guidance, not statute [M-0025, M-0026]. The author chart flattens in the saved text, so lists are as given in the circular [M-0027].
  • Statute text is as saved 2026-11-10. Check for amendment before relying on it.
