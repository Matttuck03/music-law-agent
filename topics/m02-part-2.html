M02 Exclusive rights and the 114 limits

Part 2 of 2: Rules (continued), Gotchas, Open questions for counsel, Sources. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: low.

Parts: 1 2

Rules (continued)

  • Receipts: 50 percent to the recording owner [M-0302], 45 percent to featured artists [M-0303], distributed by a nonprofit collective the Judges designate [M-0304].

The 112 ephemeral copy limit

  • 112(e)(1) gives a statutory licence for one ephemeral phonorecord, to a transmitting organization entitled to transmit under 114(d)(1)(C)(iv) or a 114(f) statutory licence, if the listed conditions are met [M-0116].
  • The 112(a) ephemeral privilege excludes motion pictures and other audiovisual works [M-0117].
  • (analysis) A platform cannot rely on 112 for server copies of video with music [M-0181].

The 115 mechanical licence

  • Section 115 makes the 106(1) and 106(3) rights to make and distribute phonorecords of nondramatic musical works subject to compulsory licensing [M-0339].
  • It is limited to phonorecords whose primary purpose is distribution to the public for private use [M-0120].
  • A digital phonorecord delivery does not include digital transmission of sounds accompanying a motion picture or other audiovisual work [M-0340]. The 1976 House Report says the same of sound tracks (legislative history) [M-0121].
  • (analysis) So 115 does not license putting a song into a user video. Sync stays a direct licence [M-0179, M-0342].
  • The Music Modernization Act blanket licence is issued through the MLC to digital music providers [M-0023, M-0122]. It covers digital phonorecord deliveries including interactive streams [M-0123, M-0124], all musical works available for compulsory licensing, except as provided in subparagraph (C) (voluntary licences and individual download authority) [M-0125], and necessary server and incidental copies [M-0126].
  • Blanket licences were available only from the licence availability date [M-0127]. The MLC administers them and collects and distributes royalties [M-0128, M-0129]. Significant nonblanket licensee thresholds: over 5,000 recordings on any day in a month, or $50,000 a month or $500,000 over 12 months [M-0136]. Detail is for M03.

Gotchas

  • "We pay SoundExchange or a PRO, so we are covered": the 114 licence excludes audiovisual transmission [M-0112], and PRO licences address composition performance, not sync (see M05) [M-0088]. Neither is sync or master use.
  • "The MLC licence covers songs in videos": it does not [M-0340, M-0179].
  • "Muting or editing avoids it": re-encoding is still copying the sounds [M-0108, M-0110]. (analysis) Editing or muting alone is not a licence; removing the audio before publishing is the lawful path.
  • "Sound recordings have no performance right": only in the general sense. A digital audio transmission right exists [M-0107].
  • Rates, the MLC and the Judges' proceedings change. Check M03, M04 and M06 for 2026 figures. Facts here are statute text as of 2026-11-10.
  • (unverified) M-0317 is an inference, not a verified rule.

Open questions for counsel

  • US: does a short video with a licensed library track still need a separate live-room performance licence?
  • US: if a platform hosts a creator's upload with a commercial song, is the creator or the platform the direct infringer, and does 512 protect it (see M08)?
  • US: does a platform's re-encoding of user audio create a reproduction exposure for the platform as opposed to the user?
  • US: is a sound-alike track safe if it copies a recognisable arrangement?
  • US: could a tiny live-room audio feed qualify as an interactive service under 114(j)(7)?
