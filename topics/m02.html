M02 Exclusive rights and the 114 limits

Part 1 of 2: Bottom line, Rules. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: low.

Answers questions like: Do I need a licence from the label if I already have the songwriter's; Does the statutory webcasting licence cover an app that streams user mixes; Can a sound-alike recording avoid the master owner; Does a PRO licence cover a platform copying and processing user audio; Does the MLC blanket licence cover songs in a user mix; Why does a sound recording have no general performance right

Parts: 1 2

Bottom line

  • US federal law: a song is two copyrights. The composition (words and music) and the sound recording (the recorded performance) are separate works with separate owners [M-0025, M-0028, M-0029].
  • The statutory licences in 112, 114 and 115 do not cover music placed in a video. A platform that hosts music in user videos needs direct licences: sync from the composition owner and master use from the recording owner [M-0112, M-0340, M-0179, M-0180].
  • Re-encoding is still copying the sounds [M-0108, M-0110]. (analysis) Blurring or muting is not a licence either. This card is not legal advice; see the counsel section.

Rules

Two works

  • A sound recording is a separate work from the composition. Owning one is not a substitute for the other (regulator guidance, Circular 56A) [M-0025].
  • The Copyright Office treats a sound recording as a derivative work of the composition (regulator guidance) [M-0026].
  • Authors of the composition are composers, lyricists and songwriters. Authors of the recording are performers, producers and sound engineers [M-0027].
  • 17 U.S.C. 102(a) lists musical works with any accompanying words [M-0028] and sound recordings [M-0029, M-0105] as separate categories.

Rights under 106

  • Both works carry the reproduction right (copies or phonorecords) [M-0108, M-0334], subject to sections 107 through 122 [M-0334].
  • The composition owner has an exclusive public performance right [M-0106, M-0335, M-0183].
  • A sound recording owner has only the 106(1), (2), (3) and (6) rights [M-0296]. The performance right is only by digital audio transmission [M-0107, M-0336]. There is no general public performance right under 106(4) [M-0109].

The 114(b) limit and sound-alikes

  • The recording's reproduction right covers only duplication that recaptures the actual sounds [M-0110].
  • An independent sound-alike fixation does not infringe the recording copyright [M-0111]. The composition is a separate copyright, so (analysis) a sound-alike still needs a composition licence [M-0111].

The 114 statutory licence

  • 114(d)(2) covers subscription digital audio transmissions, eligible nonsubscription transmissions and preexisting satellite radio transmissions, if conditions are met [M-0114, M-0298].
  • The transmission must not be part of an interactive service [M-0115, M-0298]. An interactive service lets a member of the public receive a program made for them or, on request, a particular recording [M-0113, M-0299].
  • An eligible nonsubscription transmission is a noninteractive, nonsubscription digital audio transmission made as part of a service providing audio programming [M-0300].
  • A "digital audio transmission" does not include transmission of any audiovisual work [M-0112, M-0297]. So the 114(d)(2) licence does not extend to a video with music [M-0112, M-0180, M-0312].
  • Rates: the Judges use a willing buyer, willing seller standard [M-0167]. Rates and terms last 5 years unless otherwise agreed and bind affected owners [M-0168]. Specific rates are for M04 and M06.
  • An interactive service may not publicly perform a recording unless a licence exists for each musical work in it [M-0301]. (analysis) Even an interactive service would still need a composition performance licence, from a PRO or the owner [M-0313].
