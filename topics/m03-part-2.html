Rules (continued)

  • 2026 only: the larger of 13.1 cents per work or 2.52 cents per minute of playing time or fraction, for physical phonorecords and permanent downloads [M-0142]. The cost of living notice sets this effective December 1, 2025, applicable January 1 to December 31, 2026 [M-0143], using CPI-U 324.800 against a November 2022 base of 298.012 [M-0144].
  • Ringtones: a flat 24 cents per work [M-0145].
  • Streaming and limited downloads: the all-in royalty is the greater of a percent of service provider revenue and the TCC prong [M-0147]. The percent runs 15.1 (2023) to 15.35 (2027) [M-0146].
  • Phonorecords IV applies from January 1, 2023 through December 31, 2027 [M-0148]. A March 2024 correction fixed capitalization and a late-fee term [M-0149].
  • Phonorecords V, for 2028 to 2032, began on December 31, 2025 [M-0150]. Petitions to participate were due January 30, 2026 [M-0151].
  • Proposed only: on July 10, 2026 the Judges published a proposed partial settlement that would continue the physical, permanent download, ringtone and bundle rates with only continuing inflation adjustments [M-0152, M-0153]. Comments were due August 10, 2026. Final adoption is unconfirmed as of 2026-11-10. It is not law. The notice does not settle streaming (inference per the row note) [M-0153].

Gotchas

  • "The MLC covers me" is wrong for video. The blanket licence is a 115 licence for phonorecords and digital deliveries, not sync [M-0122, M-0179].
  • The 13.1 cent figure is 2026 only and adjusts annually [M-0142, M-0143]. Check the Federal Register for 2027 before quoting a rate.
  • Phonorecords V is a proposal as of the July 10, 2026 notice. Do not say the 2028 to 2032 rates are set [M-0152].
  • Rates cover composition royalties only, not the master recording.
  • The statutory percent table ends with royalty year 2027 [M-0146, M-0148].
  • MLC website statements (start date, FAQ answers) are MLC's own words, captured 2026-11-10, and may change [M-0133, M-0135].
  • Open source gaps: Phonorecords V final rule is not in the ledger. Do not guess it.

Open questions for counsel

  • US: Does any platform feature, such as a standalone audio or playlist function, make the platform a digital music provider that must file a notice of licence with the MLC?
  • US: For user video with licensed library music, what warranties and indemnities should a platform require from the library and from creators?
  • US: Does a platform's private re-encode of a user's own audio need any licence, or only the user's rights?
  • US: Should a platform watch Phonorecords V for any term change affecting streaming rates after 2027?

  14. [M-0179] VERIFIED. Analysis: a 115 compulsory licence, including the MLC blanket licence under 115(d), is limited to phonorecords made mainly for distribution to the public for private use, and 101 excludes sounds accompanying audiovisual works from "phonorecords", so 115 does not license putting a song into a user video; sync stays a direct licence.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 115 compulsory license for making and distributing phonorecords. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 115(a)(1)(A). Jurisdiction: analysis. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "A person may obtain a compulsory license only if the primary purpose in making phonorecords of the musical work is to distribute them to the public for private use"
