M03 Mechanicals, 115, the MLC, MMA

Part 1 of 2: Bottom line, Rules. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: high.

Answers questions like: Who issues the blanket mechanical licence and since when; Does a mixing platform need a mechanical licence to store or stream songs; What is the current mechanical rate; Does the MLC blanket licence cover songs in a mix posted with video; Is the Phonorecords V settlement final

Parts: 1 2

Bottom line

  • US federal law. The 115 mechanical licence covers the composition only, for phonorecords made mainly for private-use distribution, and it does not reach music placed in a video [M-0120, M-0340, M-0342]. Sync (composition) and master use (recording) need direct licences from the rights holders (analysis) [M-0179].
  • The MLC issues the MMA blanket licence to digital music providers for covered activities [M-0023, M-0128]. The Copyright Office continued the MLC designation on June 3, 2026 [M-0131].
  • The 2026 rate for physical phonorecords and permanent downloads is 13.1 cents per work or 2.52 cents per minute, whichever is larger, and it is a 2026 rate only [M-0142, M-0143].
  • Not legal advice. A licensed music attorney must confirm any claim that a platform needs no licence.

Rules

What 115 covers (17 U.S.C.)

  • 115 makes the 106(1) and 106(3) rights to make and distribute phonorecords of nondramatic musical works subject to compulsory licensing [M-0339].
  • A person may obtain the compulsory licence only if the primary purpose is distributing phonorecords to the public for private use [M-0120].
  • The definition of a digital phonorecord delivery excludes digital transmission of sounds accompanying a motion picture or other audiovisual work [M-0340].
  • The 1976 House Report (legislative history, not binding) said the licence does not reach sound tracks accompanying audiovisual works. Clause numbering has since changed, so treat it as history only [M-0121].
  • Analysis: 115, including the 115(d) blanket licence, does not license putting a song into a user video. Sync stays a direct licence [M-0179, M-0342].
  • The Copyright Office said in 2015 (regulator report, not binding) that consumer audiovisual uses should stay in the free market, and it did not recommend a compulsory licence for them [M-0140]. The MMA did not add audiovisual uses [M-0120].
  • "Sync" is an industry term for reproducing a musical work synchronized to visual content [M-0141] (industry practice).

The MMA blanket licence and the MLC

  • A digital music provider can obtain a blanket mechanical licence through the MLC [M-0023, M-0122]. It covers digital phonorecord deliveries, meaning permanent downloads, limited downloads and interactive streams [M-0123, M-0124].
  • It covers all musical works available for compulsory licensing for covered activities, except as provided in subparagraph (C), which covers voluntary licences [M-0125].
  • It includes server, intermediate, archival and incidental reproductions reasonable and necessary for covered activities [M-0126].
  • The MLC administers blanket licences, including notices of licence and usage reports [M-0128]. It collects and distributes royalties from providers [M-0129].
  • A provider obtains the blanket licence by submitting a notice of licence to the MLC (regulation, 37 CFR 210.24) [M-0137].
  • Blanket licences were available only from the licence availability date [M-0127]. The MLC says it began administering them on January 1, 2021 (MLC website, volatile) [M-0133].
  • A provider may keep or enter voluntary licences with publishers instead of, or alongside, the blanket licence (MLC FAQ) [M-0135].
  • A significant nonblanket licensee is one operating under voluntary or individual download licences that offers more than 5,000 different sound recordings on any day in a month, or meets revenue thresholds ($50,000 a month; $500,000 over 12 months) [M-0136].
  • The Register designated the MLC and the Digital Licensee Coordinator effective July 8, 2019 [M-0130]. The Office opened a periodic review in 2024 [M-0132] and, in its first review, continued both designations effective June 3, 2026 [M-0007, M-0131].
  • The DLC coordinates communication among the providers affected by the MMA (MLC FAQ; the statute is the primary source) [M-0134].

Rates (Copyright Royalty Board, regulations)

  • Part 385 sets rates only where a 115 compulsory licence is obtained and the exclusive rights are implicated [M-0138]. Voluntary licence rates and terms apply in lieu of part 385 [M-0139].
