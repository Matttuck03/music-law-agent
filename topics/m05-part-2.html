M05 PROs and consent decrees

Part 2 of 3: Rules (continued). Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: medium.

Parts: 1 2 3

Rules (continued)

  • DOJ described a full-work licence as each PRO licensing the work so the user can perform it without infringement risk. It described a fractional licence as each PRO licensing only its own share, so the user needs the other co-owners' PROs too [M-0270, M-0271].
  • The S.D.N.Y. held in 2016 that the BMI decree neither bars fractional licensing nor requires full-work licensing [M-0272]. The Second Circuit affirmed on 2017-12-19 by summary order, which has no precedential effect [M-0273].
  • DOJ's 2021 statement says the Second Circuit disagreed with its view and that the BMI decree does not require full-work licences. Whether PROs should offer them is still debated [M-0274, M-0275].
  • Do not say a PRO must license full works. For BMI the decree does not require full-work licences [M-0275]. ASCAP's treatment is not decided by these rows.

Rates and the rate court

  • The Second Circuit held in Pandora v. ASCAP (2015) that the ASCAP decree unambiguously precludes partial withdrawals of public performance licensing rights [M-0281].
  • It upheld a 1.85 percent of revenue rate for Pandora and ASCAP. The rate period is 2011 to 2015 and it is not a current rate [M-0282].
  • The MMA partly repealed the bar on considering sound recording digital performance rates when setting musical work performance rates [M-0279].
  • The MMA requires DOJ to notify Congress before moving to terminate a decree [M-0280].

Who is not under a decree

  • The decrees regulate only ASCAP and BMI. Other PROs are free of the same constraints [M-0278].
  • SESAC has never been under an antitrust consent decree [M-0320, M-0333]. A commenter quoted by the Office says SESAC, GMR, Pro Music Rights and AllTrack are not governed by decrees. That is a commenter view, not an Office finding [M-0293].
  • The Office says the decrees are an important factor in new PRO formation, because other PROs are not under a decree [M-0292].
  • Interested parties disagree. ASCAP says music users must pay SESAC and GMR what their repertories are worth [M-0331]. RMLC and MPA say non-decree PROs can extract disproportionate royalties [M-0332]. These are party positions, not findings.

SESAC and the Meredith settlement (court decision, S.D.N.Y. 2015)

  • Television stations alleged SESAC's all-or-nothing blanket licence violated Sherman Act section 1 [M-0319]. SESAC agreed to pay $58.5 million into a settlement fund [M-0321].
  • SESAC agreed to offer class stations a blanket licence and alternatives, protection from infringement claims while negotiating, and binding arbitration of industry-wide terms [M-0323, M-0324, M-0325]. The restrictions run 2016 to 2035 and cover local commercial television stations in the class [M-0322, M-0326].
  • This is the television history, not a radio or digital arrangement [M-0333].

Copyright Office PRO inquiry (regulator)

  • The Office opened a notice of inquiry on PROs and the public performance right on 2025-02-10 at Congress's request [M-0283, M-0008]. Comments were due 2025-04-11 and replies 2025-05-27 [M-0284].
  • Topics included added cost from paying additional PROs and clarity for entities seeking PRO licences [M-0286, M-0287].

  1. [M-0270] VERIFIED. DOJ described a full-work licence as one where each PRO offers non-exclusive licences to the work entitling the user to perform it without risk of infringement liability (a framework the BMI decree was held not to require).

    Source: DOJ Antitrust Division, Statement on the Closing of the Review of the ASCAP and BMI Consent Decrees (August 4, 2016; full-work licensing). Pinpoint: DOJ statement Aug. 4, 2016, Sec. II. Jurisdiction: regulator. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Under a full-work license, each PRO would offer nonexclusive licenses to the work entitling the user to perform the work without risk of infringement liability"

  2. [M-0271] VERIFIED. DOJ described a fractional licence as one where each PRO licenses only its own interests and the licensee must obtain additional licences from the PROs representing other co-owners before performing the work.

    Source: DOJ Antitrust Division, Statement on the Closing of the Review of the ASCAP and BMI Consent Decrees (August 4, 2016; full-work licensing). Pinpoint: DOJ statement Aug. 4, 2016, Sec. II. Jurisdiction: regulator. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Under a fractional license, each PRO would offer a license only to the interests it holds in a work, and require that the licensee obtain additional licenses from the PROs representing other co-owners before performing the work"
