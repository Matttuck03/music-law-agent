M05 PROs and consent decrees

Part 3 of 3: Rules (continued), Gotchas, Open questions for counsel, Sources. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: medium.

Parts: 1 2 3

Rules (continued)

  • The Office delivered its letter to House Judiciary leaders on 2025-11-20 and received over 5,000 comments [M-0288, M-0289]. The letter summarises commenter views and says these are not Office policy recommendations [M-0290].
  • GMR and SESAC have joined BMI and ASCAP in committing to put repertoire data on Songview [M-0291].

SoundExchange is not a PRO

  • SoundExchange says it is the sole organization designated by the US government to administer the section 114 sound recording licence [M-0294]. It covers the master side digital performance right, not the composition. See M06.
  • It says it collects from services such as Pandora, SiriusXM and iHeartRadio [M-0295].

EU (foreign law)

  • Directive 2014/26/EU Art. 5(2): rightholders may authorise a collective management organisation of their choice for the rights and territories of their choice [M-0308].
  • Art. 16(1): organisations and users must negotiate licences in good faith and share necessary information [M-0309].
  • Art. 16(2): licensing terms must rest on objective and non-discriminatory criteria [M-0310]. See M19.

Gotchas

  • "We have a blanket licence" does not mean sync or master are cleared [M-0256, M-0311].
  • DOJ's full-work view is DOJ's view only. It was rejected for the BMI decree [M-0269, M-0272, M-0273].
  • The Second Circuit BMI ruling is a summary order with no precedential effect [M-0273].
  • The 1.85 percent Pandora rate is a 2011 to 2015 period rate, not a current rate [M-0282].
  • The ASCAP decree text is a saved 2016 filing edition and the BMI row covers Article XIV only. No termination or change was found as of 2026-11-10, but check docket changes.
  • The Office's 2025 letter is a summary of views, not a recommendation or a change in law [M-0290].
  • A PRO licence for the US does not cover other territories. AFJ2 through-to-the-audience licences need not cover performances outside the United States [M-0261].
  • Gap, needs source: RMLC v. GMR and SESAC settlement terms are not in the ledger. Do not describe them.

Open questions for counsel

  • US federal: would a livestream with a performer's background music need ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and GMR licences, and would a platform blanket licence cover creators' streams?
  • US federal: does the through-to-the-audience licence fit a platform, and how should the request be made under AFJ2 and the BMI decree?
  • US federal: for co-owned songs, how should a platform handle fractional licensing across four PROs?
  • US federal: is the 2025 Copyright Office PRO inquiry likely to produce legislation that changes decree rights?
  • EU: would an EU rollout negotiate with collective management organisations under Directive 2014/26/EU, and which territories?
