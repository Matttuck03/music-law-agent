M05 PROs and consent decrees

Part 1 of 3: Bottom line, Rules. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: medium.

Answers questions like: Who are the US performing rights organizations and what do they license; Do the ASCAP and BMI consent decrees give a mixing platform a right to a licence and a rate court; Can a PRO license sync, master or remix rights for a mix; Does a PRO licence cover every co-owner of a song; What if SESAC or GMR will not license; What is different in the EU

Parts: 1 2 3

Bottom line

  • US federal: a PRO collectively licenses public performance rights in musical works [M-0328, M-0341]. ASCAP's decree bars it from licensing sync (the composition placed in a video) or any right other than public performance [M-0256]. Master use (the recording) is not a composition right at all (analysis). Whether BMI, SESAC and GMR have the same limit is inference (analysis) [M-0311].
  • ASCAP's decree gives a music user a right to a licence on written request and a rate court [M-0259, M-0263]. For BMI the rows show a rate court with the fee burden on BMI [M-0266, M-0268]; no BMI row here shows the right to a licence. SESAC is not under a decree [M-0320]. GMR is not under the decrees on the DOJ statement and a commenter's view, not an Office finding [M-0278, M-0293].
  • A PRO licence is not a clearance for a video. The platform or the creator still needs direct sync and master licences (analysis) [M-0311].
  • Decree law is volatile. The decree texts here are saved editions, and the Copyright Office ran an inquiry in 2025 and delivered a letter on 2025-11-20 with no policy recommendations [M-0283, M-0288, M-0290].

Rules

What a PRO is (US federal)

  • A PRO contracts with songwriters and publishers for authority to license public performance rights, then offers collective licences of those rights to users [M-0328].
  • The Copyright Office lists ASCAP (1914), SESAC (1931) and BMI (1939) as the established PROs. It says three more formed later: GMR (2013), PRO Music Rights (2018) and AllTrack (2019) [M-0285, M-0329].
  • DOJ described GMR in 2016 as a fourth PRO with a collection considerably smaller than ASCAP or BMI [M-0330].

ASCAP decree (court decision, Second Amended Final Judgment, S.D.N.Y. 2001, saved 2016 filing edition)

  • ASCAP may license only public performance rights in compositions, and only on a nonexclusive basis [M-0256].
  • ASCAP members keep the right to license performance rights directly to music users. ASCAP may not interfere [M-0257].
  • A blanket licence is a nonexclusive licence to perform ASCAP music at a fee that does not vary with how much ASCAP music the user performs [M-0258].
  • ASCAP must grant a nonexclusive licence to all works in its repertory to any music user that asks in writing [M-0259].
  • A through-to-the-audience licence covers performances in content a licensee delivers to another music user with whom it has an economic relationship [M-0260].
  • ASCAP must issue that licence on request to broadcasters, on-line users and operators of new transmission technologies. It need not cover performances outside the United States [M-0261]. The fee must take into account the value of all performances made under it [M-0262].
  • If no fee is agreed within 60 days of the licence request, the music user may apply to the rate court for a reasonable fee [M-0263]. ASCAP carries the burden of proving its fee is reasonable, except that a per-segment licensee carries a tracking burden [M-0264].
  • While negotiation or a rate proceeding is pending, either side may ask the court for an interim fee [M-0265].
  • Status: no termination found in a 2026-11-10 read-only check of justice.gov [M-0256, M-0257].

BMI decree (court decision, Final Judgment as amended 1994, Article XIV only saved)

  • In a BMI rate proceeding BMI carries the burden of proving its requested fee is reasonable [M-0266].
  • Once the rate court fixes a fee, BMI must offer a comparable fee to similarly situated applicants who later ask [M-0267].
  • An applicant or BMI may ask the rate court for an interim fee [M-0268].
  • DOJ's 2021 statement says the decrees require ASCAP and BMI to offer the same terms to similarly situated users in the same industry, and to provide through-to-the-audience licences to certain users on request [M-0276, M-0277].

Full-work against fractional licensing

  • DOJ's 2016 statement closed its review without seeking to change the decrees. It took the view that they require full-work licences [M-0269]. That is DOJ's view only.
