M06 SoundExchange, 114 and 112

Part 1 of 3: Bottom line, Rules. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: high.

Answers questions like: Does the 114 statutory licence cover a platform that streams user mixes; Who is SoundExchange and what does it collect; Does the 112 ephemeral copy exemption cover platform server copies; What are the Web VI rates and do they apply to a mixing platform; Does a platform storing or processing user audio need a licence; Is there a performance right in sound recordings for terrestrial radio

Parts: 1 2 3

Bottom line

US federal law: the 17 U.S.C. 114 statutory licence is for audio only transmissions of the sound recording (the master). It does not cover a short video with music, because a "digital audio transmission" excludes any audiovisual work [M-0112, M-0297, M-0312]. The 112 ephemeral copy licence does not help either [M-0181]. SoundExchange says it is the designated collective for the 114 royalties, so it is not who clears music for a video platform [M-0294, M-0304]. A video platform needs direct sync (composition) and master use (recording) licences from the rights holders.

Rules

What 114 gives and withholds (US federal statute)

  • A sound recording owner has the 106(1), (2), (3) and (6) rights and no general public performance right under 106(4) [M-0109, M-0296].
  • The sound recording reproduction right covers only duplication that recaptures the actual sounds [M-0110].
  • An independent sound-alike does not infringe the sound recording copyright. The musical work is a separate copyright, so (analysis) a sound-alike still needs a composition licence [M-0111].
  • 114(d)(2) puts three kinds of audio transmission under a statutory licence if the listed conditions are met: subscription, eligible nonsubscription and preexisting satellite digital audio radio [M-0114, M-0298].
  • The transmission must not be part of an interactive service [M-0115, M-0298].
  • An interactive service lets a member of the public receive a program made for the recipient or, on request, a particular sound recording [M-0113, M-0299].
  • An eligible nonsubscription transmission is a noninteractive, nonsubscription digital audio transmission made as part of an audio programming service [M-0300].
  • A digital audio transmission embodies a sound recording and does not include transmission of any audiovisual work [M-0112, M-0297].
  • An interactive service may not publicly perform a sound recording unless the musical work performance is licensed [M-0301]. (analysis) That licence may come from a performing rights society or the copyright owner [M-0313].

Rates and who sets them (US federal statute and regulation)

  • The Copyright Royalty Judges (three, appointed by the Librarian) set 112(e) and 114 rates [M-0169, M-0170].
  • The standard for 114(d)(2) rates is what a willing buyer and willing seller would agree in the marketplace [M-0167].
  • Rates run for 5 years unless another period is agreed, and bind all affected owners and performers [M-0168].
  • The Judges may adopt a settlement among some or all participants, and decline for non-parties only on an objection and a record showing no reasonable basis [M-0171].
  • Web V (37 CFR 380) set webcasting rates for 2021 to 2025 [M-0157, M-0158, M-0306]. That period ended 2025-12-31 [M-0157].
  • Web V commercial webcaster rates for 2025: $0.0032 per performance (subscription) and $0.0025 (nonsubscription) [M-0154, M-0307]. Minimum fee $1,000 per channel or station per year [M-0155]. Credit split 5 percent to ephemeral recordings and 95 percent to section 114 [M-0156].
  • Web VI began by Federal Register notice on 2024-01-05 [M-0184]. Final rules for commercial broadcasters, Educational Media Foundation, noncommercial educational webcasters and certain public radio stations took effect 2026-03-10 for 2026 to 2030 [M-0010].
