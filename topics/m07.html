M07 Sync and master use licensing for video

Part 1 of 3: Bottom line, Rules. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: high.

Answers questions like: Does the MLC blanket licence or any statutory licence cover songs in a mix posted with video; What licences does a user need to put a commercial song in a video mix; Does a TikTok or Meta sound licence carry over to another platform; Does a platform processing a user's audio need a music licence

Parts: 1 2 3

Bottom line

  • US federal: no statutory licence covers a song placed in a video. Sections 114 and 115 stop at audio, so a video with music needs two direct licences: sync from the composition owner (publisher) and master use from the recording owner (label or distributor) [M-0342, M-0112, M-0179].
  • The MLC blanket licence is a mechanical licence for audio deliveries. It does not reach sounds accompanying an audiovisual work [M-0122, M-0340].
  • Platform sound collections are often tied to one platform. Do not assume any of them covers another platform [M-0386].
  • Nothing here says any song is cleared. Name the missing licence and who grants it.

Rules

The two copyrights (US federal)

  • A recorded song carries two copyrights: the musical work (composition, usually publisher or PRO side) and the sound recording (master, usually label or distributor) (analysis).
  • Both carry the reproduction right [M-0108, M-0334]. Musical works also carry public performance [M-0106, M-0335]. Sound recordings carry public performance only by digital audio transmission [M-0107, M-0336].
  • A sound recording has no general public performance right under 106(4) [M-0109, M-0296]. Its reproduction right covers only duplication that recaptures the actual sounds [M-0110].
  • A sound alike made as an independent fixation does not infringe the master. The composition is a separate copyright, so (analysis) a sound alike still needs a composition licence [M-0111].

Why 115 does not cover video (statute)

  • Section 115 makes the 106(1) and 106(3) rights to make and distribute phonorecords of nondramatic musical works subject to compulsory licensing [M-0339].
  • The compulsory licence is limited to phonorecords whose primary purpose is distribution to the public for private use [M-0120]. Section 101 excludes sounds accompanying an audiovisual work from "phonorecords", so (analysis) 115 does not license music in video [M-0179].
  • A digital phonorecord delivery does not include the digital transmission of sounds accompanying a motion picture or other audiovisual work [M-0340, M-0124]. Sync and master use licences are therefore needed (analysis) [M-0342].
  • 1976 House Report (legislative history, not binding): the compulsory licence does not reach sound tracks accompanying a motion picture or other audiovisual work [M-0121].
  • The MLC administers blanket licences, notices of licence and usage reports, and collects and distributes royalties [M-0128, M-0129]. The blanket covers digital phonorecord deliveries of musical works for covered activities [M-0122, M-0123, M-0125]. It includes incidental reproductions reasonable and necessary for those activities [M-0126]. That is audio delivery, not video.
  • The Copyright Office (2015 report, regulator view, not binding) said consumer audiovisual (sync) uses should stay in the free market, with no compulsory licence [M-0140]. The 2018 MMA did not add audiovisual [M-0140, M-0120]. Sync is an industry term for reproduction (and possibly derivative) rights in a musical work synchronized to visual content [M-0141].

Why 114 does not cover video (statute)

  • A "digital audio transmission" does not include the transmission of any audiovisual work [M-0112, M-0297]. The 114(d)(2) licence therefore does not extend to a video with music [M-0112].
