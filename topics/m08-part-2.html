M08 DMCA 512 safe harbor for a platform that hosts user mixes

Part 2 of 2: Rules (continued), Gotchas, Open questions for counsel, Sources. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: medium.

Parts: 1 2

Rules (continued)

  • Lenz v. Universal (9th Cir. 2016, amended): a rightsholder must consider fair use before sending a takedown or risk 512(f) damages [M-0360]. The test is subjective good faith [M-0361].
  • Fourth Circuit reading of "repeat infringer" as repeat alleged infringer is described in the Copyright Office report [M-0376]. Cox v. Sony (U.S. 2026) addressed contributory liability, not the 512(i) reading (row note on M-0376). Contributory liability now needs inducement or a service tailored to infringement [M-0001, M-0252]. This sits outside the safe harbor and is covered in card M09.

Policy view (regulator report, not law)

  • The Copyright Office 2020 report recommends no wholesale change to 512, only fine tuning [M-0373]. It says courts' readings of the knowledge requirements may be narrower than Congress intended [M-0374]. It says willful blindness needs deliberate avoidance of specific infringements [M-0375]. It says notice and staydown needs more study [M-0377].

Gotchas

  • 512 is not a licence. A takedown or a counter notice does not clear a song, and winning on safe harbor does not mean the music was licensed.
  • "The notice was sloppy" is not a reason to ignore it. Missing signature or authority makes a notice defective, but do not just ignore it. Take it to counsel; needs source for 512(c)(3)(B) [M-0350, M-0351].
  • Sending takedowns carelessly is risky for the sender too: knowing misrepresentation carries damages, and Lenz requires fair use consideration [M-0359, M-0360]. Takedown notices a platform sends itself need counsel review.
  • Agent designations lapse after three years. A lapsed designation can cost the safe harbor [M-0349, M-0354].
  • The safe harbor is not conditioned on monitoring [M-0358], but knowledge, red flags and willful blindness can still defeat it [M-0345, M-0364].
  • Informal repeat infringer handling surviving in Ventura is not a safe rule [M-0372]. The Copyright Office noted a Fourth Circuit reading of repeat alleged infringer [M-0376].
  • Blurring, muting or re-encoding audio is not a licence and does not erase prior infringement.
  • Currency (as of 2026-11-10): the 37 CFR 201.38 text is a saved eCFR copy dated 2026-10-26 [M-0352]. The Copyright Office report is a report, not law [M-0373]. Cox v. Sony was decided 2026-03-25 and is limited to contributory liability [M-0001, M-0252]. Vicarious liability was not decided [M-0252].
  • Contested gloss: the "right and ability to control" case law is not fully covered by the ledger [M-0347].

Open questions for counsel

  • US federal: does 512(c) protect a platform's live streams where music is streamed in real time, or only stored uploads?
  • US federal: does a separate re-encoding service's processing of user audio, on device or on a server, create its own reproduction outside 512(c), and does that service need its own agent designation?
  • Ninth Circuit: given Mavrix, how should a platform structure moderation and featuring so staff acts are not treated as the site's own?
  • US federal: what repeat infringer policy text, strike count and reinstatement rules should a platform adopt, given the Fourth Circuit reading of "repeat" [M-0376]?
  • US federal: after Cox v. Sony, what does a platform's exposure look like if a song is flagged and it keeps promoting the clip?
  • Needs source: Grande remand outcome and any 2026 512 decisions are not in the ledger. No row addresses live streaming under 512 or fingerprint filtering as a standard technical measure.

  2. [M-0361] VERIFIED. Lenz: the test is subjective good faith belief; courts do not second-guess a genuine belief that use is not fair.

    Source: Lenz v. Universal Music Corp., 815 F.3d 1145 (9th Cir. 2016) (amended opinion). Pinpoint: 815 F.3d 1145 (slip op. 19). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "If, however, a copyright holder forms a subjective good faith belief the allegedly infringing material does not constitute fair use, we are in no position to dispute the copyright holder’s belief"

  3. [M-0376] VERIFIED. The report notes the Fourth Circuit reads 'repeat infringer' as repeat alleged infringer, not repeat adjudicated.

    Source: U.S. Copyright Office, Section 512 of Title 17: A Report of the Register of Copyrights (May 2020). Pinpoint: Executive Summary, repeat infringer policies. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "a “repeat infringer” under section 512 means repeat alleged infringer, not repeat adjudicated"

  4. [M-0001] VERIFIED. The Supreme Court decided Cox Communications v. Sony Music (No. 24-171) on March 25, 2026, reversing and remanding; contributory liability needs intent shown by inducement or a service tailored to infringement.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: Syllabus, Held (decided March 25, 2026; judgment 93 F.4th 222 reversed and remanded). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "contributorily liable for a user's infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement, which can be shown only if the party induced the infringement or the provided service is tailored to that infringement"

  5. [M-0252] VERIFIED. Analysis: after Cox v. Sony (U.S. 2026) a service provider is contributorily liable for a user's infringement only if it induced the infringement or the service is tailored to it; knowing that some users infringe is not enough. Vicarious liability is a separate theory that the Court did not decide (cert limited to contributory).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: analysis. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Held: The provider of a service is contributorily liable for a user’s infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement,"
