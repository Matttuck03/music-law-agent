M09 Secondary liability after Cox

Part 2 of 2: Rules (continued), Gotchas, Open questions for counsel, Sources. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: high.

Parts: 1 2

Rules (continued)

  • The Copyright Office 2020 report recommends no wholesale change to section 512 [M-0373]. It says courts may read knowledge requirements more narrowly than Congress intended [M-0374], and that willful blindness needs deliberate avoidance of specific infringements [M-0375]. Notice and staydown needs more study [M-0377].

Gotchas

  • "Knowledge is not enough" is not "we are safe." Inducement, tailored service, direct liability, vicarious liability and the 512 conditions all remain [M-0222, M-0252].
  • Lower court cases that rely on "knew or should have known" or knowledge plus failure to act are overtaken [M-0224, M-0232]. Re-check any other circuit's secondary liability case before citing.
  • The BMG repeat infringer holding is a separate 512(i) point and is not disturbed [M-0230]. Do not drop the repeat infringer policy because of Cox.
  • Grande: only the April 6, 2026 vacatur is in our sources [M-0227]. The remand result is unknown. Needs source.
  • Sotomayor's aiding and abetting remark is a concurrence [M-0225]. Plaintiffs may plead it, so do not call it dead.
  • Cox is an ISP case. Whether courts apply it identically to hosting platforms is not settled in our sources (analysis).
  • Currency: as of 2026-11-10, post-Cox lower court case law is not in the ledger. Volatility is high.

Open questions for counsel

  • US federal: given our sound features, does any product copy or marketing risk "inducement" under Cox?
  • US federal: with Cox silent on vicarious liability, what is a platform's exposure from ad or subscription revenue on videos with unlicensed music?
  • US federal: does a platform's server side re-encoding of user audio make it a direct actor, and does 512(c) or 512(a) fit?
  • Ninth and Second Circuits: how have courts applied Cox to hosting platforms since March 2026, and what happened on the Grande remand in the Fifth Circuit?
  • Does our repeat infringer policy meet BMG v. Cox, and how should it treat creators with multiple unproven notices?
  • Should a platform's designated agent and notice workflow be reviewed against current Copyright Office filings?

  2. [M-0374] VERIFIED. The report finds courts' readings of knowledge requirements may be narrower than Congress intended (actual vs red flag, willful blindness).

    Source: U.S. Copyright Office, Section 512 of Title 17: A Report of the Register of Copyrights (May 2020). Pinpoint: Executive Summary, knowledge requirements. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "current interpretations of the section 512 knowledge requirements for OSPs may be narrower than Congress initially intended"

  3. [M-0375] VERIFIED. The report says courts require willful blindness to involve deliberate avoidance of specific infringements, not infringement generally.

    Source: U.S. Copyright Office, Section 512 of Title 17: A Report of the Register of Copyrights (May 2020). Pinpoint: Executive Summary, willful blindness. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "requires that the willful blindness involve deliberate avoidance of specific incidences of infringement, rather than avoidance of acts of infringement generally"

  4. [M-0377] VERIFIED. The Office's 2020 report says notice-and-staydown (and website blocking) need additional study, including non-copyright implications and public input, before any proposal.

    Source: U.S. Copyright Office, Section 512 of Title 17: A Report of the Register of Copyrights (May 2020). Pinpoint: Executive Summary (additional study conclusion). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "the Office believes that additional study, including of potential non-copyright implications, with public input, would be needed in order to explore the possible contours of any such future proposals."

  5. [M-0222] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient to establish the required intent.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), Part II.B. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient to establish the required intent to infringe."

  6. [M-0252] VERIFIED. Analysis: after Cox v. Sony (U.S. 2026) a service provider is contributorily liable for a user's infringement only if it induced the infringement or the service is tailored to it; knowing that some users infringe is not enough. Vicarious liability is a separate theory that the Court did not decide (cert limited to contributory).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: analysis. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Held: The provider of a service is contributorily liable for a user’s infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement,"

  7. [M-0224] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): the Fourth Circuit's knowledge-plus-insufficient-action rule went beyond the two recognised bases and conflicted with the Court's precedent.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), Part III. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "contributory liability cannot rest only on a provider’s knowledge of infringement and insufficient action to prevent it."

  8. [M-0232] VERIFIED. BMG v. Cox held the 'knew or should have known' contributory standard is negligence and too low.

    Source: BMG Rights Mgmt v. Cox Communications, 881 F.3d 293 (4th Cir. 2018). Pinpoint: 881 F.3d at 310 (Part III.B). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "The formulation “should have known” reflects negligence and is therefore too low a standard."

  9. [M-0230] VERIFIED. BMG v. Cox (4th Cir. 2018): to use the section 512 safe harbour a provider must have adopted and reasonably implemented a policy to terminate repeat infringers.

    Source: BMG Rights Mgmt v. Cox Communications, 881 F.3d 293 (4th Cir. 2018). Pinpoint: 881 F.3d at 301 (Part II.A). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "adopted and reasonably implemented ... a policy that provides for the termination in appropriate circumstances of subscribers ... who are repeat infringers.” 17 U.S.C."

    Source: Supreme Court docket No. 24-967, Grande Communications Networks, LLC v. UMG Recordings, Inc. (GVR in light of Cox, Apr. 6, 2026). Pinpoint: No. 24-967 docket entry Apr 06 2026. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.
