M10 Content ID systems and platform sound libraries

Part 1 of 2: Bottom line, Rules. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: high.

Answers questions like: Can a mixing platform use TikTok or Meta library music; Does a library licence from Epidemic Sound or Musicbed cover mixes on a platform; Does a mixing platform need to build Content ID style filtering; What must a platform do when a rightsholder sends a takedown; What would EU Article 17 require of a platform that hosts user mixes; Is YouTube Audio Library music safe to use in mixes

Parts: 1 2

Bottom line

  • No saved platform or library licence is shown to cover music on a third party platform. TikTok's Commercial Music Library grants no rights outside TikTok [M-0382, M-0383, M-0386], and Meta Sound Collection is limited to Meta products (2023 archive copy) [M-0385].
  • A Content ID style filter is a product choice in the US, not a legal duty: the DMCA safe harbor is not conditioned on monitoring [M-0358]. In the EU, Article 17 can require upload filtering style duties (EU law).
  • These tools and libraries touch the composition and the master only by licence or claim. A library licence is a direct sync and master licence from the library, and only if its terms fit the use (analysis).

Rules

Content ID style systems (platform terms)

  • YouTube Content ID scans each upload against files that owners submit. A match leads to block, monetize or track [M-0378]. It is open only to owners with exclusive rights to a substantial body of frequently uploaded original material [M-0379]. Edition captured 2026-11-10.
  • YouTube Audio Library music is described as copyright safe and not claimed through Content ID [M-0411]. The page states no licence scope outside YouTube [M-0411]. Some tracks need credit under a Creative Commons licence [M-0412]. YouTube disclaims responsibility for music from other libraries [M-0413].

US federal: DMCA 512(c) (statute)

  • The 512(c) safe harbor covers infringement by reason of storage at a user's direction [M-0343].
  • Conditions: no actual knowledge [M-0344]; no awareness of facts making infringement apparent, called red flag knowledge [M-0345]; act expeditiously to remove once aware [M-0346]; no direct financial benefit from infringement where the provider can control it [M-0347]; act expeditiously on a compliant notice [M-0348].
  • The provider must designate an agent on its service and with the Copyright Office [M-0349].
  • A repeat infringer policy must be adopted, reasonably implemented and told to subscribers, or the limitations do not apply [M-0357].
  • The safe harbor is not conditioned on monitoring or seeking out infringement, except where a standard technical measure applies [M-0358].
  • A valid notice needs an authorised signature [M-0350] and a statement under penalty of perjury that the sender is authorised to act for the owner [M-0351].
  • Counter notice: the uploader states under penalty of perjury a good faith belief of mistake or misidentification [M-0356]. The provider restores the material not less than 10 nor more than 14 business days after receipt, unless the notifier first reports a court action [M-0355].
  • Knowing material misrepresentation in a notice or counter notice makes the sender liable for damages and fees [M-0359]. In the Ninth Circuit a rightsholder must consider fair use before sending a takedown [M-0360]. The test is subjective good faith [M-0361] (Lenz v. Universal, court decision).
  • The Copyright Office 2020 report (a report, not law) recommends only fine tuning of 512 [M-0373]. It says courts may read knowledge requirements more narrowly than Congress meant [M-0374], that willful blindness needs deliberate avoidance of specific infringements [M-0375], and that the Fourth Circuit reads repeat infringer as repeat alleged infringer [M-0376]. It says notice and staydown needs further study [M-0377].

EU: Directive 2019/790 Article 17 (foreign law)

  • A content sharing service provider communicates to the public when it gives access to user uploads, and needs authorisation [M-0387]. The Article 14(1) hosting safe harbor does not apply to these situations [M-0388].
