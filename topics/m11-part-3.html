M11 Sampling and interpolation

Part 3 of 3: Gotchas, Open questions for counsel, Sources. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: medium.

Parts: 1 2 3

Gotchas

  • "Sampling is fair use" is wrong as a rule. Estate of Smith found fair use on its own facts, in a district court, and we have no saved row on any later Second Circuit decision (needs source) [M-0212].
  • "Licensing the recording clears the song" is wrong. The case behind it was sued on the composition [M-0193].
  • The Sixth versus Ninth Circuit split is as of the saved opinions. Later Supreme Court review of the split is not in the saved set (as of 2026-11-10).
  • Sound-alike safety is only for the master, only for wholly independent fixation [M-0111].
  • Grand Upright and Estate of Smith are district court decisions. Grand Upright was a preliminary injunction, not a final judgment [M-0187].
  • Pre-1978 works: composition rights follow the deposited sheet music in the Second Circuit [M-0201].
  • Pending bills and AI sound-alike issues are in M15 to M17, not here.

Open questions for counsel

  • US federal, Second, Sixth, Ninth Circuits: which de minimis rule would a court apply to an unlicensed sample in a video hosted by a platform, and where could a plaintiff sue?
  • US federal: if a platform is a host under 512 (see M08), does a sample claim change its safe harbour position?
  • US federal: what clearance wording should a platform require when a creator says a sample is "cleared"?
  • US federal: does a platform's server-side re-encoding and storage of a user's audio create reproduction, performance or host-style exposure for the platform, especially without terms of use or a DMCA contact?
  • US federal: what is the current status of Estate of Smith on appeal (a 2d Cir. 2020 summary order is mentioned in some reports but we have no row for it), and does it support a fair use position on short samples?

  3. [M-0111] VERIFIED. Sound-alike recordings that are an independent fixation do not infringe the sound recording copyright (114(b) imitation rule). The musical work remains a separate copyright, so (analysis) a sound-alike still needs a musical work licence.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "do not extend to the making or duplication of another sound recording that consists entirely of an independent fixation of other sounds, even though such sounds imitate or simulate those in the copyrighted sound recording."

  4. [M-0187] VERIFIED. Grand Upright (S.D.N.Y. 1991) granted a preliminary injunction against unlicensed sampling, condemned it in strong terms ("Thou shalt not steal") and referred the defendants to the U.S. Attorney for consideration of prosecution under 17 U.S.C. 506(a) and 18 U.S.C. 2319.

    Source: Grand Upright Music Ltd. v. Warner Bros. Records, Inc., 780 F. Supp. 182 (S.D.N.Y. 1991) (Caselaw Access Project mirror). Pinpoint: 780 F. Supp. at 185. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

  5. [M-0201] VERIFIED. Sheeran (2d Cir. 2024): for a pre-1978 work, the Copyright Act of 1909 protects only the musical composition as defined by the sheet music deposited with the Copyright Office.

    Source: Structured Asset Sales, LLC v. Sheeran, 120 F.4th 1066 (2d Cir. Nov. 1, 2024) (Thinking Out Loud). Pinpoint: 120 F.4th 1066, Part I (intro). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.
