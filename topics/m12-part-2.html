M12 Infringement, fair use, registration, damages, limitations

Part 2 of 2: Rules (continued), Gotchas, Open questions for counsel, Sources. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: medium.

Parts: 1 2

Rules (continued)

  • Fourth Circuit, BMG v. Cox (2018): a provider needs a repeat infringer policy adopted and reasonably implemented to use the section 512 safe harbour [M-0230]. Cox lost it by not enforcing the policy meaningfully [M-0231, M-0253]. The provider bears the burden of proof [M-0233].
  • BMG's "knew or should have known" contributory standard was called negligence and too low [M-0232]. The Supreme Court in Cox (2026) later held contributory liability cannot rest only on knowledge plus insufficient action [M-0224].

Gotchas

  • No seconds rule, no "non commercial" rule, no "credit the artist" rule. The four factors decide [M-0203, M-0205].
  • Transformative is a matter of degree and is judged on the specific use; licensing or monetising weighs against it [M-0208, M-0209, M-0210].
  • Nealy assumed the discovery rule without deciding it [M-0024]. Do not say the Supreme Court adopted it.
  • Fifth and Ninth Circuit similarity rules differ in detail; name the circuit [M-0195, M-0194]. Batiste is a Fifth Circuit rule on access, not nationwide [M-0196].
  • Sheeran concerns a pre 1978 work and the 1909 Act only [M-0201].
  • Williams v. Gaye is mixed: verdict stood, vicarious liability reversed [M-0198].
  • BMG v. Cox on contributory liability is overtaken by Cox 2026 [M-0232, M-0224]. Its repeat infringer point on section 512(i) stands [M-0230].
  • Statutory damage figures are those in the saved text as of 2026-11-10 [M-0215].
  • State publicity claims and Section 230 differ by circuit: Third Circuit Hepp (2021) held 230 does not preclude them, while the Ninth Circuit reads the carve out as federal IP only [M-0485, M-0486]. Extension to voice clones is inference; see M16 and M17.
  • Currency: Grande remand outcome is a needs source gap; do not guess.

Open questions for counsel

  • US federal: for a monetised video with 20 seconds of a commercial track under a creator's own edit, what is the realistic fair use position under Campbell and Warhol?
  • US federal: which of a platform's catalogue risks are registered works, and does section 412 limit statutory damages for newly registered music?
  • Ninth Circuit: does accrual under section 507(b) follow the discovery rule for continuing uploads, given Nealy only assumed it?
  • US federal: does a live stream performance of a track create separate infringements per stream for damages purposes?
  • US federal: does a platform's re-encoding of user audio expose the platform itself to a direct or secondary claim?
