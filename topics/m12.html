M12 Infringement, fair use, registration, damages, limitations

Part 1 of 2: Bottom line, Rules. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: medium.

Answers questions like: Is a 15 second clip of a song in a monetised mix fair use; How much can a copyright owner recover from a platform or a user; Does an owner have to register before suing; How long after an upload can a music claim be brought; How does a plaintiff prove a song was copied; Does a mix that uses many songs multiply the damages

Bottom line

US federal law: using a song in a video without a licence can infringe whichever copyright is used, unless a defence such as fair use applies, the composition, the recording, or both [M-0220, M-0028, M-0029]. Fair use is a case by case defence with no safe number of seconds [M-0203, M-0251]. Statutory damages run $750 to $30,000 per work, up to $150,000 if willful, but only for works registered in time [M-0215, M-0217, M-0214]. The suit deadline is three years from accrual, and a timely claim can reach old infringements (discovery rule assumed) [M-0218, M-0219].

Rules

Infringement and proof (US federal unless stated)

  • Anyone who violates an exclusive right of the copyright owner is an infringer [M-0220].
  • Musical works, including accompanying words, are protected [M-0028]. Sound recordings are a separate category [M-0029, M-0105]. One song is therefore two works (analysis, M-0254).
  • Ninth Circuit en banc (court decision, 2020): the inverse ratio rule is rejected, so strong proof of access does not lower the similarity showing [M-0194].
  • Fifth Circuit (court decision, 2020): even with overwhelming proof of access, factual copying needs some probative similarity [M-0195]. Without proof of access, copying can be inferred only if the works are strikingly similar [M-0196]. Summary judgment for the defendants was affirmed for lack of evidence of copying [M-0197].
  • Ninth Circuit (court decision, 2018, Blurred Lines): the verdict stood because evidence of extrinsic and intrinsic similarity was not absent [M-0199]. The panel affirmed in part and reversed in part: damages and a running royalty were proper, while the vicarious liability finding and one party's verdict were reversed [M-0198].
  • Ninth Circuit (court decision, 2022, Dark Horse): commonplace musical elements, without an original combination, are not protected, so the verdict was set aside [M-0200].
  • Second Circuit (court decision, 2024, Thinking Out Loud): a pre 1978 work under the 1909 Act is protected only as to the composition as defined by the deposited sheet music [M-0201]. A selection and arrangement claim built on a four chord progression and syncopated harmonic rhythm failed as a matter of law [M-0202].

Fair use

  • Section 107 is a four factor, non exhaustive test and states no number of seconds [M-0203]. Factor four is the effect on the potential market for or value of the work [M-0204].
  • Campbell (Supreme Court, 1994): fair use is case by case and bright line rules do not fit [M-0205]. Factor one asks whether the new work merely supersedes the original or adds new expression, meaning or message [M-0206]. The more transformative the use, the less weight commercialism carries [M-0207].
  • Warhol (Supreme Court, 2023): factor one asks whether the use has a further purpose or different character, a matter of degree weighed against commercialism [M-0208]. The court looked at the specific use alleged to infringe, there the licensing, not the work in the abstract [M-0210]. Licensing the image to a magazine did not favour fair use [M-0209].
  • Analysis: fair use is decided on the facts, including commercial nature and amount used, so background music in a monetised video must be argued case by case [M-0251].

Registration, damages, limitations

  • Subject to exceptions in section 411, no civil action for infringement of a US work may start until preregistration or registration has been made [M-0213].
  • No statutory damages or fees for infringement of a published work that began before registration, unless registered within three months of publication [M-0214].
  • Statutory damages: $750 to $30,000 per work, at the owner's election [M-0215]. They are assessed per work for all infringements in the action and may be elected any time before final judgment [M-0216]. Willful infringement raises the maximum to $150,000 per work [M-0217].
  • Analysis: exposure is per work, not per view or per video, and a song can be two works, composition and recording [M-0254].
  • Civil limitations period: three years after the claim accrued [M-0218].
  • Nealy (Supreme Court, 2024): assuming without deciding that the discovery rule applies, a timely claim can recover damages for any infringement, with no separate three year damages bar [M-0024, M-0219].
