M13 Work for hire, transfers, termination

Part 2 of 3: Rules (continued). Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: medium.

Parts: 1 2 3

Rules (continued)

  • Section 101 excludes sounds accompanying a motion picture or other audiovisual work from "sound recordings" and "phonorecords" [M-0030, M-0031, M-0118, M-0119, M-0337, M-0338]. A music track embedded in a short video is still treated as keeping its own composition and recording copyrights, but that is an inference (unverified) [M-0032].
  • Performing rights societies license public performance of nondramatic musical works for owners [M-0341]. They are not the source of a sync or master grant.

Duration (US federal)

  • Works from 1 January 1978 on: life of the author plus 70 years, with exceptions for joint, anonymous, pseudonymous and made-for-hire works [M-0044].

Termination of grants (US federal)

  • Section 203 applies to grants by an author executed on or after 1 January 1978, other than works made for hire and other than by will [M-0055]. Window: five years starting 35 years after execution (or after publication, or 40 years after execution, whichever ends earlier, if the grant covers publication) [M-0056].
  • Notice is served two to ten years before the effective date [M-0057]. A copy must be recorded before the effective date or termination does not take effect [M-0058].
  • It works notwithstanding any agreement to the contrary [M-0059]. Unless terminated, a grant lasts for the term of copyright unless it says otherwise [M-0061].
  • Derivative works exception: a derivative work made under the grant before termination may continue to be used under the grant's terms, but no new derivative works may be made afterwards [M-0060].
  • A further grant of terminated rights is valid only if made after the effective date, except that the original grantee or its successor can agree a further grant after notice is served [M-0062].
  • Section 304(c) covers pre-1978 grants of the renewal term, other than works made for hire [M-0063]. Window: five years starting 56 years after copyright was first secured, or 1 January 1978, whichever is later [M-0064]. Notice is again two to ten years ahead, with a copy recorded [M-0065]. Same derivative works exception [M-0066].
  • Mills Music v. Snyder (U.S. Supreme Court 1985) (court decision): a publisher keeps its contractual share of royalties from pre-termination recordings under the 304(c)(6)(A) exception, because the recordings are derivative works used under the grant [M-0067, M-0068].
  • Copyright Office final rule, 89 FR 56586 (regulation, effective 8 August 2024): the exception does not apply to any blanket licence, so nobody is treated as owner or royalty payee of a work used under the blanket licence because of that exception [M-0071, M-0073]. It also does not apply to individual download licences, and not to voluntary licences unless the MLC is directed otherwise [M-0072]. The Office issued it because the MLC's termination dispute policy conflicted with Office guidance [M-0074].
