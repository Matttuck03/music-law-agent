M13 Work for hire, transfers, termination

Part 1 of 3: Bottom line, Rules. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: medium.

Answers questions like: Who owns a song or recording a creator made for a label or publisher; Does a user who mixes others' music own the mix; Can a songwriter or artist take back a music grant years later; Does a licence signed before termination survive it; Does a transfer need to be in writing; Who owns music made for the platform by a contractor

Parts: 1 2 3

Bottom line

US federal law: the first owner of a copyright is the author, unless the work is made for hire, in which case the employer or commissioning party is the author [M-0037, M-0048]. A transfer of ownership needs a signed writing [M-0040]. Authors (not work-for-hire employers) can usually terminate grants decades later, and no contract can waive that right [M-0055, M-0059]. For a platform this decides who can sign a sync licence (composition) or a master use licence (recording): you must trace title from the author through every transfer, and no ownership claim by a creator, label or publisher proves a song is cleared.

This is not legal advice. A licensed music attorney must confirm chain of title, work for hire clauses and termination notices before anyone relies on this card.

Rules

Who is the author (US federal, 17 U.S.C. 101 and 201)

  • Copyright vests initially in the author; authors of a joint work are co-owners [M-0037]. A joint work is made by two or more authors who intend their contributions to merge into a unitary whole [M-0036].
  • A work made for hire is either work by an employee within the scope of employment, or a commissioned work in one of nine listed categories with a signed written agreement [M-0045].
  • For commissioned works the categories include a contribution to a collective work, part of a motion picture or other audiovisual work, and a compilation, and the parties must expressly agree in a signed writing [M-0046].
  • The employer or commissioning party is the author and owns all rights unless the parties agree otherwise in a signed writing [M-0048].
  • The category list does not name sound recordings. Whether a recording is made for hire turns on the employee prong or on fitting a category such as compilation or part of an audiovisual work (analysis) [M-0047].
  • Courts apply the 1909 Act "instance and expense" test to older works when deciding work made for hire under Section 304(c): Marvel v. Kirby (2d Cir. 2013) (court decision) [M-0054]. The saved quote is PARTIAL (line numbers interleave), so rely on the claim, not a block quote.
  • Waite v. UMG (S.D.N.Y. 31 March 2020, motion to dismiss) (court decision): the court did not decide whether the recordings were made for hire, because the label raised it only on limitations. It found the label's authority for treating albums as compilations unpersuasive [M-0049]. It also noted that the industry frequently inserts work-for-hire language into recording contracts [M-0050]. The artists had asked for a declaration that recordings cannot be made for hire; that is a pleading, not a holding [M-0051].
  • Ray Charles Foundation v. Robinson (9th Cir. 2015) (court decision): the foundation could challenge whether works were made for hire, and so not terminable. It was a real party in interest and had standing. The merits were not decided [M-0069, M-0070].

Transfers (US federal, 17 U.S.C. 201, 204, 205)

  • Ownership can be transferred in whole or part by any means of conveyance or by operation of law [M-0038]. Each exclusive right, and any subdivision, can be owned separately [M-0039].
  • A transfer of ownership, other than by operation of law, is not valid unless in a writing signed by the owner or the owner's authorized agent [M-0040].
  • "Transfer of copyright ownership" covers an assignment or exclusive licence but not a nonexclusive licence [M-0034]. So a nonexclusive licence is not caught by the Section 204(a) writing rule as a transfer (analysis, narrow).
  • A signed transfer can be recorded in the Copyright Office, with a real signature or a sworn or official certification of a true copy [M-0041].
  • Between two conflicting transfers, the first executed wins if recorded within one month (two months abroad) or before the later one is recorded [M-0042]. The statute has a further rule for the later transfer (see 17 U.S.C. 205(d)); it is not covered by a ledger row here.
  • A nonexclusive licence, recorded or not, beats a conflicting transfer if it is in a signed written instrument and was taken before the transfer or in good faith before its recordation and without notice [M-0043].

Which copyright, which grant

  • A recording and the underlying composition are separate works with separate copyrights [M-0025]. The Office treats a recording as a derivative work of the composition [M-0026, M-0035]. Authors differ: composition by composers, lyricists and songwriters; recording by performers, producers and sound engineers as the circular lists them [M-0027].
