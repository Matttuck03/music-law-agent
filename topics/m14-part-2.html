M14 Deal terms

Part 2 of 3: Rules (continued). Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: medium.

Parts: 1 2 3

Rules (continued)

  • Copyright Office 2024 final rule (regulation, effective 8 August 2024): the derivative works exception does not apply to any blanket licence [M-0071], nor to any individual download licence, nor to any voluntary licence unless the MLC is directed otherwise [M-0072]. The Office acted because the MLC's termination dispute policy conflicted with Office guidance [M-0074]. Four listed sections applied immediately; MLC compliance with the rest waited for a later payee snapshot, and the Office may extend deadlines [M-0073].

How the deals are built (industry practice, company filings)

  • Artist deals: royalties on sales and other uses, advances recoupable from future royalties, recording costs typically recoupable [M-0075]. A typical new artist contract requires delivery of an EP or album and gives the label a series of options [M-0076]. Many deals are expanded rights (360) deals sharing touring, merchandising, sponsorship and other income [M-0077, M-0078].
  • Publishing: WMG's publisher owns or administers compositions and earns mechanical, performance, sync, digital and other income [M-0079]. Each sync licence is generally negotiated with the prospective licensee, and the publisher pays a contractual share of sync income to songwriters or heirs [M-0080]. Reservoir says the same, with co-publishers also sharing [M-0100]. WMG defines sync as use of the composition in combination with visual images [M-0081].
  • The Copyright Office said in 2015 that consumer audiovisual (sync) uses should stay in the free market, with no compulsory licence recommended [M-0140]. Sync is an industry term for reproduction (and possibly derivative) rights in a work synchronized to visual content [M-0141]. This is a regulator report, not binding.
  • Reservoir licenses sync to film, television, advertising and digital platforms from social media to fitness; sync was 13% of revenue for the year ended 31 March 2026 [M-0102]. Such social media sync deals are direct licences (analysis).
  • Reservoir licenses digital music services itself or through a distributor, with fees set licence by licence [M-0104]. It treats its songwriters and artists as independent contractors and flags reclassification risk [M-0103]. It also owns or acquires label, catalogue and income participation interests [M-0101].
  • Distribution deals end by contract: WMG's ADA unit distributed BMG's recordings until the agreement ended in September 2023 [M-0082].

What a streaming licence looks like (company filing)

  • Spotify takes sound recording and audiovisual licences from the three major label groups and Merlin, with royalties, marketing commitments and operating rules, audit and termination rights [M-0083]. They are generally multi-year, not automatically renewable, worldwide, and terminable for non-payment, material breach or change of control [M-0084]. It also licenses independent labels and aggregators such as CD Baby, DistroKid and TuneCore [M-0085].
  • For compositions, Spotify generally gets mechanical, performance and lyric rights. For audio only use in the US, mechanicals run through the Section 115 licence and the MLC blanket licence since January 2021 [M-0086]. That statutory route is audio only, not video.
  • Spotify has recently entered direct licences with some US publishers for audiovisual and other rights [M-0087]. US performance rights come through ASCAP, BMI, GMR and SESAC blanket licences with usage reporting and one to four year terms [M-0088]. Failing to obtain or keep licences is a stated business risk [M-0089].
