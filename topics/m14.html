M14 Deal terms

Part 1 of 3: Bottom line, Rules. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: medium.

Answers questions like: Who owns a song after a record deal or publishing deal; Does a transfer of music rights need a signed writing; Can an artist terminate a label grant and what happens to mixes already made; Who can license a song for sync in a video, and is that negotiated each time; Does the Spotify or label deal cover use in a user mix; Can a performer's digital replica be licensed in a services contract

Parts: 1 2 3

Bottom line

  • US federal: a transfer of copyright ownership needs a writing signed by the owner [M-0040]. A nonexclusive licence is not a transfer, but a signed written licence can beat a later transfer [M-0043]. Sync (the composition) and master use (the recording) are negotiated directly with the rights holders [M-0080, M-0140].
  • Label and publisher deals decide who controls each copyright. Public company filings say most label artist deals are work made for hire, with royalties, recoupable advances and options [M-0052, M-0075, M-0076]. These are company statements, not law.
  • A streaming service's licences do not cover another platform. They are the service's own contracts [M-0083, M-0084]. A platform needs its own direct licences for music in video.
  • Termination rights (17 U.S.C. 203) can reopen grants after 35 years, but a derivative work made before termination may keep being used [M-0056, M-0060]. Never promise that a song is cleared; ask the holder.

Rules

Transfers and records (US federal, statute)

  • A transfer of ownership, other than by operation of law, is not valid unless in a writing signed by the owner or the owner's authorized agent [M-0040]. Plain English: a handshake or a text thread does not move ownership. An exclusive licence is a transfer of ownership, so it also needs the signed writing (analysis).
  • A transfer or other document may be recorded in the Copyright Office if signed, or certified as a true copy of the signed original [M-0041].
  • Of two conflicting transfers, the first executed wins if recorded within one month after signing in the US (two abroad) or before the later transfer is recorded [M-0042].
  • A signed written nonexclusive licence, recorded or not, prevails over a conflicting transfer if taken before the transfer or in good faith before its recordation and without notice [M-0043].

Work made for hire and the label practice (court decision, industry)

  • Warner Music Group says virtually all its agreements with artists since 1971 provide that artists render services as work made for hire [M-0052]. Reservoir Media says the same of its recording artist agreements and says a termination right exists for compositions not made for hire [M-0099]. Both are the companies' own statements about their practice, not rules.
  • WMG warns that if a recording were found not to be made for hire, artists or heirs could terminate US rights in a window starting 35 years after release for post 1977 grants [M-0053]. The statute counts from execution of the grant, see below.
  • Waite v. UMG (S.D.N.Y. 31 March 2020, motion to dismiss): the court did not decide whether the recordings were works made for hire, because the label raised it only on limitations [M-0049]. It said the label's limitations argument was weakened by the industry habit of inserting work made for hire language, which would force artists to sue within three years or lose the Section 203 right [M-0050]. The artists sought a declaration that sound recordings cannot be works made for hire; the court called authorship a close issue on limitations [M-0051]. This is a pleading stage ruling, not a final holding.
  • Ray Charles Foundation v. Robinson (9th Cir. 2015): the estate's sole beneficiary had standing to challenge termination notices [M-0069] and could challenge whether the works were made for hire and so not terminable [M-0070]. Standing only; the merits were not decided [M-0069].

Termination, 17 U.S.C. 203 (statute)

  • Section 203 applies to grants by an author executed on or after 1 January 1978, other than works made for hire and wills [M-0055].
  • The window is five years starting 35 years after execution of the grant (or 35 years after publication or 40 after execution, whichever ends earlier, if the grant covers publication) [M-0056].
  • Notice must be served two to ten years before the effective date [M-0057], and a copy must be recorded before that date to take effect [M-0058].
  • Termination works notwithstanding any agreement to the contrary [M-0059]. Unless terminated, a grant lasts for the term of copyright [M-0061].
  • A derivative work made under the grant before termination may keep being used on the grant's terms. No new derivative works may be made after termination [M-0060]. A further grant is valid only after the effective date, except that the original grantee may agree to one after notice is served [M-0062].
