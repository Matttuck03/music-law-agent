M15 AI training and outputs

Part 3 of 3: Gotchas, Open questions for counsel, Sources. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: high.

Parts: 1 2 3

Gotchas

  • "Training is fair use" is not settled for music. The US training rulings saved are about books and are district court orders [M-0421, M-0423]. Kadrey's illegality line is dicta [M-0423].
  • Do not call the Udio or Suno dismissals a ruling that AI music is lawful. They are stipulated dismissals with no terms in the saved text [M-0011, M-0012, M-0429].
  • Pleadings are not findings [M-0424, M-0430]. The Suno 1201 order is a leave to amend ruling [M-0013].
  • The Copyright Office Part 3 is a pre-publication version and persuasive only [M-0418, M-0020]. Part 2 is persuasive, not binding [M-0415].
  • The TRAIN Act, and any digital replica bill, are not law [M-0006]. A subpoena right would not itself make training lawful or unlawful [M-0479].
  • GEMA v. OpenAI concerns lyrics, first instance, with an appeal pending [M-0431].
  • Removing, muting or editing audio is not a licence and does not clear a track. Where the track is protected, the missing licence is sync for the composition and master use for the recording, from the publisher and the label; see M07.
  • Currency: Part 3 final, appeals in GEMA, Bartz settlement, the UK policy path and Suno case history all move. Last checked 2026-11-10.

Open questions for counsel

  • US: does a creator who prompts a music generator and edits the result hold a copyright in the output that they can license to a platform?
  • US: do a platform's creator terms need an AI disclosure and a warranty that AI music does not copy a protected song?
  • US: what Suno or Udio terms, if any, allow commercial use on a user upload platform?
  • US: does a platform's processing of creator audio need an express no training clause in its terms and vendor contracts?
  • EU: if a platform serves EU users and offers an AI feature, does it count as a general purpose model provider under AI Act Art. 53, or only a downstream deployer?
  • EU and UK: what does a reasonable machine readable rights reservation look like for creator uploads on a platform?
  • US: has the TRAIN Act moved since 2026-08-20, and is any music AI case on appeal?

  2. [M-0423] VERIFIED. Kadrey v. Meta (N.D. Cal., Chhabria J., June 25, 2025): the court said in many circumstances it will be illegal to copy protected works to train generative AI without permission.

    Source: Kadrey v. Meta Platforms, Inc., No. 3:23-cv-03417-VC, ECF 598, order on cross-motions for partial summary judgment (fair use) (N.D. Cal. June 25, 2025). Pinpoint: ECF 598, Part on market harm. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

  12. [M-0006] VERIFIED. The TRAIN Act is pending as S. 2455 and H.R. 7209; neither is law.

  13. [M-0479] VERIFIED. S. 2455 (TRAIN Act, bill not law) would let a copyright owner ask a district court clerk to issue a subpoena to a generative AI model developer for copies or records sufficient to identify the owner's works used in training.

    Source: S. 2455 (119th), TRAIN Act (Transparency and Responsibility for Artificial Intelligence Networks), as introduced in Senate July 24, 2025. Pinpoint: S.2455 proposed 17 U.S.C. 514(b)(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "may request the clerk of any United States district court to issue a subpoena to a developer for disclosure of copies of, or records sufficient to identify with certainty, the copyrighted works"

  14. [M-0431] VERIFIED. GEMA v. OpenAI (LG Munich I, Nov. 11, 2025, 42 O 14139/24): memorising works in a language model is a reproduction under UrhG s.16. Translation: memorisation of language works in the AI model is a reproduction because the work is physically fixed and can be made indirectly perceptible.

    Source: LG München I, Urteil vom 11.11.2025, 42 O 14139/24, GEMA v OpenAI (Bayern.Recht official text, German). Pinpoint: Leitsatz 2 (Rn. 181). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Die Memorisierung von Sprachwerken im KI-Sprachmodell stellt eine Vervielfältigung nach § 16 UrhG dar, weil das Werk körperlich festgelegt ist und es mittelbar wahrnehmbar gemacht werden kann."
