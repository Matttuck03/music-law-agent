M15 AI training and outputs

Part 1 of 3: Bottom line, Rules. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: high.

Answers questions like: Can a user put AI generated music in a mix; Who owns AI generated music; Is training an AI model on songs fair use; Does the TRAIN Act or any AI music bill apply today; Does the EU or UK let AI companies train on music; Can a platform process audio a user made with AI

Parts: 1 2 3

Bottom line

US federal law has no AI training rule in a statute. Whether training on recordings and songs is fair use is undecided for music: the fair use test is case by case, and the only US training rulings saved are about books, not music [M-0203, M-0421]. Purely AI-generated material is not copyrightable in the Copyright Office's view, so a creator who prompts a generator may own little or nothing in the track [M-0415, M-0416]. AI output can still copy a real song, so a licence or consent question remains for both copyrights, the composition and the master. As of 2026-11-10 the TRAIN Act is a bill, not law [M-0006].

Rules

US federal: training

  • Section 107 makes fair use a four factor, non exhaustive test, and the statute states no number of seconds or safe length [M-0203]. Factor four is the effect on the market for or value of the work [M-0204].
  • Copyright Office, Part 3 report (regulator guidance, pre-publication version of May 2025, persuasive not binding): the Office expects some training uses will be fair use and some will not [M-0418, M-0020]. As of 2026-11-10 no final Part 3 is published [M-0020].
  • The same report says fairness depends on which works were used, from what source, for what purpose, and with what controls on outputs [M-0419]. It treats lost licensing revenue as part of market harm [M-0420].
  • Bartz v. Anthropic (court decision, N.D. Cal., 23 June 2025, district court, books not music): training on lawfully acquired books was fair use, described as exceedingly transformative [M-0421]. Downloading and keeping pirated copies for a central library was not excused, and the court set a trial on those copies [M-0422]. Later settlement and appeals are not in the saved sources.
  • Kadrey v. Meta (court decision, N.D. Cal., 25 June 2025): the judge said in many circumstances it will be illegal to copy protected works to train generative AI without permission [M-0423]. That is dicta in a market dilution discussion; the same order gave Meta summary judgment on that record. Do not cite it as a holding that training is illegal.
  • Music cases are pleadings and procedure so far. UMG, Capitol and Sony sued Suno in D. Mass. in June 2024 (No. 1:24-cv-11611) alleging copying of recordings to train its model [M-0424] (pleading, not a finding). The UMG complaint against Udio pleaded that unlicensed AI services could saturate the market and compete with genuine recordings [M-0430] (allegation only).
  • UMG dismissed its claims against Udio on 3 November 2025 and Warner dismissed on 25 November 2025, without prejudice [M-0011, M-0429]. Warner and Suno stipulated to dismissal of Warner's claims against Suno on 9 December 2025; settlement terms are not in the saved text [M-0012].
  • The Suno case continues for the other labels. On 18 August 2026 the court found the labels' 17 U.S.C. 1201(a)(1) stream ripping claim plausible and allowed amendment [M-0013]. That is a pleading ruling, not a merits ruling. Amended complaint filed 25 August 2026 [M-0014]. Suno argued the YouTube measure limits copying but does not control access; the court described that argument and still found the claim plausible [M-0427].
  • Section 1201(a)(1)(A) bars circumventing a measure that controls access to a work [M-0489]. Section 1201(a)(2) bars trafficking in circumvention tools [M-0490]. Section 1201 does not affect fair use or other infringement defences [M-0491].
  • Concord v. Anthropic (court decision, N.D. Cal., 6 Oct 2025): the court denied dismissal of contributory, vicarious and DMCA 1202(b) claims over lyrics output [M-0492]. Pleading stage, lyrics not audio. Its contributory test predates Cox v. Sony (2026), so do not use it for the current contributory standard (see M09).

US federal: outputs and authorship

  • Musical works and sound recordings are separate categories of copyrightable work [M-0028, M-0029].
  • Copyright Office, Part 2 (regulator guidance, persuasive not binding): copyright does not extend to purely AI-generated material or to material with insufficient human control over expressive elements [M-0415].
  • Prompts alone do not give enough control to make the user the author, given current generally available technology [M-0416]. This is tied to today's technology and may change.
  • The Office does not recommend legislation on copyrightability and expects courts to give more guidance [M-0417].

US federal: pending bill

  • TRAIN Act (pending bill, not law): S. 2455, introduced 24 July 2025 by Sen. Welch, referred to Senate Judiciary [M-0478]. H.R. 7209, an identical House bill, introduced 22 January 2026 by Rep. Dean, referred to House Judiciary [M-0505]. Neither is law [M-0006].
  • If passed, S. 2455 would let a copyright owner with a good faith belief ask a district court clerk for a subpoena to a generative AI developer for records sufficient to identify the owner's works used in training. It targets the developer, with no host duty [M-0479]. Status files are dated 2026-08-10 (S. 2455) and 2026-08-20 (H.R. 7209) [M-0006].
