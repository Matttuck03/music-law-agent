M16 Voice clones and digital replicas

Part 3 of 3: Rules (continued), Gotchas, Open questions for counsel, Sources. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: high.

Parts: 1 2 3

Rules (continued)

  • (state statute) Right of Publicity Act s.30(b): no person may knowingly distribute or make available a sound recording or audiovisual work with actual knowledge it contains an unauthorized digital replica [M-0456].
  • Public Act 103-0836 added digital replica provisions to that Act [M-0506]. The claimed effective date of 2025-01-01 is (unverified) [M-0017]. State no date as fact.
  • The Digital Voice and Likeness Protection Act (P.A. 103-830) voids services contract clauses that allow a replica in place of work without a reasonably specific description, where the person also lacked counsel or union representation [M-0457]. P.A. 104-0282 amended it, effective 2026-01-01 [M-0018].

United Kingdom (foreign law)

  • A performer's consent is needed to record a live performance or broadcast it live (CDPA s.182) [M-0243], to copy a recording of it (s.182A) [M-0244], and to make it available on demand (s.182CA) [M-0245].
  • Showing, playing or communicating a performance by means of a recording the person knows or has reason to believe was made without consent infringes (s.183) [M-0247].
  • Equitable remuneration is owed to the performer from the copyright owner when a published recording is played in public or communicated to the public, other than on demand (s.182D) [M-0246].
  • Section 191HA lets a performer recover rights after 50 years if the producer does not issue or offer the recording. Rarely relevant to a short-video app [M-0248].
  • These rights protect the performance itself. The ledger holds no UK row on whether an AI voice clone is a protected performance. Do not state that it is.

Gotchas

  • NO FAKES is not law. Every S. 4591, S. 1367, H.R. 2794 and H.R. 8915 statement is a bill description. Status is as of the status file updated 2026-08-10 for S. 4591 and the 2026-10-23 file for H.R. 8915 [M-0465, M-0466].
  • Bill text changed between 2025 and 2026. Check which version a source describes [M-0499, M-0503].
  • A US federal voice or likeness right does not exist yet. Lanham Act false endorsement needs confusion about sponsorship [M-0462].
  • Lehrman v. Lovo is an early motion ruling. Do not read it as a final merits holding [M-0460].
  • Soundalikes are not free. Midler and Waits turned on deliberate imitation of a famous voice to sell a product [M-0458, M-0459]. Whether a creator video that is not an ad fits those cases is open (analysis).
  • A song licence, a library licence or a platform sound licence covers music, not a person's voice cloned to perform it (analysis). Do not tell a creator a library covers a platform without the saved terms text.
  • State death periods differ: New York 40 years [M-0453], the bill 70 years at most [M-0476].
  • The Illinois effective date is not confirmed (unverified) [M-0017].
  • Needs source: Lehrman v. Lovo later history, live streaming under replica laws, and UK law on AI clones.

Open questions for counsel

  • US federal: does a UGC video app with creator uploads fit prong (i) of S. 4591 (online service), and does an agent registration plus stay down plan fit its file pipeline?
  • Tennessee and Illinois: what counts as actual knowledge of an unauthorized clone for a host, a notice, or a pattern of reports?
  • California and New York: do 3344 and 50 and 51 reach a non ad creator video on a UGC app, and does 50-f(9) cover a short-video host?
  • Is a voice changing or voice cloning feature in a platform a tool within the Tennessee distributor rule [M-0445]?
  • UK: does an AI clone of a performer's voice infringe CDPA performer rights, and who is liable if a platform serves UK users?
  • What wording should a platform use in creator terms to require consent for any cloned voice and to allow quick removal?
