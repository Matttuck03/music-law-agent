M17 Publicity and false endorsement

Part 2 of 3: Rules (continued). Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: high.

Parts: 1 2 3

Rules (continued)

  • Safe harbor conditions in S. 4591 as reported: act on compliant notices and remove or disable access as soon as feasible [M-0468, M-0469]. A UGC service must also remove later uploads matching the fingerprint of the noticed replica (stay down) [M-0470]. The same conditions appear in H.R. 8915 [M-0501, M-0502] and in the 2025 text [M-0496, M-0497, M-0500].
  • The repeat violator termination policy condition is written for the prong (iii) services, not the UGC prong [M-0498, M-0487]. That is the S. 1367 text; the S. 4591 equivalent is not separately cited here.
  • UGC services must register a designated agent with the Copyright Office. Failure raises a presumption of no good faith effort after 90 days [M-0471, M-0503]. The 2025 text made it conclusive with no grace period [M-0499].
  • An online service is liable only if its agent got a compliant notice or court order, or it willfully avoided one [M-0472]. Voluntary tool use to remove content does not alone confer actual knowledge [M-0477].
  • The extra exclusions (news, documentary, commentary or parody, fleeting use) do not apply where the replica depicts sexually explicit conduct [M-0473].
  • It would treat the right as intellectual property law, so Section 230 would not shield platforms [M-0475].
  • State law: it preempts state claims over digital replicas in expressive works but saves state laws in force by 2025-01-02 and state laws specifically on sexually explicit replicas [M-0474].
  • Term: 10 years after death, renewable in 5 year periods on proof of use, ending at 70 years after death [M-0476].
  • Copyright Office Part 1 (July 2024) (regulator guidance, recommendations only) urged a federal replica law with a notice safe harbor, an actual knowledge standard, no full preemption and no Section 230 shield [M-0481, M-0482, M-0483, M-0484].
