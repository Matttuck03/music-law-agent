M17 Publicity and false endorsement

Part 3 of 3: Gotchas, Open questions for counsel, Sources. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: high.

Parts: 1 2 3

Gotchas

  • Needs a licensed attorney: every application point above and the open questions below.
  • A song licence is not a voice licence. Composition, master and the singer's voice are three separate permissions (analysis).
  • Muting, pitch shifting or calling something a "tribute" is not consent (analysis). A soundalike can still be actionable [M-0458, M-0459]. Both cases are Ninth Circuit only.
  • NO FAKES is not law. Do not tell a creator it applies or that it protects a platform. As of the 2026-08-10 capture, S. 4591 is on the Senate calendar and later action is unchecked [M-0004, M-0465].
  • Several rows rest on mirror texts. Lehrman v. Lovo is a motion to dismiss ruling and later history is unchecked [M-0460].
  • The Illinois effective date is not confirmed by saved source text (unverified) [M-0017, M-0506].
  • Tennessee, California and New York have different consent forms: written consent in New York, prior consent in California [M-0450, M-0446].
  • Post mortem terms differ: New York 40 years [M-0453]; S. 4591 would use a 10 to 70 year structure [M-0476].
  • Currency: state replica laws and NO FAKES change fast. Re-check before relying after 2027-02-08 (90 days).

Open questions for counsel

  • Tennessee and Illinois: does a UGC host with no knowledge, but a takedown process, face liability for a user's voice clone under the ELVIS Act or 765 ILCS 1075/30(b)?
  • New York: does 50-f(9) shield a short video app as an advertising medium, or only paid ad channels?
  • California: does 3344 reach user posted soundalikes in non advertising videos hosted by a platform?
  • Federal: if S. 4591 passes as reported, what notice and stay down build does a platform need, and who is its designated agent?
  • Illinois: what is the confirmed effective date of Public Act 103-0836?
  • Processing tools: on current facts, is a tool that only re-encodes a user's own file privately an online service under any prong of S. 4591?
  • Section 230: do state publicity claims against a UGC host survive under the intellectual property carve out in each circuit that matters to the platform?

  1. [M-0458] VERIFIED. Midler v. Ford: deliberately imitating a widely known professional singer's distinctive voice to sell a product is a tort in California.

    Source: Midler v. Ford Motor Co., 849 F.2d 460 (9th Cir. 1988) (Caselaw Access Project mirror). Pinpoint: 849 F.2d 460, 463. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "when a distinctive voice of a professional singer is widely known and is deliberately imitated in order to sell a product, the sellers have appropriated what is not theirs and have committed a tort in California."

  2. [M-0459] VERIFIED. Waits v. Frito-Lay (9th Cir. 1992): the Ninth Circuit affirmed the judgment for voice misappropriation and Lanham Act s.43(a) false endorsement over a soundalike commercial, except that it vacated the Lanham Act damages award as duplicative.

    Source: Waits v. Frito-Lay, Inc., 978 F.2d 1093 (9th Cir. 1992) (Caselaw Access Project mirror). Pinpoint: 978 F.2d 1093, 1096. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Because it is duplicative, we vacate the award of damages under the Lanham Act. We affirm in all other respects."
