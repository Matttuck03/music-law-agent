M17 Publicity and false endorsement

Part 1 of 3: Bottom line, Rules. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: high.

Answers questions like: Can a user put a pop star's voice or a soundalike in a mix; Does an AI voice clone of a singer in an uploaded mix create liability for the platform; Does the ELVIS Act or California law reach a platform that hosts the mix; Would NO FAKES make a platform set up takedown and stay down; Does a processing tool face any digital replica duty when it processes user audio

Parts: 1 2 3

Bottom line

US state publicity law and the federal Lanham Act protect a person's voice and likeness separately from copyright in the song [M-0462, M-0019] (analysis). A licence for the composition and the master does not clear a singer's voice, a soundalike or an AI clone (analysis). Tennessee, California, New York and Illinois each have statutes on voice or digital replicas [M-0019, M-0446, M-0450, M-0456]. Federal NO FAKES (S. 4591, H.R. 8915) is a bill, not law, as of the 2026-08-10 and 2026-10-23 status captures [M-0465, M-0466].

Rules

Federal false endorsement (US federal)

  • Lanham Act 43(a)(1)(A) bars false or misleading representations likely to cause confusion about affiliation, sponsorship or approval [M-0462].
  • Midler v. Ford (9th Cir. 1988) (court decision, Ninth Circuit only): deliberately imitating a widely known professional singer's distinctive voice to sell a product is a tort in California. It is limited to that fact pattern [M-0458].
  • Waits v. Frito-Lay (9th Cir. 1992) (court decision, Ninth Circuit only): the Ninth Circuit affirmed judgment for voice misappropriation and Lanham Act false endorsement over a soundalike ad, but vacated the Lanham Act damages as duplicative [M-0459].
  • Lehrman v. Lovo (S.D.N.Y. 2025-07-10) (court decision): at the motion to dismiss stage, voice clone claims mostly failed under federal trademark and copyright law but could proceed under N.Y. Civil Rights Law 50 and 51 [M-0460]. The motion was denied for the original voice recording copyright claim, contract, NY Civil Rights Law and GBL claims [M-0461]. Later history is unchecked.

Tennessee

  • The ELVIS Act (Pub. Ch. 588, 2024) adds an individual's voice to the protected rights of publicity [M-0019]. Voice includes a simulation of the voice [M-0444].
  • Liability attaches to publishing, performing, distributing or making available a voice or likeness knowing it was not authorized [M-0443]. Effective 2024-07-01 per the same row.
  • It also reaches distributors of a tool whose primary purpose is producing a particular identifiable person's voice or likeness, with knowledge it was unauthorized [M-0445].

California

  • Cal. Civ. Code 3344(a)(1): knowing use of another's name, voice, signature, photograph or likeness on goods, or to advertise or sell, without prior consent is actionable [M-0446].
  • Section 3344.1 defines a digital replica as a computer generated, highly realistic representation readily identifiable as a person's voice or visual likeness. Authorized sampling, remixing and mastering are carved out of the definition [M-0447].
  • For a deceased personality, producing or distributing a digital replica in an expressive audiovisual work or sound recording without prior consent carries the greater of $10,000 or actual damages, subject to the listed exceptions [M-0448].

New York

  • Civil Rights Law 50: using a living person's name, portrait, picture, likeness or voice for advertising or trade without written consent is a misdemeanor [M-0450]. Section 51 allows an injunction and damages [M-0451].
  • Section 50-f: using a deceased performer's digital replica in an audiovisual work, sound recording or live musical performance with knowledge it was unauthorized creates liability [M-0452]. The post mortem right ends forty years after death [M-0453].
  • Section 50-f(9) exempts owners or employees of an advertising medium (including streaming services) unless they had actual knowledge by prior notification. The row notes it does not obviously cover user content hosts [M-0454].

Illinois

  • 765 ILCS 1075/30(b): no person may knowingly distribute or make available to the public a sound recording or audiovisual work with actual knowledge it contains an unauthorized digital replica [M-0456].
  • Public Act 103-0836 added a digital replica definition to the Right of Publicity Act [M-0506]. The January 1, 2025 effective date is unconfirmed; saved text has no effective date line [M-0017] (unverified). The compiled statute row also describes it as effective 1-1-25, not rechecked [M-0456].

Pending federal bills (not law)

  • NO FAKES was reintroduced 2026-05-20 as S. 4591 and H.R. 8915 [M-0003]. S. 4591 was reported with a substitute on 2026-06-24 and placed on the Senate calendar [M-0004, M-0465]. H.R. 8915 was referred to House Judiciary with no later action [M-0466]. Status captures: S. 4591 2026-08-10, H.R. 8915 2026-10-23.
  • The 2025 bills, S. 1367 and H.R. 2794, did not advance past committee referral [M-0005, M-0463, M-0464].
  • As reported, S. 4591 defines online service to include a site or app that predominantly provides public access to user uploaded material [M-0467]. S. 1367 had the same definition [M-0495].
