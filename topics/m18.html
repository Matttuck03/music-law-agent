M18 UK music copyright

Part 1 of 2: Bottom line, Rules. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: high.

Answers questions like: Do UK users need separate licences for the song and the recording; Is a mixing platform liable in the UK if a user uploads a mix with songs; Does the UK have a fair use rule or a parody exception a platform can rely on; Can a platform train or tune AI on UK music; Who owns a UK recording made by a producer or an employee; Do UK performers have rights over a recording used in a mix

Parts: 1 2

Bottom line

UK law, as of 2026-11-10: a recorded song has two separate copyrights, the musical work (composition) and the sound recording (master), and using both in a video needs permission for each [M-0238]. Putting a video with music online for UK viewers is a restricted act (communication to the public, including on demand) that needs a licence from the rights holders, and a platform can be liable for authorising it [M-0236, M-0237, M-0235, M-0255]. UK law has no US style statutory licence for sync, and no general fair use. The AI and copyright rules are under review and are not settled [M-0436].

Rules

What is protected and who owns it (UK, statute)

  • Copyright subsists in original musical works and in sound recordings as separate works [M-0238].
  • A sound recording is a recording of sounds from which they may be reproduced, or a recording of a literary, dramatic or musical work from which sounds reproducing it may be produced [M-0094].
  • No new copyright arises in a sound recording to the extent it is a copy taken from a previous sound recording [M-0095].
  • The author of a work is its first owner, subject to the exceptions in s.11 [M-0090].
  • Where an employee makes a literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work or film in the course of employment, the employer is first owner, subject to contrary agreement [M-0091].
  • The author of a sound recording is the producer [M-0092]. (analysis) Because s.11(2) does not list sound recordings, the first owner of a recording is the producer unless assigned or agreed otherwise [M-0093]. This is an inference from the statute text.
  • Assignment must be in writing signed by or on behalf of the assignor [M-0096]. An exclusive licence must also be in writing signed by or on behalf of the owner [M-0097].
  • An agreement to assign future copyright, signed by the prospective owner, takes effect as an assignment when the copyright comes into existence [M-0098].

What the owner controls (UK, statute)

  • The owner has the exclusive right to copy, issue copies, rent or lend, perform in public, communicate to the public and adapt [M-0234].
  • Copyright is infringed by a person who, without licence, does or authorises another to do a restricted act [M-0235].
  • Communication to the public covers a musical work, sound recording, film or broadcast [M-0236]. It includes making a work available on demand at a time and place individually chosen [M-0237].
  • The High Court can grant an injunction against a service provider with actual knowledge of another person using its service to infringe (s.97A) [M-0242].

Performers (UK, statute)

  • Performers have their own rights, separate from copyright. Recording a live performance, or broadcasting it live, without consent infringes them [M-0243].
  • Copying a recording of a performance without consent infringes [M-0244]. Making a recording available on demand without consent infringes [M-0245].
  • Showing, playing in public or communicating a performance by means of a recording the person knows or has reason to believe was made without consent infringes [M-0247].
  • A performer is entitled to equitable remuneration from the copyright owner when a commercially published recording is played in public or communicated to the public, other than on demand under s.182CA [M-0246]. It is a payment right, not a bar to use, and is assignable only to a collecting society [M-0246].
  • A performer who assigned rights to a producer can serve notice to terminate if the producer, after 50 years, fails to issue copies or make the recording available on demand [M-0248]. Rarely relevant to a short video app.

Exceptions (UK, statute)

  • Fair dealing for caricature, parody or pastiche does not infringe [M-0240]. A contract term that restricts an act permitted by that exception is unenforceable [M-0241].
  • The text and data analysis exception covers copies made only for non-commercial research by a person with lawful access, with acknowledgement [M-0239]. It does not cover commercial AI training.

AI and policy (UK, regulator report, not law)

  • The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 required an economic impact assessment of four policy options within 9 months [M-0434] and a report on copyright works in developing AI systems [M-0435].
  • The government report of March 2026 says a broad exception with opt-out is no longer its preferred way forward [M-0436, M-0015]. This is a policy statement, not law, and no AI training exception has been enacted [M-0436].
