M19 EU music law for an upload platform

Part 1 of 2: Bottom line, Rules, Gotchas, Open questions for counsel. Law last checked 2026-11-10. Volatility: high.

Answers questions like: Would a platform that hosts user mixes be an online content sharing service provider in the EU; Does the EU hosting safe harbor protect a platform for user mixes with music; Who licenses music in the EU and on what terms; Can users rely on parody or quotation when uploading a mix; Does EU law let rightholders block AI training on their music; What does the AI Act require of model providers about copyright

Parts: 1 2

Bottom line

  • EU law (foreign law): if a platform is an online content sharing service provider, it performs an act of communication to the public when users upload and must get authorisation, and the usual hosting safe harbor does not apply [M-0387, M-0388]. Without authorisation it is liable unless it meets the best efforts tests [M-0389, M-0390, M-0391].
  • Authorisation means licences for both copyrights: the composition (usually publisher or a collective management organisation) and the master (label or distributor) (analysis). Collective bodies must license on objective and non-discriminatory terms [M-0310].
  • Editing, muting or re-encoding a file is not a licence (analysis).
  • Whether a given platform is in scope, and which national law applies, needs an EU licensed attorney. Nothing here says any song is cleared.

Rules

Article 17 of Directive (EU) 2019/790 (foreign law, EU directive)

  • Article 17(1): a provider performs an act of communication to the public when it gives access to user uploads, and must get authorisation [M-0387].
  • Article 17(3): the Article 14(1) e-Commerce hosting safe harbor does not apply to these situations [M-0388].
  • Article 17(4)(a): without authorisation the provider is liable unless it made best efforts to obtain one [M-0389].
  • Article 17(4)(b): best efforts, to high industry standards of professional diligence, to make unavailable works for which rightholders gave relevant and necessary information [M-0390].
  • Article 17(4)(c): act expeditiously on a notice and make best efforts to prevent future uploads, called notice and stay down [M-0391].
  • Article 17(6): a lighter regime for services available in the Union under three years with turnover under EUR 10 million [M-0392]. Check the full conditions of the Article with counsel before relying on this.
  • Article 17(7): users can rely on quotation, criticism, review, caricature, parody or pastiche when uploading [M-0393].
  • Article 17(8): no general monitoring obligation [M-0394].
  • Article 17(9): complaints are processed without undue delay, and decisions to disable or remove get human review [M-0395].

Courts and the Commission

  • CJEU Grand Chamber, Poland v Parliament and Council, C-401/19, 26 April 2022 (court decision): the Court dismissed the annulment action and upheld Article 17 [M-0396]. Its holding on Article 17(4)(b) and (c) depends on safeguards (see the ledger note on M-0396).
  • Same case, para 90: providers cannot be required to filter content whose unlawfulness would need an independent assessment of context and exceptions [M-0397].
  • Commission 2021 guidance (regulator guidance, non-binding): automated blocking at upload should in principle be limited to manifestly infringing uploads [M-0398].

Collective management of music (Directive 2014/26/EU, foreign law)

  • Rightholders may authorise a collective management organisation of their choice for the rights and territories of their choice [M-0308].
  • Organisations and users must negotiate licences in good faith and share necessary information [M-0309].
  • Licensing terms must rest on objective and non-discriminatory criteria [M-0310].

AI and music in the EU (foreign law, court decision, voluntary code)

  • CDSM Art 3: a text and data mining exception for research organisations and cultural heritage institutions, for scientific research [M-0399].
  • CDSM Art 4(1), (3): a general mining exception for lawfully accessible works, unless the rightholder reserved use in an appropriate manner, such as machine readable means for content online [M-0400, M-0442]. National transposition varies [M-0442].
  • AI Act Art 53(1)(c): providers of general purpose AI models need a copyright policy that identifies and honours Article 4(3) reservations [M-0439]. Art 53(1)(d): they must publish a training content summary using the AI Office template [M-0440]. These obligations apply from 2 August 2025 [M-0441].
  • EU GPAI Code of Practice, copyright chapter (voluntary, binds signatories only): Measure 1.3(1) commits to honour machine readable reservations when crawling for mining [M-0493]. Measure 1.4(1)(a) commits to technical safeguards against outputs that reproduce protected training content [M-0494].
  • GEMA v OpenAI, LG Munich I, 11 Nov 2025, 42 O 14139/24 (German first instance court decision, lyrics only, not audio): memorising works in a language model is a reproduction under UrhG s.16 [M-0431]. The s.44b mining exception covers copies made to build training data, not training the model [M-0432]. Model operators, not users, are liable for infringing outputs from simple prompts [M-0433]. Appeal pending per the source header; later status unchecked.

Gotchas

  • This is EU level text. Each Member State transposed the directives; national law and national courts decide details [M-0442].
  • Article 17(7) exceptions are user rights, not a way for a platform to treat all uploads as exempt. Parody of a track does not make a full song under a video safe (analysis).
  • The Article 17(6) lighter regime has an age and turnover test [M-0392]. It lapses as a platform ages (analysis).
  • "No general monitoring" [M-0394] sits beside "best efforts to make unavailable" [M-0390]. The CJEU reconciles them with safeguards [M-0397].
  • GEMA v OpenAI is one first instance German court on lyrics. It is not an EU wide rule and not about audio [M-0431].
  • AI Act dates: Art 113(b) is unchanged by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 per the ledger note on M-0441, which says the source file for that regulation is still needed.
  • The GPAI Code is voluntary and its text was captured 2026-11-10 without a check for later Commission updates [M-0494].
  • Open gaps: no saved row on live streaming under Article 17 or on any national transposition. Status as of 2026-11-10.

Open questions for counsel

  • EU: would a platform serving EU users with user uploaded short video be an online content sharing service provider under Article 17, and under which Member State law?
  • EU: does a live stream count as upload and making available under Article 17(1)?
  • EU: does a tool that re-encodes a creator's own file fall inside Article 17 if it never publishes the file?
  • EU: which collective management organisations would license composition rights for EU launch?
  • EU: does the Article 17(6) lighter regime fit a platform today and when does it end?
  • EU and Germany: how should we treat the pending GEMA v OpenAI appeal before using AI features trained on music?
