M22 Covers and the compulsory mechanical licence

Part 2 of 2: Gotchas, Open questions for counsel, Sources. Law last checked 2026-11-13. Volatility: medium.

Parts: 1 2

Gotchas

  • "A cover licence covers a remix" is wrong. The compulsory licence is composition only [M-0542], bars changes to the basic melody or fundamental character [M-0540], and one distributor says in terms that derivative works such as samples cannot go through its cover process [M-0597].
  • "I bought a cover licence, so I can post the video" is wrong. No video under the compulsory licence [M-0544].
  • "My arrangement is mine" is wrong under the compulsory licence. It is not protected as a derivative work without the owner's express consent [M-0541].
  • A song that has never been released with the owner's authority is not open to the compulsory licence [M-0545].
  • The TuneCore page is a 2020 archive copy and may be out of date. The CD Baby page is an archive copy dated 2026-02-16. Both were captured 2026-11-13. Re-check live terms before relying on them.
  • DistroKid and SoundCloud cover and remix rules were not captured (pages blocked). The base makes no claim about them.
  • Outside the US the compulsory mechanical licence in section 115 does not apply. The base has no rows on cover licensing in other countries (gap).

Open questions for counsel

  • US federal: does a platform that hosts and streams user covers qualify as a digital music provider that can use the blanket mechanical licence?
  • US federal: where is the line between a permitted arrangement and a change to the "fundamental character" of a song, for tempo changes, key changes and re-ordered sections?
  • US federal: how do the notice rules work today for a user who releases a cover as a download or on physical media, given the 2021 change to the blanket licence?
  • US federal: what is the least a user needs to post a cover with video on the platform?
  • Non-US: which licences replace section 115 for covers in the UK, EU, Canada and Australia?

Claims cited on this page

Each claim gives its status, source, pinpoint, jurisdiction, the date it was checked and a verbatim quote from the source.

  1. [M-0542] VERIFIED. The compulsory licence is for making and distributing phonorecords of a nondramatic musical work (the composition); it is not a licence of any sound recording.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 115 compulsory license for making and distributing phonorecords. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 115(a)(1)(A)-(B). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "obtain a compulsory license to make and distribute phonorecords of a nondramatic musical work"

  2. [M-0540] VERIFIED. A compulsory licence under 115 includes the privilege of an arrangement only to the extent necessary to conform the work to the style of the performance; it may not change the basic melody or fundamental character.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 115 compulsory license for making and distributing phonorecords. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 115(a)(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "the arrangement shall not change the basic melody or fundamental character of the work"

  3. [M-0597] VERIFIED. CD Baby says derivative works such as samples cannot be cleared through its cover song licensing process.

    Source: CD Baby help: cover songs. Pinpoint: CD Baby help: Things to note. Jurisdiction: platform-terms. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "Derivative works, such as samples, cannot be cleared through the cover song licensing process."

  4. [M-0544] VERIFIED. The Office states a phonorecord does not include sounds accompanying a motion picture or other audiovisual work, so the compulsory licence does not cover putting a song in video.

    Source: Copyright Office Circular 73, Compulsory license for making and distributing phonorecords. Pinpoint: Circular 73. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "A phonorecord does not include sounds accompanying a motion picture or other audiovisual work"

  5. [M-0541] VERIFIED. An arrangement made under the compulsory licence is not protected as a derivative work except with the express consent of the composition owner.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 115 compulsory license for making and distributing phonorecords. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 115(a)(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "shall not be subject to protection as a derivative work under this title, except with the express consent of the copyright owner"

  6. [M-0545] VERIFIED. First-use rule: the compulsory licence is available only if phonorecords of the work have previously been distributed to the public in the US under the authority of the copyright owner, or, for a digital music provider's digital phonorecord deliveries, the first fixation of the sound recording was made under the musical work owner's authority and the recording owner authorised the deliveries.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 115 compulsory license for making and distributing phonorecords. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 115(a)(1)(A)(i)-(ii). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "phonorecords of such musical work have previously been distributed to the public in the United States under the authority of the copyright owner of the work"
