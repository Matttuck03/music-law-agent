M23 Stem tools and DJ mixes

Part 2 of 4: Rules (continued). Law last checked 2026-11-13. Volatility: high.

Parts: 1 2 3 4

Rules (continued)

  • What a mix is, right by right. Reproduction: both the song and the recording carry it [M-0108]. Derivative works: each owner controls them [M-0522], and for the master that covers rearranging, remixing or altering the actual sounds [M-0523]. Performance: songs have a public performance right [M-0106]; recordings have one only for digital audio transmission [M-0107, M-0296].
  • Compilation or derivative work. A compilation is a work formed by collecting and assembling existing materials, selected, coordinated or arranged so the whole is an original work [M-0569]. A derivative work recasts, transforms or adapts earlier works [M-0521]. A government paper says remixes will in many cases be derivative works, compilations or collective works (policy paper, 2016) [M-0554]. No saved authority decides which one a DJ mix is. Unsettled.
  • Either way, the mix maker's own copyright covers only what the maker added [M-0529], and not any part where earlier material was used unlawfully [M-0528].
  • Stem splitting. The master owner's reproduction right covers copying that recaptures the actual sounds [M-0110]. A stem holds the actual recorded sounds, so it falls within that wording (analysis). No saved case rules on stem separation, private or public. Unsettled.
  • Home recording law is narrow. US federal, 17 U.S.C. 1008: no infringement action may be based on making or selling the listed recording devices and media, or on a consumer's noncommercial use of such a device or medium to make musical recordings [M-0572]. The device definition turns on a recording function designed or marketed mainly for making private-use digital audio copies [M-0573].
  • Ninth Circuit (court decision, RIAA v. Diamond, 1999): a computer hard drive is outside the Act's definition of a digital music recording, and a player that copies only from a hard drive is not a covered device [M-0574, M-0575]. The court described the Act's purpose as private, noncommercial recording by consumers [M-0576]. Whether software stem splitting on a computer or server is inside this shelter is not decided, and Diamond makes it doubtful (analysis).
  • US federal, 17 U.S.C. 117(a) lets the owner of a copy of a computer program make another copy or adaptation of that program on stated conditions. Its text names only computer programs [M-0577]. It gives no stated right to copy or adapt music files (analysis).
  • The 114 statutory licence, entry test. It reaches subscription transmissions, eligible nonsubscription transmissions and one satellite category, if the listed conditions are met [M-0114]. The transmission must not be part of an interactive service [M-0115, M-0298]. An interactive service lets a member of the public receive a program made for the recipient or, on request, a particular sound recording [M-0113, M-0299]. Taking requests for the public at large does not alone make a service interactive, within limits [M-0585].
  • The 114 licence, archive rules. The transmission may not be part of an archived program of less than 5 hours [M-0578]. An archived program of 5 hours or more may not stay available for more than 2 weeks [M-0579].
  • The 114 licence, programming rules. The service may not exceed the sound recording performance complement or publish an advance schedule of the specific recordings [M-0580]. The complement caps, in any 3-hour period on a channel, how many selections come from one record or one featured artist [M-0581].
  • The 114 licence, other conditions. The service may take no affirmative steps to cause or induce listeners to make copies [M-0582]. The transmission must be made from a phonorecord lawfully made under the copyright owner's authority [M-0583]. The service must show the title, record and featured artist in text while the recording plays [M-0584].

  1. [M-0108] VERIFIED. Both musical works and sound recordings carry the reproduction right (copies or phonorecords).

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "to reproduce the copyrighted work in copies or phonorecords;"

  2. [M-0522] VERIFIED. The copyright owner has the exclusive right to prepare derivative works, and to authorize others to do so.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "the owner of copyright under this title has the exclusive rights to do and to authorize any of the following"

  3. [M-0523] VERIFIED. The sound recording owner's derivative right (106(2)) is limited to works in which the actual sounds fixed in the recording are rearranged, remixed, or otherwise altered in sequence or quality.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "limited to the right to prepare a derivative work in which the actual sounds fixed in the sound recording are rearranged, remixed, or otherwise altered in sequence or quality"

  4. [M-0106] VERIFIED. Musical works carry an exclusive right of public performance.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(4). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "in the case of literary, musical, dramatic, and choreographic works, pantomimes, and motion pictures and other audiovisual works, to perform the copyrighted work publicly;"

  5. [M-0107] VERIFIED. Sound recordings carry a public performance right only by digital audio transmission.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(6). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "in the case of sound recordings, to perform the copyrighted work publicly by means of a digital audio transmission."

  6. [M-0296] VERIFIED. Sound recording owners have only the 106(1), (2), (3) and (6) rights and no general public performance right under 106(4).

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(a). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "The exclusive rights of the owner of copyright in a sound recording are limited to the rights specified by clauses (1), (2), (3) and (6) of section 106, and do not include any right of performance under section 106(4)"

  7. [M-0569] VERIFIED. A compilation is a work formed by collecting and assembling preexisting materials selected, coordinated or arranged so the whole is an original work of authorship.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 101 definitions. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 101 'compilation'. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "selected, coordinated, or arranged in such a way that the resulting work as a whole constitutes an original work of authorship"

  8. [M-0521] VERIFIED. A derivative work is one based on preexisting works; the statute's examples include a musical arrangement and a sound recording, and 'any other form in which a work may be recast, transformed, or adapted'.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 101 definitions. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 101 'derivative work'. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "any other form in which a work may be recast, transformed, or adapted"

  9. [M-0554] VERIFIED. 2016 Commerce white paper: remixes include mashups and sampling, and in many cases they will be derivative works, compilations or collective works under the Copyright Act.

    Source: Dept of Commerce Internet Policy Task Force, White Paper on Remixes, First Sale, and Statutory Damages (Jan. 2016). Pinpoint: White Paper (Jan. 2016), p. 7. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "In many cases, these remixes will constitute derivative works, compilations, or collective works, as defined in the Copyright Act"

  10. [M-0529] VERIFIED. Copyright in a derivative work or compilation covers only the new material the author contributed and gives no exclusive right in the preexisting material.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 103 compilations and derivative works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 103(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "The copyright in a compilation or derivative work extends only to the material contributed by the author of such work"

  11. [M-0528] VERIFIED. Under Section 103(a), protection for a work employing preexisting copyrighted material does not extend to any part of the work in which the material has been used unlawfully.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 103 compilations and derivative works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 103(a). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "protection for a work employing preexisting material in which copyright subsists does not extend to any part of the work in which such material has been used unlawfully"

  12. [M-0110] VERIFIED. The sound recording reproduction right covers only duplication that recaptures the actual sounds.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "is limited to the right to duplicate the sound recording in the form of phonorecords or copies that directly or indirectly recapture the actual sounds fixed in the recording."

  13. [M-0572] VERIFIED. The Audio Home Recording Act bars infringement actions based on the manufacture, importation or distribution of a digital audio recording device, digital audio recording medium, analog recording device or analog recording medium, or on a consumer's noncommercial use of such a device or medium for making digital or analog musical recordings.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 1008 Prohibition on certain infringement actions. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 1008. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "based on the noncommercial use by a consumer of such a device or medium for making digital musical recordings or analog musical recordings"

  14. [M-0573] VERIFIED. The Act's digital audio recording device definition turns on a device whose digital recording function is designed or marketed for the primary purpose of making a digital audio copied recording for private use.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 1001 Definitions (Audio Home Recording). Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 1001(3). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "designed or marketed for the primary purpose of, and that is capable of, making a digital audio copied recording for private use"

  15. [M-0574] VERIFIED. RIAA v. Diamond (9th Cir. 1999): the Rio player was not a digital audio recording device because it copies only from a computer hard drive, not from transmissions or digital musical recordings.

    Source: RIAA v. Diamond Multimedia, 180 F.3d 1072 (9th Cir. 1999). Pinpoint: 180 F.3d 1072, end of Part IV. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "Because the Rio cannot make copies from transmissions, but instead, can only make copies from a computer hard drive, it is not a digital audio recording device."

  16. [M-0575] VERIFIED. RIAA v. Diamond (9th Cir. 1999): a computer hard drive is excluded from the Act's definition of a digital music recording, so the Rio, which copies from a hard drive, does not record directly from a digital music recording.

    Source: RIAA v. Diamond Multimedia, 180 F.3d 1072 (9th Cir. 1999). Pinpoint: 180 F.3d 1072, Part III. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "a hard drive is a material object in which one or more programs are fixed; thus, a hard drive is excluded from the definition of digital music recordings"

  17. [M-0576] VERIFIED. RIAA v. Diamond describes the Act's purpose as the consumer right to make recordings of copyrighted music for private, noncommercial use, quoting the Senate Report.

    Source: RIAA v. Diamond Multimedia, 180 F.3d 1072 (9th Cir. 1999). Pinpoint: 180 F.3d 1072, Part III. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "The purpose of the Act is to ensure the right of consumers to make analog or digital audio recordings of copyrighted music for their private, noncommercial use"

  18. [M-0577] VERIFIED. 17 U.S.C. 117(a) lets the owner of a copy of a computer program make or authorise another copy or adaptation of that program on stated conditions (an essential step in using it with a machine, or archival purposes); its text names only computer programs.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 117 Computer programs. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 117(a). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "it is not an infringement for the owner of a copy of a computer program to make or authorize the making of another copy or adaptation of that computer program"

  19. [M-0114] VERIFIED. 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2) subjects subscription digital audio transmissions, eligible nonsubscription transmissions and preexisting satellite digital audio radio service transmissions to statutory licensing if the listed conditions are met.

  26. [M-0579] VERIFIED. Under the 114 statutory licence a transmission may not be part of an archived program of 5 hours or more that is made available for more than 2 weeks, so a permanent on-demand mix archive does not meet that condition.

  28. [M-0581] VERIFIED. The sound recording performance complement allows, in any 3-hour period on a channel, no more than 3 different selections from any one phonorecord (no more than 2 consecutively), or 4 different selections by the same featured artist or from any set or compilation of phonorecords (no more than 3 consecutively).

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(j)(13). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "3 different selections of sound recordings from any one phonorecord lawfully distributed for public performance or sale in the United States, if no more than 2 such selections are transmitted consecutively"

    Quote: "the transmitting entity takes no affirmative steps to cause or induce the making of a phonorecord by the transmission recipient"

  30. [M-0583] VERIFIED. The statutory licence requires the transmission to be made from a phonorecord lawfully made under the copyright owner's authority.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2)(C)(vii). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2)(C)(ix). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.
