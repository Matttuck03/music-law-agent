M23 Stem tools and DJ mixes

Part 3 of 4: Rules (continued), Platform application. Law last checked 2026-11-13. Volatility: high.

Parts: 1 2 3 4

Rules (continued)

  • What 114 does not do. It is about performing recordings by digital audio transmission [M-0114, M-0107]. An interactive service also needs a licence for each song inside the recordings [M-0301]. A digital audio transmission does not include any audiovisual work, so video of a DJ set is outside it [M-0112, M-0297].
  • How licensed mix services clear tracks. Mixcloud (company blog, not terms and not law; blog dated 2024-03-28, page path after redirect dated 2025-01-01, captured 2026-11-13) says a streaming service must hold licences clearing both the composition and the sound recording of every track in a stream [M-0586]. It says it was the first streaming service to clear the copyrights for radio shows and mixes [M-0587]. Its checklist asks whether a platform has direct deals with the labels whose music is played [M-0589]. It says downloading needs an additional licensed right, the mechanical right [M-0588].
  • Apple Music (company notice to artists dated 2021-11-16, captured 2026-11-13) says it developed a process to identify and compensate the individual creators in DJ mixes [M-0592], works with major and independent labels, and pays rights holders within DJ mixes directly [M-0593].
  • Live sets. Mixcloud describes its live product as a licensed live streaming platform that also archives the stream as a recording (company blog, captured 2026-11-13) [M-0590]. It says that on a platform that has not cleared the music, rights holders can have a mix taken down or muted [M-0591].

Platform application

  • The tool itself. A stem splitter and mixer has lawful uses: a user's own songs, licensed stems, music whose owners agreed (analysis). Under Sony, Grokster and Cox the ability to misuse it is not what creates liability [M-0560, M-0563, M-0222]. What the platform says and builds is. Adverts, tutorials, presets or search features aimed at chart hits are the kind of affirmative steps Grokster describes [M-0562], and a product built around unlicensed hits risks being called tailored to infringement [M-0223].
  • Keep the two roles apart. As a tool maker the platform is judged by intent [M-0221]. As a host of user mixes it is judged by the 512(c) conditions [M-0343]. As the party that itself copies, renders or streams, it may be judged as a direct infringer [M-0220]. Each role needs its own answer from counsel.
  • Recorded mixes shared with the public. A saved mix that listeners start whenever they like looks like an archived program, and a user mix is almost always under 5 hours (analysis). The 114 licence bars that [M-0578]. So public mix hosting of commercial tracks needs direct master licences, plus licences for the songs [M-0301, M-0106]. This matches what the licensed services say they do [M-0586, M-0589].
  • A radio-style channel of mixes could in theory use 114, but only with the complement, no advance track lists, no downloads, track text on screen and lawfully made source copies [M-0580, M-0581, M-0582, M-0583, M-0584]. A user-driven mixing product is a poor fit for those limits (analysis).
  • Live mixing to an audience. Online, each track is performed as a song and as a recording [M-0106, M-0107]. The only saved service statement says both must be cleared by the service [M-0586]. If the live set is saved for replay, the archive rules above apply to the replay [M-0578, M-0579].
  • Stems. Whether the split happens on the user's device or on the platform's servers changes who made the copy (analysis). Server-side splitting and storage put the platform closer to direct copying [M-0110, M-0220]. This is unsettled and is a lead question for counsel.
  • Risk carriers: the user for what the user uploads, mixes and shares; the platform as tool maker, host and possibly direct actor; labels and publishers as claimants; a licensed service partner, if one is used, for what its own licences cover.

  1. [M-0114] VERIFIED. 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2) subjects subscription digital audio transmissions, eligible nonsubscription transmissions and preexisting satellite digital audio radio service transmissions to statutory licensing if the listed conditions are met.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "a subscription digital audio transmission not exempt under paragraph (1), an eligible nonsubscription transmission, or a transmission not exempt under paragraph (1) that is made by a preexisting satellite digital audio radio service shall be subject to statutory licensing"

  2. [M-0107] VERIFIED. Sound recordings carry a public performance right only by digital audio transmission.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(6). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "in the case of sound recordings, to perform the copyrighted work publicly by means of a digital audio transmission."

  3. [M-0301] VERIFIED. An interactive service may not publicly perform a sound recording unless a licence has been granted for the public performance of any copyrighted musical work contained in it.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(3)(C). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "an interactive service may not publicly perform a sound recording unless a license has been granted for the public performance of any copyrighted musical work contained in the sound recording"

  4. [M-0112] VERIFIED. A "digital audio transmission" under 17 U.S.C. 114(j)(5) does not include the transmission of any audiovisual work, so the 114(d)(2) statutory licence for digital audio transmissions does not extend to audiovisual transmission such as a video with music.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(j)(5). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "This term does not include the transmission of any audiovisual work."

  5. [M-0297] VERIFIED. A digital audio transmission is a digital transmission that embodies a sound recording and does not include transmission of any audiovisual work.

    Quote: "A digital audio transmission is a digital transmission as defined in section 101, that embodies the transmission of a sound recording. This term does not include the transmission of any audiovisual work"

  14. [M-0560] VERIFIED. Sony v. Universal (1984): selling copying equipment is not contributory infringement if the product is widely used for legitimate purposes or is merely capable of substantial noninfringing uses.

    Source: Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, 464 U.S. 417 (1984). Pinpoint: 464 U.S. 417, syllabus (Pp. 434-442). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "does not constitute contributory infringement if the product is widely used for legitimate, unobjectionable purposes, or, indeed, is merely capable of substantial noninfringing uses"

  15. [M-0563] VERIFIED. Grokster: mere knowledge that a product could be or is used to infringe is not enough for liability, and ordinary product support is not inducement.

    Source: MGM Studios v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005). Pinpoint: 545 U.S. 913, 937. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "mere knowledge of infringing potential or of actual infringing uses would not be enough here to subject a distributor to liability"

  16. [M-0222] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient to establish the required intent.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), Part II.B. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient to establish the required intent to infringe."

  18. [M-0223] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): intent can be shown by inducement or by a service tailored to infringement (not capable of substantial or commercially significant noninfringing uses).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

  19. [M-0221] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): contributory liability requires that the provider intended its service be used for infringement, shown only by inducement or a service tailored to infringement.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Held: The provider of a service is contributorily liable for a user’s infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement,"

  24. [M-0580] VERIFIED. Under the statutory licence the transmission may not exceed the sound recording performance complement, and the service may not publish an advance schedule of the specific recordings.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2)(C)(i)-(ii). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

  25. [M-0581] VERIFIED. The sound recording performance complement allows, in any 3-hour period on a channel, no more than 3 different selections from any one phonorecord (no more than 2 consecutively), or 4 different selections by the same featured artist or from any set or compilation of phonorecords (no more than 3 consecutively).

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(j)(13). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "3 different selections of sound recordings from any one phonorecord lawfully distributed for public performance or sale in the United States, if no more than 2 such selections are transmitted consecutively"

  26. [M-0582] VERIFIED. The statutory licence requires the service to take no affirmative steps to cause or induce the recipient to make a phonorecord, so a download button on mixes is outside it.

  30. [M-0110] VERIFIED. The sound recording reproduction right covers only duplication that recaptures the actual sounds.
