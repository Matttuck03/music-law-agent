M23 Stem tools and DJ mixes

Part 4 of 4: Gotchas, Open questions for counsel, Sources. Law last checked 2026-11-13. Volatility: high.

Parts: 1 2 3 4

Gotchas

  • "DJs do it in clubs, so it is legal online" does not follow. The right to perform a song in public is separate from the right to copy it (analysis row) [M-0183]. The base has no saved text of a club or venue licence, so it makes no claim about what such a licence covers (gap). Online, the one saved service statement says both the song and the recording must be cleared for every track [M-0586].
  • "We are just a tool" is not a shield by itself. Sony protects a product with substantial lawful uses [M-0560], but inducement is judged by what the maker says and does [M-0562, M-0221].
  • "The webcasting licence covers our mixes" is wrong for on-demand or saved mixes [M-0115, M-0578, M-0579] and for any download feature [M-0582].
  • "It is for private use, so home recording law covers it" is not established. The Act is tied to listed devices and media [M-0572], and the Ninth Circuit kept computer hard drives outside it [M-0575].
  • The Mixcloud and Apple rows are company statements about their own services. They are not law and not terms of service. Capture date 2026-11-13.
  • Live-stream music rules for Twitch, YouTube, Instagram and SoundCloud were not captured. The base makes no claim about them (gap).
  • Cox v. Sony is from 2026. Outcomes on remand and any new stem or mixing cases are not tracked here. Re-check before relying.
  • Statutory damages are counted per work [M-0216], and a single mix can hold many works (analysis). See card M12.

Open questions for counsel

  • US federal: is a stem split of a lawfully bought track a reproduction under 106(1) and 114(b), and does it matter whether the user's device or the platform's server does it?
  • US federal: does the home recording shelter in section 1008 reach software stem splitting at all after RIAA v. Diamond?
  • US federal: is a blended DJ mix a derivative work of each song and recording, a compilation, or both, and what turns on the label?
  • US federal: when a user streams a live mix through the platform, who performs the works publicly, the user, the platform or both?
  • US federal: which exact licences does an on-demand mix library need for masters and for songs, and can any part run through 114, the blanket mechanical licence or the performing rights organisations?
  • US federal: does the 512(c) safe harbor cover a mix that the platform's own tool rendered from user-supplied tracks, or only files users upload ready-made?
  • US federal: what marketing, onboarding and design choices would a court read as inducement or as tailoring the service to infringement after Cox?
  • US federal: what does a club or venue performance licence actually cover, and does any of it carry over to online streaming?

Claims cited on this page

Each claim gives its status, source, pinpoint, jurisdiction, the date it was checked and a verbatim quote from the source.

  1. [M-0183] VERIFIED. Analysis: 106(4) gives the owner of a musical work the exclusive right to perform it publicly, a right separate from the 106(1) reproduction and 106(3) distribution rights that mechanical licensing concerns and from sync; what a PRO licence covers is checked in other rows.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(4). Jurisdiction: analysis. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "in the case of literary, musical, dramatic, and choreographic works, pantomimes, and motion pictures and other audiovisual works, to perform the copyrighted work publicly;"

  2. [M-0586] VERIFIED. Mixcloud's own blog says the streaming service must hold licences clearing both the composition and the sound recording of every track in a stream.

    Source: Mixcloud blog: how to live stream music without copyright takedown issues (Mixcloud's own blog). Pinpoint: Mixcloud blog 2024-03-28, 'How can you live stream safely'. Jurisdiction: platform-terms. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "the streaming service you use needs licences that clear both the musical composition and the sound recording of every track you play"

  3. [M-0560] VERIFIED. Sony v. Universal (1984): selling copying equipment is not contributory infringement if the product is widely used for legitimate purposes or is merely capable of substantial noninfringing uses.

    Source: Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, 464 U.S. 417 (1984). Pinpoint: 464 U.S. 417, syllabus (Pp. 434-442). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "does not constitute contributory infringement if the product is widely used for legitimate, unobjectionable purposes, or, indeed, is merely capable of substantial noninfringing uses"

  4. [M-0562] VERIFIED. Grokster (2005): one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe, shown by clear expression or other affirmative steps, is liable for third-party infringement.

    Source: MGM Studios v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005). Pinpoint: 545 U.S. 913, 919 (syllabus Held; opinion at 936-937). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe copyright, as shown by clear expression or other"

  5. [M-0221] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): contributory liability requires that the provider intended its service be used for infringement, shown only by inducement or a service tailored to infringement.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Held: The provider of a service is contributorily liable for a user’s infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement,"

  6. [M-0115] VERIFIED. The statutory licence requires that the transmission not be part of an interactive service.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2)(A)(i). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "the transmission is not part of an interactive service;"

  7. [M-0578] VERIFIED. The 114(d)(2) statutory licence for eligible nonsubscription transmissions requires that the transmission is not part of an archived program of less than 5 hours.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2)(C)(iii)(I). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "is not part of an archived program of less than 5 hours duration"

  8. [M-0579] VERIFIED. Under the 114 statutory licence a transmission may not be part of an archived program of 5 hours or more that is made available for more than 2 weeks, so a permanent on-demand mix archive does not meet that condition.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2)(C)(iii)(II). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "is not part of an archived program of 5 hours or greater in duration that is made available for a period exceeding 2 weeks"

  9. [M-0582] VERIFIED. The statutory licence requires the service to take no affirmative steps to cause or induce the recipient to make a phonorecord, so a download button on mixes is outside it.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2)(C)(vi). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "the transmitting entity takes no affirmative steps to cause or induce the making of a phonorecord by the transmission recipient"

  10. [M-0572] VERIFIED. The Audio Home Recording Act bars infringement actions based on the manufacture, importation or distribution of a digital audio recording device, digital audio recording medium, analog recording device or analog recording medium, or on a consumer's noncommercial use of such a device or medium for making digital or analog musical recordings.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 1008 Prohibition on certain infringement actions. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 1008. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "based on the noncommercial use by a consumer of such a device or medium for making digital musical recordings or analog musical recordings"

  11. [M-0575] VERIFIED. RIAA v. Diamond (9th Cir. 1999): a computer hard drive is excluded from the Act's definition of a digital music recording, so the Rio, which copies from a hard drive, does not record directly from a digital music recording.

    Source: RIAA v. Diamond Multimedia, 180 F.3d 1072 (9th Cir. 1999). Pinpoint: 180 F.3d 1072, Part III. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "a hard drive is a material object in which one or more programs are fixed; thus, a hard drive is excluded from the definition of digital music recordings"

  12. [M-0216] VERIFIED. Statutory damages are assessed per work for all infringements involved in the action; the owner may elect them any time before final judgment.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 504 remedies, damages and profits. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 504(c)(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "an award of statutory damages for all infringements involved in the action, with respect to any one"
