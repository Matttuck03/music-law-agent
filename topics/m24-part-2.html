M24 Licensed platform models, distributor rules and stem model licences

Part 2 of 2: Rules (continued), Platform application, Gotchas, Open questions for counsel, Sources. Law last checked 2026-11-13. Volatility: high.

Parts: 1 2

Rules (continued)

  • python-audio-separator and the UVR models (industry, checked 2026-11-13): the tool's code is MIT, and it asks users to credit the UVR models. It gives no licence for the model files [M-0608]. Its default BS-Roformer checkpoint and its Mel-Band Roformer checkpoint are listed with no licence [M-0609, M-0610]. A separate BS-RoFormer code project is MIT and is silent on checkpoints [M-0611].
  • UVR (industry, checked 2026-11-13): the developers say they trained all the models in the package except the Demucs v3 and v4 4-stem models [M-0612]. The README asks for credit under MIT and has no separate licence text for the models [M-0613].
  • Training data (industry, checked 2026-11-13): the MUSDB18 set includes 46 tracks from MedleyDB that are licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 [M-0614]. The dataset page does not state terms for the DSD100 tracks [M-0615].

Platform application

  • Model 1, direct deals for a mix library. The platform signs master licences with labels and distributors, and song licences with publishers and performing rights organisations. Every saved statement from a lawful mix service points to this model [M-0586, M-0589, M-0593]. It is slow and costly, and labels can say no (analysis).
  • Model 2, partner with a service that already holds the licences. Mixes are published and streamed only inside the partner. The platform would rely on the partner's licences and terms. The base has only the partner's own public statements, not its contract terms [M-0586, M-0590]. Whether a partner's licences reach mixes made with split stems is unknown (gap).
  • Model 3, radio-style streaming under the 114 statutory licence, plus performing rights licences for the songs. It works only for non-interactive listening with the programming limits in card M23 [M-0115, M-0578]. It does not fit saved mixes that users pick and play.
  • Model 4, a licensed catalogue. Users mix only tracks or stems the platform has licensed for remixing. This removes the "any music" problem but needs remix-grade grants from owners, which are derivative work grants [M-0522]. The base has no saved terms from stem catalogue or sample pack services (gap).
  • Sending user work to stores through a distributor. Covers have a path [M-0596]. Mixes and mashups built from original audio do not go through the cover path and need the master and the composition cleared one by one [M-0597, M-0599].
  • Picking a stem model. Treat the code and the weights as two separate licence checks (analysis) [M-0600, M-0603]. For a paid product, do not ship weights that are stated non-commercial [M-0606] or stated research-only [M-0603]. Do not ship weights whose licence is silent without a written grant from whoever can give one [M-0604, M-0609, M-0610].
  • Why the weights are a problem at the root: public stem models are commonly trained on a research set that includes non-commercial tracks [M-0614, M-0601]. Whether that limit carries over to trained weights is a legal question the base does not answer (unsettled).
  • Clean options for the stem engine (analysis): train on audio the platform owns or has licensed for training, license a commercial stem engine under a written contract, or get a written commercial grant from a model's owner. The base has no saved terms for commercial stem engines (gap).
  • A model's licence and the music's copyright are separate questions. A clean model licence does not make it lawful to split a song the user has no rights in [M-0605, M-0110].
  • Risk carriers: the platform for the model it ships and for any deal it signs; the user for what goes to stores under the user's name; model owners and dataset owners as possible claimants on the weights; labels and publishers on the music.

Gotchas

  • "The repo is MIT, so we can sell it" is wrong for the weights. The Demucs maintainers say so in terms [M-0603].
  • "It does not say we cannot" is not permission. Silence is not a grant [M-0604, M-0607].
  • A credit request is not a licence. The UVR pages ask for credit and give no model licence text [M-0608, M-0613].
  • The Mixcloud and Apple rows are public statements by the companies about themselves. They do not show the deal terms, the price, or whether stem-based mixes are covered.
  • Mixcloud's line that downloads need "the mechanical right" is a simplification. In US law a download of a mix also copies and distributes the recordings (analysis) [M-0108].
  • The TuneCore page is a 2020 archive copy. The CD Baby help page is an archive copy dated 2026-02-16. Service terms change. Re-capture before relying on them.
  • Not captured: SoundCloud, DistroKid, Beatport, Beatsource, Splice, BandLab, Twitch, YouTube and Instagram terms. The base makes no claim about them.
  • Model licences can change with a commit. All stem model rows were checked 2026-11-13. Pin the exact version checked.

Open questions for counsel

  • Which licences does an on-demand mix library need in the US, and from whom: labels, distributors, publishers, the Mechanical Licensing Collective, each performing rights organisation?
  • If mixes are published only through a licensed partner service, what must the partner contract say for the platform to rely on its licences, and do those licences cover stem-based blends?
  • Does a non-commercial or research-only limit on training data carry over to the trained weights, and who could enforce it?
  • Who can grant a commercial licence for weights when the maintainer says he cannot?
  • If the platform trains its own stem model, what rights does it need in the training audio? See card M15 for the open AI training questions.
  • Can the platform pass distributor rules on to users in its own terms, and what should it check before sending a user's cover to stores?
