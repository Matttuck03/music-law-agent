Rules (continued)

  • Canada (foreign law), Copyright Act s.29.21: an individual may use an existing published work to make a new work, and use or share it, if the use is solely for non-commercial purposes [M-0616], the source is mentioned where reasonable [M-0617], the individual had reasonable grounds to believe the source copy was not infringing [M-0618], and the use has no substantial adverse effect on the market for the original, including that it is not a substitute for it [M-0619].
  • Canada (foreign law): s.29.21(2) defines an intermediary as a person or entity who regularly provides space or means for works to be enjoyed by the public [M-0620]. The exception protects the individual's acts. Whether and how far it protects a platform is an inference, not saved text. Fair dealing in s.29 lists research, private study, education, parody and satire [M-0621]. The fairness test is case law and is not in the base. Moral rights (s.28.2) were not captured (gap).
  • Australia (foreign law), Copyright Act 1968: fair dealing for parody or satire does not infringe copyright in a work [M-0622] or in an audio-visual item [M-0623], which includes a sound recording [M-0624]. Fair dealing for criticism or review needs sufficient acknowledgement [M-0625]. Authors have a right of integrity against derogatory treatment, which for a musical work includes material alteration or distortion that harms honour or reputation [M-0626]. The base holds no Australian user content exception and makes no claim about other parts of the Act.
  • UK (foreign law), CDPA 1988: the owner controls copying, communication to the public and adaptation [M-0234]. Communication to the public covers music and recordings and includes on-demand access [M-0236, M-0237]. Fair dealing for caricature, parody or pastiche does not infringe [M-0240], and a contract cannot override it [M-0241].
  • UK (foreign law), moral rights: the author of a musical work has the right not to have it subjected to derogatory treatment [M-0627]. Treatment is derogatory if it distorts or mutilates the work or otherwise harms the author's honour or reputation [M-0628]. Communicating a derogatory treatment to the public infringes the right [M-0629]. The exceptions in ss.81 and 82 were not reviewed.
  • EU (court decision, Pelham I, 2019): a recognisable sample may be a quotation only if the use is in dialogue with the sampled work and the other conditions are met [M-0630].
  • EU (court press release No 50/26, 14 April 2026, on Pelham II; unofficial summary, judgment text not saved): pastiche covers creations that evoke existing works while being noticeably different, including by sampling, in a recognisable artistic dialogue [M-0631]. The test is whether a person familiar with the work would recognise the pastiche, and intent is not required [M-0632]. Pastiche is not a catch-all and does not cover concealed imitation or plagiarism [M-0633]. Confirm against the judgment before relying.
  • EU (foreign law), Copyright Directive 2019/790 Art 17: a content-sharing platform itself communicates user uploads to the public and must get authorisation [M-0387]. The hosting safe harbor does not apply to that act [M-0388]. Without authorisation it must show best efforts to license, to keep identified works unavailable, and to take down and keep down on notice [M-0389, M-0390, M-0391]. Providers under 3 years old with turnover under EUR 10 million have a lighter regime [M-0392]. Users may rely on quotation, parody and pastiche [M-0393]. There is no general monitoring duty [M-0394], and complaints need human review [M-0395].
