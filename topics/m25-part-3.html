M25 DJ-mix platform application, feature by feature

Part 3 of 5: Platform application (continued). Law last checked 2026-11-13. Volatility: high.

Parts: 1 2 3 4 5

Platform application (continued)

  • What happens: the app plays and blends tracks in real time. Nothing is published.
  • Rights touched: playing music privately is outside the public performance right, which is limited to public performance (analysis) [M-0106]. The open points are the copies made on the way (stems are copies of the actual sounds) [M-0110] and whether a private blend "prepares" a derivative work [M-0522]. No saved case decides either. Unsettled.
  • Shelters that do not clearly apply: the home recording statute is tied to listed devices and media [M-0572], and the Ninth Circuit kept computer hard drives outside it [M-0575]. The Sony fair use finding was about home taping of TV [M-0561].
  • Who carries the risk: mainly the user (analysis). The platform is judged as a tool maker, so its marketing and design matter most [M-0562, M-0223]. Keeping all audio on the device keeps the platform's own servers out of the copying (analysis).
  • Feature 2, saving a mix.
  • A saved mix is a fixed copy of every song and recording in it [M-0108], with the sounds rearranged or altered [M-0523].
  • Saved only on the user's device: the user made the copy. Saved to the platform's cloud: the platform now stores it. That can fit "storage at the direction of a user" [M-0343], but only if the 512(c) conditions are met, and counsel must confirm the fit when the platform's own tool rendered the file.
  • Who carries the risk: the user first; the platform once the file sits on its servers.
  • Feature 3, sharing a mix publicly on the platform.
  • Needs: licences for the recordings and for the songs [M-0301, M-0106, M-0107]. The 114 statutory licence does not fit an on-demand or saved mix [M-0115, M-0578, M-0579]. The licensed services say they use direct deals [M-0589, M-0593].
  • Without licences the platform leans wholly on the 512(c) safe harbor [M-0343]. Red flag knowledge is a live risk on a service whose purpose is mixing well-known tracks (analysis) [M-0345]. A service built around unlicensed hits also risks the "tailored to infringement" label [M-0223].
  • Who carries the risk: the user as uploader; the platform as host and possibly as the party performing the works; labels and publishers are the claimants.
  • Feature 4, live mixing to an audience.
  • Each track is publicly performed as a song [M-0106] and, online, as a recording [M-0107]. The one saved service statement says the service must clear both for every track [M-0586].
  • A non-interactive audio stream could in theory use the 114 licence with its programming limits (see card M23). Video is outside 114 [M-0112] and adds sync questions [M-0141]. A replay of the set is an archived program, so the archive limits of the 114 licence apply to it [M-0578, M-0579]. A replay that does not fit them needs direct licences.
  • Who carries the risk: the DJ user and the platform together. How the safe harbor applies to live streams is not covered in the base (gap).
  • Feature 5, users uploading commercial tracks to mix.
  • This is the "any music" choice. The upload is storage at the user's direction [M-0343]. The platform needs the full 512(c) kit: a registered agent, renewed every 3 years [M-0349, M-0354], notice and takedown [M-0348], counter notice [M-0355, M-0356], and a repeat infringer policy that is actually applied [M-0357, M-0376].
  • The safe harbor does not require the platform to monitor [M-0358]. It is lost on actual or red flag knowledge without removal [M-0344, M-0345, M-0346].
  • Design matters. Features that invite users to bring chart hits, such as search by hit title or presets named after famous tracks, look like the affirmative steps in Grokster [M-0562].
  • Who carries the risk: the user for the upload; the platform if it loses the safe harbor or is found to induce.
  • Feature 6, storing stems.
  • A stem holds the actual recorded sounds, so making and keeping it is within the reproduction wording for recordings (analysis) [M-0110]. No case rules on it. Unsettled.
  • Per-user stems kept at the user's direction are closer to user storage [M-0343]. A shared library of split hits that the platform builds and serves to other users is the platform's own copying and distribution, not user storage (analysis) [M-0220].

Claims cited on this page

Each claim gives its status, source, pinpoint, jurisdiction, the date it was checked and a verbatim quote from the source.

  1. [M-0106] VERIFIED. Musical works carry an exclusive right of public performance.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(4). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "in the case of literary, musical, dramatic, and choreographic works, pantomimes, and motion pictures and other audiovisual works, to perform the copyrighted work publicly;"

  2. [M-0110] VERIFIED. The sound recording reproduction right covers only duplication that recaptures the actual sounds.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "is limited to the right to duplicate the sound recording in the form of phonorecords or copies that directly or indirectly recapture the actual sounds fixed in the recording."

  3. [M-0522] VERIFIED. The copyright owner has the exclusive right to prepare derivative works, and to authorize others to do so.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "the owner of copyright under this title has the exclusive rights to do and to authorize any of the following"

  4. [M-0572] VERIFIED. The Audio Home Recording Act bars infringement actions based on the manufacture, importation or distribution of a digital audio recording device, digital audio recording medium, analog recording device or analog recording medium, or on a consumer's noncommercial use of such a device or medium for making digital or analog musical recordings.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 1008 Prohibition on certain infringement actions. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 1008. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "based on the noncommercial use by a consumer of such a device or medium for making digital musical recordings or analog musical recordings"

  5. [M-0575] VERIFIED. RIAA v. Diamond (9th Cir. 1999): a computer hard drive is excluded from the Act's definition of a digital music recording, so the Rio, which copies from a hard drive, does not record directly from a digital music recording.

    Source: RIAA v. Diamond Multimedia, 180 F.3d 1072 (9th Cir. 1999). Pinpoint: 180 F.3d 1072, Part III. Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "a hard drive is a material object in which one or more programs are fixed; thus, a hard drive is excluded from the definition of digital music recordings"

  6. [M-0561] VERIFIED. Sony v. Universal: the VTR was capable of substantial noninfringing uses both because many copyright holders would not object to time-shifting and because unauthorised private, noncommercial home time-shifting of broadcast television was fair use.

    Source: Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, 464 U.S. 417 (1984). Pinpoint: 464 U.S. 417, syllabus (Pp. 442-456). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "even the unauthorized home timeshifting of respondents' programs is legitimate fair use"

  7. [M-0562] VERIFIED. Grokster (2005): one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe, shown by clear expression or other affirmative steps, is liable for third-party infringement.

    Source: MGM Studios v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005). Pinpoint: 545 U.S. 913, 919 (syllabus Held; opinion at 936-937). Jurisdiction: case-law. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe copyright, as shown by clear expression or other"

  8. [M-0223] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): intent can be shown by inducement or by a service tailored to infringement (not capable of substantial or commercially significant noninfringing uses).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "A service is tailored to infringement if it is “not capable of ‘substantial’ or ‘commercially significant’ noninfringing uses.”"

  9. [M-0108] VERIFIED. Both musical works and sound recordings carry the reproduction right (copies or phonorecords).

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "to reproduce the copyrighted work in copies or phonorecords;"

  10. [M-0523] VERIFIED. The sound recording owner's derivative right (106(2)) is limited to works in which the actual sounds fixed in the recording are rearranged, remixed, or otherwise altered in sequence or quality.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "limited to the right to prepare a derivative work in which the actual sounds fixed in the sound recording are rearranged, remixed, or otherwise altered in sequence or quality"

  11. [M-0343] VERIFIED. The 512(c) safe harbor covers infringement by reason of storage at the direction of a user on the provider's system.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "for infringement of copyright by reason of the storage at the direction of a user of material that resides on a system or network controlled or operated by or for the service provider"

  12. [M-0301] VERIFIED. An interactive service may not publicly perform a sound recording unless a licence has been granted for the public performance of any copyrighted musical work contained in it.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(3)(C). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "an interactive service may not publicly perform a sound recording unless a license has been granted for the public performance of any copyrighted musical work contained in the sound recording"

  13. [M-0107] VERIFIED. Sound recordings carry a public performance right only by digital audio transmission.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(6). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "in the case of sound recordings, to perform the copyrighted work publicly by means of a digital audio transmission."

  14. [M-0115] VERIFIED. The statutory licence requires that the transmission not be part of an interactive service.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2)(A)(i). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "the transmission is not part of an interactive service;"

  15. [M-0578] VERIFIED. The 114(d)(2) statutory licence for eligible nonsubscription transmissions requires that the transmission is not part of an archived program of less than 5 hours.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2)(C)(iii)(I). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "is not part of an archived program of less than 5 hours duration"

  16. [M-0579] VERIFIED. Under the 114 statutory licence a transmission may not be part of an archived program of 5 hours or more that is made available for more than 2 weeks, so a permanent on-demand mix archive does not meet that condition.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(d)(2)(C)(iii)(II). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "is not part of an archived program of 5 hours or greater in duration that is made available for a period exceeding 2 weeks"

  17. [M-0589] VERIFIED. Mixcloud's live-stream checklist asks whether the platform has direct deals with the record labels whose music is played.

    Source: Mixcloud blog: how to live stream music without copyright takedown issues (Mixcloud's own blog). Pinpoint: Mixcloud blog 2024-03-28, checklist. Jurisdiction: platform-terms. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "Does it have direct deals with the record labels whose music you play?"

  18. [M-0593] VERIFIED. Apple Music says it works with major and independent labels and pays rights holders within DJ mixes directly.

    Source: Apple Music for Artists: Apple is using Shazam to identify and compensate all DJ mix creators. Pinpoint: Apple Music for Artists notice 2021-11-16. Jurisdiction: platform-terms. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "Apple Music is directly paying rights holders within these DJ mixes and creating long-term monetary value for all creators involved"

  19. [M-0345] VERIFIED. Absent actual knowledge, the provider must not be aware of facts or circumstances from which infringement is apparent (red flag knowledge).

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1)(A)(ii). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "is not aware of facts or circumstances from which infringing activity is apparent"

  20. [M-0586] VERIFIED. Mixcloud's own blog says the streaming service must hold licences clearing both the composition and the sound recording of every track in a stream.

    Source: Mixcloud blog: how to live stream music without copyright takedown issues (Mixcloud's own blog). Pinpoint: Mixcloud blog 2024-03-28, 'How can you live stream safely'. Jurisdiction: platform-terms. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "the streaming service you use needs licences that clear both the musical composition and the sound recording of every track you play"

  21. [M-0112] VERIFIED. A "digital audio transmission" under 17 U.S.C. 114(j)(5) does not include the transmission of any audiovisual work, so the 114(d)(2) statutory licence for digital audio transmissions does not extend to audiovisual transmission such as a video with music.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(j)(5). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "This term does not include the transmission of any audiovisual work."

  22. [M-0141] VERIFIED. The sync right is an industry term for reproduction (and possibly derivative) rights in a musical work synchronized to visual content.

    Source: U.S. Copyright Office, Copyright and the Music Marketplace (February 2015). Pinpoint: Copyright and the Music Marketplace (2015), ch. 1 background. Jurisdiction: industry. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "the right to synchronize musical works to visual content (e.g., in a music video). The synchronization (or synch right) is a species of the reproduction right and may also implicate the derivative work right."

  23. [M-0349] VERIFIED. The 512(c) safe harbor applies only if the provider has designated an agent, by making it available on its service including its website and by providing it to the Copyright Office.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "by making available through its service, including on its website in a location accessible to the public, and by providing to the Copyright Office"

  24. [M-0354] VERIFIED. A designation expires three years after registration unless renewed by amending or resubmitting it.

  26. [M-0355] VERIFIED. On a counter notice the provider replaces the removed material not less than 10 nor more than 14 business days after receipt, unless the notifier first tells its designated agent (of a court action; see rest of 512(g)(2)(C)).
