Platform application (continued)

  • The stem model's own licence is a separate check (see card M24) [M-0603, M-0606].
  • Who carries the risk: the platform, more than for any other private feature, when the splitting and storage happen on its servers.
  • Feature 7, export and download.
  • A download is a new copy that leaves the platform, so it is reproduction and distribution of each song and recording [M-0108]. The 114 licence bars inducing listeners to make copies [M-0582]. One licensed service says downloads need an extra licensed right [M-0588].
  • The compulsory mechanical licence does not cover it: that licence is for the composition only, bars changes to the basic melody or character, and is not a licence of any recording [M-0540, M-0542].
  • Export to other services passes the problem on. The saved distributor pages do not take sample-based works through their cover route [M-0597].
  • Who carries the risk: the user who exports and posts elsewhere; the platform for offering public downloads of other people's mixes.
  • Feature 8, charging money.
  • Not charging is not a defence [M-0220]. But charging changes several tests. Commercial use weighs in fair use factor one [M-0549, M-0551, M-0553]. A direct financial benefit from infringement the platform can control costs it the safe harbor, and the benefit need not be large (court decision, Ninth Circuit) [M-0347, M-0515]. Canada's user content exception needs a solely non-commercial purpose on the individual's side [M-0616].
  • Vicarious liability, which turns on financial benefit and control, was left undecided in Cox (analysis row) [M-0252].
  • Lower-risk pricing (analysis): charge for the tool and for storage, not per mix of named commercial tracks, and keep adverts and payouts away from unlicensed mixes. Counsel should confirm. A subscription can still count as a financial benefit if infringing use is what draws users. The base has no row that decides this (gap).
  • Country flags for rollout.
  • Canada: the s.29.21 exception may protect an individual user's non-commercial mix if all four conditions hold [M-0616, M-0617, M-0618, M-0619]. A mix that can stand in for the original tracks is at risk on the market condition [M-0619]. The platform's own position is not settled by the saved text [M-0620].
  • Australia: only parody or satire and criticism or review are in the saved set [M-0622, M-0625]. Add the integrity right [M-0626]. Treat as stricter than Canada until counsel says otherwise.
  • UK: pastiche and parody are fair dealing exceptions, not a blanket right [M-0240]. On-demand sharing is communication to the public [M-0237]. Moral rights can be asserted against a distorting mix [M-0628, M-0629].
  • EU: the platform is itself liable for user uploads under Art 17 unless it is licensed or meets the best efforts tests [M-0387, M-0389]. Users may rely on pastiche [M-0393], and the 2026 Pelham II press release reads pastiche to include some sampling [M-0631], but not as a catch-all [M-0633]. A straight DJ blend that plays whole tracks is not shown to qualify.
  • Risk carriers overall: the user for what the user uploads, saves, shares and exports; the platform as tool maker, host, possible direct actor and payee; labels and publishers as claimants; authors as moral rights claimants in the UK and Australia.
