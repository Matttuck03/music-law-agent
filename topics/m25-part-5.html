M25 DJ-mix platform application, feature by feature

Part 5 of 5: Gotchas, Open questions for counsel, Sources. Law last checked 2026-11-13. Volatility: high.

Parts: 1 2 3 4 5

Gotchas

  • "Private" must be real. Unlisted links, friends lists and "share with followers" are sharing. The base has no row on how many listeners make a performance public (gap). Ask counsel before calling any sharing feature private.
  • Private mixing is the lowest-risk step, not a cleared one. The stem copy and the derivative work questions are both open [M-0110, M-0522].
  • The safe harbor covers what users store. It does not clearly cover what the platform itself renders, splits, curates or promotes (analysis) [M-0343].
  • Cox removed "knowledge plus inaction" as a route to contributory liability [M-0224]. It did not change the safe harbor conditions, and it left vicarious liability open [M-0252].
  • Canada's exception is for individuals and needs a non-commercial purpose [M-0616]. It is not a licence for a paid platform.
  • The Pelham II rows rest on a press release, not the judgment [M-0631]. EU member states also apply these exceptions through their own national laws, which are not in the base.
  • One mix can hold many works, and statutory damages are per work [M-0254].
  • Geo-blocking and age of the company change the EU answer [M-0392]. Re-check before an EU launch.

Open questions for counsel

  • US: is on-device stem splitting and real-time blending of a lawfully bought track an infringement by the user, and does the platform's exposure change if no audio ever reaches its servers?
  • US: at what point does "sharing with friends" become a public performance or a distribution?
  • US: does 512(c) protect the platform for mixes rendered by its own tool from user uploads, and for stems it stores per user?
  • US: which pricing models avoid a "direct financial benefit" finding, and how should adverts and creator payouts be handled around unlicensed mixes?
  • Canada: does s.29.21 protect the platform as an intermediary when it charges a subscription, and how do courts read "solely non-commercial" and the market effect test for a DJ mix?
  • UK and Australia: can a blend or mashup be a derogatory treatment, and can the platform get moral rights waivers that hold?
  • EU: after Pelham II, can any DJ mix or mashup count as pastiche, and what does Art 17 require of a new, small platform in its first 3 years?
  • All countries: can the platform launch in one country only, and what blocking is enough to keep foreign law out?

Claims cited on this page

Each claim gives its status, source, pinpoint, jurisdiction, the date it was checked and a verbatim quote from the source.

  1. [M-0110] VERIFIED. The sound recording reproduction right covers only duplication that recaptures the actual sounds.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 114 scope of exclusive rights in sound recordings. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 114(b). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "is limited to the right to duplicate the sound recording in the form of phonorecords or copies that directly or indirectly recapture the actual sounds fixed in the recording."

  2. [M-0522] VERIFIED. The copyright owner has the exclusive right to prepare derivative works, and to authorize others to do so.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 106 exclusive rights in copyrighted works. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 106(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "the owner of copyright under this title has the exclusive rights to do and to authorize any of the following"

  3. [M-0343] VERIFIED. The 512(c) safe harbor covers infringement by reason of storage at the direction of a user on the provider's system.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "for infringement of copyright by reason of the storage at the direction of a user of material that resides on a system or network controlled or operated by or for the service provider"

  4. [M-0224] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): the Fourth Circuit's knowledge-plus-insufficient-action rule went beyond the two recognised bases and conflicted with the Court's precedent.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), Part III. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "contributory liability cannot rest only on a provider’s knowledge of infringement and insufficient action to prevent it."

  5. [M-0252] VERIFIED. Analysis: after Cox v. Sony (U.S. 2026) a service provider is contributorily liable for a user's infringement only if it induced the infringement or the service is tailored to it; knowing that some users infringe is not enough. Vicarious liability is a separate theory that the Court did not decide (cert limited to contributory).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: analysis. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Held: The provider of a service is contributorily liable for a user’s infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement,"

  6. [M-0616] VERIFIED. Canada s.29.21(1)(a): the use or the authorization to disseminate the new work must be solely for non-commercial purposes.

    Source: Copyright Act (Canada) s.29.21 non-commercial user-generated content. Pinpoint: s.29.21(1)(a). Jurisdiction: Canada. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "is done solely for non-commercial purposes;"

  7. [M-0631] VERIFIED. CJEU Pelham II (C-590/23, 14 April 2026), per the Court's press release: pastiche covers creations that evoke existing works while noticeably different from them and use, including by sampling, characteristic protected elements to engage in a recognisable artistic or creative dialogue.

    Source: CJEU Judgment C-590/23 Pelham II (14 April 2026). Pinpoint: Press Release No 50/26, summary of answer. Jurisdiction: EU. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "covers creations which evoke one or more existing works, while being noticeably different from them, and which use, including by means of sampling, some of those works’ characteristic elements protected by copyright"

  8. [M-0254] VERIFIED. Analysis: statutory damages are awarded per work infringed, up to $150,000 per work if willful (ordinary range $750 to $30,000), and only for works registered in time (s.412); exposure is per work, not per view.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 504 remedies, damages and profits. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 504(c)(2). Jurisdiction: analysis. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "the court in its discretion may increase the award of statutory damages to a sum of not more than $150,000."

  9. [M-0392] VERIFIED. Art 17(6): new providers under three years old with turnover under EUR 10 million have a lighter regime.

    Source: Directive (EU) 2019/790 on copyright and related rights in the Digital Single Market (CDSM Directive). Pinpoint: Art. 17(6). Jurisdiction: EU. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "the services of which have been available to the public in the Union for less than three years and which have an annual turnover below EUR 10 million"
