Rights basics

7. What rights sit in one track?

  • Two separate copyrights: the song and the recording [M-0025]. Both carry a right to copy [M-0108]. Songs carry a public performance right [M-0106]. Recordings carry one only for digital audio transmission [M-0107].

8. Who has to say yes to a remix?

  • Both owners of each track. Each controls derivative works of its own work [M-0522]. For the recording that covers rearranging, remixing or altering the actual sounds [M-0523].

9. I bought the track. Can I mix it and share the mix?

  • Buying a copy is not shown in the base to carry the rights to copy, remix or perform in public. Those rights belong to the owners [M-0108, M-0522, M-0106]. The base has no row on what a download store's terms allow (gap).

10. Who owns a mix I make?

  • At most, you own the new material you added [M-0529]. Parts that use others' material unlawfully get no protection [M-0528].
  • Courts differ at the edges. The Second Circuit allows a claim to original additions when the use is fair [M-0535]. The Seventh Circuit took a harder line [M-0532].

11. Is re-playing a part myself safer than using the original audio?

  • Only if every sound is newly recorded, and then it removes the recording owner only. A new recording of independently made sounds does not infringe the recording, but the song is still a separate copyright [M-0111].

12. Could my mix be fair use?

  • Perhaps, but do not plan on it. Fair use is a defence and the user carries the burden [M-0211]. When the new use shares the purpose of the original and is commercial, factor one likely weighs against it [M-0551].
  • The base has no case on whether a DJ mix or mashup is fair use (gap).

13. Is a mashup a parody?

  • Only if it comments on the original. Parody needs to mimic the original to make its point. Satire that uses a song to comment on something else needs its own justification [M-0548]. Applying this to a mashup is analysis.

14. Is there a remix exception or a compulsory remix licence in the US?

  • No saved source shows one. A 2016 government paper found no proven need for either (policy paper, not law) [M-0556].

Covers and video

15. What does a cover licence allow?

  • Making and distributing your own audio recording of a song that was already released [M-0339, M-0545], with an arrangement that fits your style [M-0540]. Nothing more.

16. Can I post a video of my cover or mix?

  • Not under the cover licence. It does not reach audiovisual works [M-0544]. Video needs a sync licence agreed directly with the publisher [M-0140], plus the recording owner's licence if original audio is used (analysis) [M-0522].

17. Will a distributor release my remix or mashup?

  • The saved pages give covers a route [M-0596] and say sample-based works need the master cleared with the label and the song with the publisher, by direct deal [M-0599]. Captured 2026-11-13. Other distributors were not captured.

The tool and the platform

18. Is it illegal to make a stem splitter?

  • Not by itself on the contributory liability cases (analysis; no saved case is about a stem or mixing tool). Selling a product capable of substantial lawful uses is not contributory infringement [M-0560], and mere knowledge of misuse is not enough [M-0563]. Vicarious and direct liability are separate questions [M-0252].
  • It becomes a problem when the maker promotes infringing use or tailors the product to it [M-0562, M-0223].

19. Is splitting a song into stems an infringing copy?

  • Unsettled. A stem holds the actual recorded sounds, which is what the recording owner's copy right covers [M-0110] (analysis). No saved case rules on stem splitting, in private or in public.

20. Does home recording law cover private mixing?

  • Doubtful. The statute protects consumers' noncommercial use of listed recording devices and media [M-0572]. An appeals court kept computer hard drives outside the Act [M-0575]. No case applies it to stem software.

21. Can our marketing get us into trouble?

  • Yes. Liability for inducement turns on clear expression or other affirmative steps to foster infringement [M-0562].
