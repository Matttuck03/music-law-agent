The tool and the platform (continued)

  • The researchers read this as reaching a tool maker whose marketing steers users to commercial songs they do not own. That reading is not confirmed by any case (unverified) [M-0564].

22. Did Cox v. Sony make platforms safe?

  • No. It held that contributory liability needs intent, and mere knowledge is not enough [M-0221, M-0222]. It did not decide vicarious liability (analysis row) [M-0252], and one Justice said other theories could apply [M-0225].

23. Does the DMCA safe harbor protect us when users upload hits?

  • Only if every condition is met: user-directed storage [M-0343], no actual or red flag knowledge [M-0344, M-0345], fast removal [M-0348], no direct financial benefit from infringement you can control [M-0347], a registered agent [M-0349], and a repeat infringer policy [M-0357].
  • Whether it covers files the platform's own tool rendered is a question for counsel.

24. Do we have to scan every upload?

  • Not for the US safe harbor. It is not conditioned on monitoring [M-0358]. Actual knowledge that material is infringing, or awareness of facts that make it apparent, still removes the protection [M-0344, M-0345].
  • The EU differs. A platform must make best efforts to keep identified works unavailable [M-0390], though there is no general monitoring duty [M-0394].

25. Can we host saved mixes under the webcasting licence?

  • No. The section 114 licence bars interactive services [M-0115], archived programs under 5 hours [M-0578], and longer archives kept up more than 2 weeks [M-0579].

26. Could a radio-style stream of mixes use that licence?

  • In theory, with strict limits: caps on tracks per record and per artist in any 3 hours [M-0581], no advance track list [M-0580], track details on screen [M-0584], no help with copying [M-0582], lawfully made source copies [M-0583]. The songs need their own performance licences [M-0106].

27. What does a live DJ stream need?

  • Each track is performed as a song and as a recording [M-0106, M-0107]. One licensed service says the service must clear both (company blog, captured 2026-11-13) [M-0586]. Video is outside the 114 licence [M-0112].
  • Other services' live-stream terms were not captured (gap).

28. How do the licensed mix services do it?

  • By their own account, with direct label deals and a way to pay rights holders inside each mix [M-0589, M-0592, M-0593]. Mixcloud statements captured 2026-11-13. Apple notice dated 2021-11-16, captured 2026-11-13. These are company statements, not law.

29. Can users download or export mixes?

  • That adds copying and distribution of every work in the mix [M-0108]. The 114 licence bars inducing copies [M-0582]. One licensed service says downloads need an extra licensed right (captured 2026-11-13) [M-0588].

30. Does charging money change the risk?

  • Yes, in several tests. A direct financial benefit from infringement can cost the safe harbor, and it need not be large [M-0347, M-0515]. Commercial use weighs in fair use [M-0551]. Canada's user exception needs a solely non-commercial purpose [M-0616].

  1. [M-0564] UNVERIFIED (unverified). Analysis: a stem-splitting or mixing tool maker whose marketing or onboarding steers users to split and mix commercial songs they do not own is exposed to Grokster inducement through its own statements and design, not through the tool's capability alone.

    Source: MGM Studios v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005). Pinpoint: 545 U.S. 913, 919. Jurisdiction: analysis. Checked 2026-11-13.

    Quote: "going beyond mere distribution with knowledge of third-party action"

  2. [M-0221] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): contributory liability requires that the provider intended its service be used for infringement, shown only by inducement or a service tailored to infringement.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Held: The provider of a service is contributorily liable for a user’s infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement,"

  3. [M-0222] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient to establish the required intent.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), Part II.B. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient to establish the required intent to infringe."

  4. [M-0252] VERIFIED. Analysis: after Cox v. Sony (U.S. 2026) a service provider is contributorily liable for a user's infringement only if it induced the infringement or the service is tailored to it; knowing that some users infringe is not enough. Vicarious liability is a separate theory that the Court did not decide (cert limited to contributory).

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), syllabus. Jurisdiction: analysis. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "Held: The provider of a service is contributorily liable for a user’s infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement,"

  5. [M-0225] VERIFIED. Cox (2026): Justice Sotomayor, concurring in the judgment, said other common-law theories such as aiding and abetting could apply in copyright.

    Source: Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (Mar. 25, 2026). Pinpoint: 607 U.S. ___ (2026), Sotomayor concurrence. Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Quote: "precedents have left open the possibility that other common-law theories of such liability, like aiding and abetting, could apply in the copyright context."

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

  7. [M-0344] VERIFIED. The provider must lack actual knowledge that material is infringing.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(1)(A)(i). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

    Source: 17 U.S.C. 512 limitations on liability relating to material online. Pinpoint: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(2). Jurisdiction: US-federal. Checked 2026-11-10.

  12. [M-0357] VERIFIED. Under 512(i)(1)(A) a provider must have adopted and reasonably implemented, and inform subscribers of, a policy providing for termination in appropriate circumstances of subscribers and account holders who are repeat infringers; the 512 limitations apply only if so.
